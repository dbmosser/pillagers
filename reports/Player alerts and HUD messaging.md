# Put every alert where the eyes are

The research says a fight HUD should show very little text. The few urgent lines go next to the player, and the rest become icons, world marks, sounds or a log. In shooters about **88% of eye fixations land in the middle quarter of the screen**, while the health, message and score corners get **about 2%**, so corner text is close to invisible in a fight ([Kenny 2005](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/282/1/Paper04_ECMS_2005.pdf)). Games praised for clean HUDs (ARC Raiders, Hunt, Apex) give each kind of message one fixed home, put a warning on the thing it is about, and keep extraction and world events on the map or the compass. Few studios publish their queue rules. The best documented one (Fortnite) shows **one message per slot**, lets urgent ones skip the queue, merges repeats and **drops what cannot show in time** ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/using-hud-message-devices-in-fortnite-creative)). PILLAGERS breaks most of this today. The left feed can show **10 lines, each bright for 6 s and then held at 60% until newer lines push it off**, so one "codex entry" line sat through the calm, fight and downed pictures, while the two lines that mattered in the fight sat in the corner where eyes rarely go. The drafted five-kinds rule is right and the research backs it. It should go further in four places: lasting states become icons, a stale danger is dropped instead of moved to the feed, the rate cap counts per slot after repeats merge, and a teammate who is down ranks with danger to you. For Friday, the fixes that matter most are a short feed (3 lines, 5 s, then fully gone) and a danger slot moved down into the area players actually watch.

How to read this: a claim with a link is sourced. **(inference)** marks my own reasoning from the sources, which no study or studio published. **(game file)** and **(measured)** mark what I read in `dark_raiders.html` or measured in tonight's four 1080p pictures.

## Fighting players watch a small circle and miss the corners

The eye-tracking evidence is narrow but consistent. In a custom shooter, about **88% of fixations** fell inside the middle half of each screen axis, and the HUD regions got **2%**, with one player making half of those ([Kenny 2005](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/282/1/Paper04_ECMS_2005.pdf)). Better players were even more centred (**91% against 84%**). A CS:GO study found the same: the more skilled the player, the more the gaze stays at the crosshair ([Khromov 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.03200)). Every study with numbers is first person; none covers a top-down game. In a 3/4 view game, the gaze anchors are the character and the reticle, and the band between them takes the place of the crosshair **(inference)**. One more placement rule holds up: put information beside where the player expects action, not on top of it ([Caroux 2011](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51247061_Maximizing_Players'_Anticipation_by_Applying_the_Proximity-Compatibility_Principle_to_the_Design_of_Video_Games)).

The side of the eye notices that something changed but cannot read it. Peripheral vision picks up flicker and motion well, but nearby shapes crowd each other until words cannot be identified ([Strasburger 2011](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/23648/1/jov_11_5_13.pdf)). A new object appearing is what grabs attention, more than a value changing in place ([Sunny and von Muhlenen 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00958/full)). In a Resident Evil 5 user test, a brief text message flagged breakable objects and **only one player noticed it** ([Fagerholt and Lorentzon 2009](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/111921.pdf)). The Caliber team found players ignore HUD parts unless they are animated and highlighted, and summed it up as "too many visuals are effectively the same as none" ([Caliber 2023](https://playcaliber.com/en/news/638/about-our-approach-to-hud-design/)). A stack of small text rows in a corner is the worst case: it is peripheral, crowded and mostly static **(inference)**.

Memory and load set the count. The best supported limit is **about four chunks held in mind at once** ([Cowan 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286/)), and the fight already uses some of them. That makes **three short callouts the practical ceiling** in combat **(inference)**. Sound does not fix this on its own: in an aviation-style task, **29% of alarms were missed under low load and 46% under high load** ([Frontiers 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4933714/)). The alerts that matter most need both a sound and a new shape near the character **(inference)**. Skill matters too. In Battlefield 3 with the HUD switched off, experts felt more immersed and novices did not ([Iacovides 2015](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1470398/1/chip0179-iacovidesA.pdf)). For a party of first-time players, that argues for "show early, fade later", not "show nothing" **(inference)**.

## Shipped shooters give each message one home and say it where it lives

The extraction shooters closest to PILLAGERS converge on a few fixed homes. ARC Raiders puts the compass at the top centre with the round clock right under it, health and squad bars bottom left, and weapon and ammo bottom right. Each exit's own countdown lives on the map, and calling a lift sets off an alarm in the world, not a HUD line ([ARC wiki](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Game_UI)). Helldivers 2 puts the compass across the top, vitals bottom left, and objectives top right ([Helldivers wiki](https://helldivers.wiki.gg/wiki/Heads-Up_Display)). Hunt shows a teammate as downed, burning or dead with icons, and shows a banish as a scorch mark that grows on the map ([Hunt wiki](https://huntshowdown.wiki.gg/wiki/HUD)). The habit they share is to **say it where it lives**. Apex Season 23 made the heal icon flash at low health and the ammo counter pulse red at low ammo, instead of adding a banner ([Siege.gg](https://siege.gg/news/apex-legends-season-23-patch-notes)). ARC Raiders dropped enemy health bars and damage numbers and lets the machines show damage instead ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/arc-raiders-lacks-enemy-hp-bars-since-embark-doesnt-want-you-playing-the-excel-sheet-of-a-game)). Apex's kill feed reports anti-cheat bans but deliberately skips quits and disconnects, a filter on system notices ([Apex wiki](https://apexlegends.fandom.com/wiki/October_31,_2023_Patch)).

Top-down games have one advantage that first-person games do not. Arrowhead's creative director said that in the top-down Helldivers every player sees what the team is doing, while in the third-person sequel each player has their own view of events ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2024/03/12/helldivers-2-how-arrowhead-turned-the-twin-stick-shooter-original-into-a-bombastic-ps5-sequel/)). So a top-down HUD does not need text for things already visible on screen, such as your own kills or an enemy standing in view. Its messages are best spent on what is off screen or hidden **(inference)**. For things off screen, the usual tool is an arrow pinned to the screen edge that shows only while the target is out of view. Tooling offers an oval inset that keeps those arrows out of the corner panels ([PlayMaker docs](https://hutonggames.com/playmaker/docs/guides/ui-widgets/target-managers/offscreen-indicator/)).

The clutter complaints point the same way. Diablo IV beta players reported status words repeating over every monster about every half second, plus a centre banner for "skill not ready" ([Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d4/t/please-please-remove-the-floating-combat-text/13017)), and the game later added a mode that hides all floating combat text ([Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/the-best-builds-in-diablo-4-deal-billions-of-damage-players-say-its-much-better-without-numbers,1118780/)). The lesson is to announce a state once when it changes, not every frame it is true **(inference)**. The Ascent, an isometric shooter, was panned for tiny UI text ([Windows Central](https://windowscentral.com/ascent-review)). Call of Duty Modern Warfare 4's launch patch this month cut its guide-character notifications ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/modern-warfare-4-launch-patch-notes-explained/)). Black Ops 6 Zombies added sliders for the score feed and notifications after fans called its HUD cluttered ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/call-of-duty/treyarch-promised-black-ops-6-map-and-hud-changes-after-cod-next-concerns-2924608/)). Hunt 1896 moved nearly every UI element at once, reviews turned negative, and Crytek shipped fixes in stages ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/hunt-showdown/1896-ui-fix)). Most of that anger was about menus, but the safe rule carries over: change one region at a time and keep homes stable **(inference)**.

## One slot per channel, merge repeats, drop what goes stale

The clearest public spec of a message queue is Fortnite's HUD message device. Each layer shows **one message at a time**, and different layers can show together. A higher-priority message pushes the current one **back into the queue** instead of deleting it, and a "display immediately" setting skips everything. A message's own repeat is not queued twice by default: a repeat of a line already on screen **resets its timer**. The default life is **5 s**, and the default queue setting is **not to queue at all**, so a message that cannot show at once is dropped. An option re-checks a queued message just before it shows ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/using-hud-message-devices-in-fortnite-creative)). Halo 3 folds a burst of kills into one escalating medal using a **4 s window** between kills ([Halo wiki](https://halo.fandom.com/wiki/Halo_3_medals)). A System Shock 2 port played a sound for every message with no limit, and the fix was one central queue where the alert sound fires once per posted message, not from each caller ([shock2quest issue](https://github.com/tommy-xr/shock2quest/issues/916)). A design guide sums up combat suppression: in heavy combat, only critical data such as health and ammo should show, and popups animate in 200 to 300 ms ([Generalist Programmer](https://generalistprogrammer.com/game-ui-ux-design)).

Duration numbers come from subtitles, because no studio publishes alert timings. Readers manage about **12 to 15 characters a second**, each line needs at least 1 s, and subtitles stay up **1 to 6 s** ([EASIT](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/infpro/2021/248286/EASIT_IO5_report._Annex_12._subtitling_instructions.pdf)). Accessibility practice puts a full subtitle on screen for **at least 2.5 s** ([CMF](https://cmf-fmc.ca/now-next/articles/subtitles-in-video-games-the-best-practices/)). Together they give a working rule: show a line for 1 s plus 1 s for every 15 characters, never less than 2.5 s and never more than 6 s, plus about 1 s more when the line is out at the screen edge **(inference)**. "the pillbox has seen you" is 24 characters, so it needs about 2.6 s. The game gives an alert 3.5 to 4.5 s today **(game file)**, which is fine.

Two numbers are missing from every source. No studio publishes a "most lines per second" cap, and none publishes how long a queued alert may wait before it is too old. Those numbers have to be set by reasoning and playtest, and the job below marks them as inferences. Tutorials follow the same logic. Good practice shows a prompt only where and when the action is possible ([Generalist Programmer](https://generalistprogrammer.com/game-ui-ux-design)), players take in little new information in intense moments ([UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/the-ux-of-video-game-tutorials-7e7bc37e2ceb)), and Modern Warfare 4 cut its guide notifications and moved its tutorials ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/modern-warfare-4-launch-patch-notes-explained/)).

## Every alert needs a second sense, big type and few words

Xbox's guidelines say any visual cue critical to play must also reach another sense. Colour alone must never carry meaning, and rumble must always be paired with something else ([XAG 103](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103)). Overwatch built its mix so threat decides loudness: an enemy aiming at you sits higher in the mix than one nearby who is not ([PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/overwatch/overwatch-devs-on-creating-a-game-you-can-play-by-sound-and-announcing-dolby-atmos-support)). Earcon research says each family of alert sounds should have its own timbre, with rhythm or note count telling the alerts within a family apart ([Brewster](https://www.dcs.gla.ac.uk/~stephen/earcon_guidelines.shtml)). The Last of Us Part II added a glossary where players can hear every cue ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/06/09/the-last-of-us-part-ii-accessibility-features-detailed/)). For PILLAGERS that means **five sound families for the five kinds**, each fired once by the message system with its own cooldown, and none of them reusing a loot sound **(inference)**.

Text size has hard floors. Xbox asks for text at least **18 px tall at 1080p on PC and 26 px for TV viewing**, scalable to 200% ([XAG 101](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101)), and contrast of **4.5:1**, or 3:1 for text 36 px and up, measured against the worst patch of ground behind it, with plates or outlines as the fix ([XAG 102](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102)). In tonight's pictures, feed text is **about 15 to 17 px** from top to bottom and the open controls list is **about 13 px**, both under the PC floor. The danger banner is **about 29 px**, which passes **(measured, rough, from JPEG)**. A party game shown on TVs should aim for the 26 px figure in the danger slot **(inference)**. Xbox also asks that players can lengthen message times up to 10 times or dismiss them by hand ([XAG 116](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/116)), and that flashing stay under about 3 flashes a second over less than about 20% of the screen, with extra care for saturated red ([XAG 118](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/118)).

The wording research supports his house style with one adjustment. Plain short language matters: **14% of US and UK adults read below an 11-year-old's level** ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/use-simple-clear-language/)). Xbox prefers sentence case for lines of text but exempts one- and two-word labels ([XAG 101](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101)), so lower-case callouts of one to three words fit the guideline. Lower-case letters read slightly worse than capitals when text is small ([Arditi and Cho 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2016788)), so lower-case callouts should be set a little larger, not changed to capitals **(inference)**. Two more habits help: one word per thing (today the pill says "extract" and the edge marker says "extraction"), and the verb first when the player must act, as in "revive p2" **(inference)**. The Game Accessibility Guidelines also ask that essential temporary information not sit far from the player's eye line, while steady status can stay at the edges ([GAG eye line](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/avoid-placing-essential-temporary-information-outside-the-players-eye-line/)).

## The drafted five-kinds rule holds, with four changes

| Draft rule | What the research says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Only five kinds reach the screen, the rest go to a log | Corners get 2% of glances ([Kenny](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/282/1/Paper04_ECMS_2005.pdf)); too many cues equal none ([Caliber](https://playcaliber.com/en/news/638/about-our-approach-to-hud-design/)); studios are cutting notifications ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/modern-warfare-4-launch-patch-notes-explained/)) | **Supported.** Change: some leavers become an icon, map mark or world mark instead of log only; "teammate down" ranks with danger; money already has the pickup stack, so kind d is really "goal done" |
| About one new line per 2.5 s outside the alert lane | No published cap exists; 2.5 s matches the subtitle minimum ([CMF](https://cmf-fmc.ca/now-next/articles/subtitles-in-video-games-the-best-practices/)) and the game's own 2.5 s alert wait | **Keep as an inference.** Change: count per slot, not across the whole screen, and only after repeats merge |
| Lines waiting over 4 s are dropped, unless money | Fortnite's default is stricter: no queue at all ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/using-hud-message-devices-in-fortnite-creative)) | **Change.** Danger and "did not work" answers drop after 1.5 s; teammate news waits up to 4 s, re-checked before it shows; goal done waits out a fight. The money exception is not needed, because money rows go to the pickup stack, which never waits (game file) |
| Danger always shows at once | Matches Fortnite's setting that shows a message at once and skips the queue | **Supported.** Add: one danger at a time, repeats merged by source with a cooldown, and a danger that could not show is dropped. Today it moves into the feed after 2.5 s (game file) |

## PILLAGERS raid HUD, region by region

The ladder below uses Fortnite's sourced mechanics (slots, skipping the queue, requeue, drop) with my ranking **(inference)**. **P0** is danger to you now, or your teammate down. **P1** is a change in extraction. **P2** is other teammate news. **P3** is a goal or contract done. **P4** is a one-line answer to something you tried that did not work. Each region holds only one kind, so P0 never waits behind a lower line. In the danger slot, P0 replaces P1 at once and P1 goes back to wait its turn. Equal ranks keep the game's current 2 s minimum before one replaces another. Two lanes sit outside the ladder: the pickup stack (his ruling, unchanged) and the medal row (his Halo ruling). At most **one danger line, three feed lines and one answer line** show at once, plus the pickup stack, which is unchanged **(inference)**.

### Top centre: clock, extract pill, nothing else

Keep the clock with the extract pill under it. That is ARC's layout, with each exit's detail kept elsewhere ([ARC wiki](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Game_UI)). Extraction changes should happen **on the pill**: it turns teal when an exit opens, amber when one is closing, shows a countdown in its last minute, and pulses once when its state changes **(inference)**. The arcade score box stays. Weather and time of day ("fog", "dawn") belong as a small icon beside the clock, set at the ascend, not as a feed line **(inference)**. The danger banner moves out of this region (see the centre).

### Top left: pillager board, folded

The bots and their money are reference information, not alerts. Keep the board folded during play as v35.36 does, and add no new message here **(inference)**.

### Top right: contracts tick in place

Helldivers 2 puts objectives top right ([Helldivers wiki](https://helldivers.wiki.gg/wiki/Heads-Up_Display)). When a contract moves forward, only that row unfolds for about 4 s with its new count, then folds away again (a design guide describes progress that shows when gained and then hides itself: [Generalist Programmer](https://generalistprogrammer.com/game-ui-ux-design)). Progress never also writes a feed line. Only a finished contract adds one feed line and a short reward tone **(inference)**. The money number stays as it is.

### Centre: one danger slot above, one answer line below

This is the only region players look at in a fight, so it gets the urgent lines and nothing else. **The danger slot** moves down from about 16% of screen height today **(measured)** to **about 27% (y about 290 at 1080p)**. That is the top edge of the zone where 88% of fixations land ([Kenny](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/282/1/Paper04_ECMS_2005.pdf)), about 250 px above a centred character and off the character itself ([Caroux](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51247061_Maximizing_Players'_Anticipation_by_Applying_the_Proximity-Compatibility_Principle_to_the_Design_of_Video_Games)). It holds one line at about 30 px with a plate, scales in over about 200 ms, and lasts 3 s (4 s for "down", or as long as the state holds). Its sound is the danger family, plus rumble on a pad. The same source and words do not show again for about 10 s **(inference for position, size and timings)**. P1 extraction moments ("extract open", "extract closing", "extracting") share this slot in teal. In the two-window party setup, the teammate's "p2 down" is P0 in the other window, shown in the teammate's colour (P1 amber, P2 cyan) with an edge arrow pointing to them **(inference)**. The source of a danger also gets its world mark: the "!" over the enemy that saw you, as now, and an edge arrow if it is off screen.

**The downed card** today is a large box at screen centre, about 480 by 200 px **(measured)**. When the camera centres the player, that box covers the player. Shrink it into the danger slot: the timer, the bar and "[f] self-revive · 1 left" **(inference; check the camera)**. **The answer line** sits about 70 px under the character's feet, small (at least 20 px), for 2 s, once per button press, with a soft "no" click: "health full", "no ammo", "locked". The Diablo IV complaints show that a centre banner for small failures annoys players ([Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d4/t/please-please-remove-the-floating-combat-text/13017)), so it stays small and never repeats while a key is held **(inference)**. "backpack full" stays in the pickup stack as it is today. **Edge arrows** show only while the target is off screen, pinned to an oval inset from the edges so they never land on a corner panel or the pickup stack ([PlayMaker docs](https://hutonggames.com/playmaker/docs/guides/ui-widgets/target-managers/offscreen-indicator/)). "extraction c available" becomes an arrow with "c 152m" **(inference)**.

### Left feed: three lines, five seconds, then gone

Today the feed can show **10 lines**. Each stays bright for **6 s**, then drops to **60%** and stays until ten newer lines push it off **(game file)**. Cut it to **3 lines**. Each lives **5 s** (Fortnite's default), fades in over about 0.2 s and fades **fully out** over about 0.4 s. At most **one new line arrives every 2.5 s**, and repeats merge into a counted line ("x2") that resets its time **(numbers are inference, mechanics from [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/using-hud-message-devices-in-fortnite-creative))**. It carries only P2 teammate news ("p2 revived", "p2 extracted") in the teammate's colour, and P3 goal-done lines, which wait until no shot has been fired or taken for about 4 s and then show as one merged line **(inference)**. The feed is out at the edge where glances rarely go, so it holds nothing urgent. That makes **his order to move the whole left cluster further left safe**: the urgent lines now live in the centre, so the left can sit flush against the edge **(inference)**. Kill lines leave the feed, because the top-down view already shows your kills and medals and SP rows already reward them ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2024/03/12/helldivers-2-how-arrowhead-turned-the-twin-stick-shooter-original-into-a-bombastic-ps5-sequel/) for the shared view; the rest is inference).

Tonight's feed lines go elsewhere. "the pillbox has seen you" and "spotted by the overseer" go to the danger slot. "location revealed" becomes an icon by the vitals for as long as it is true. "running slowly" never shows in a raid: auto resolution deals with it, and it can appear on the pause screen if it lasts. "codex entry" goes to the log and the after-raid card. "fog" becomes the clock icon. "stronghold 1 on the map" becomes a map mark. "golden enemy" is carried by the enemy's own glow, plus an edge arrow when it is off screen. "contract item nearby" is a place, so it belongs on the map; its own item label stays exactly as it is. He should judge that one, since it is close to looting **(all inference)**.

### Bottom left: vitals carry their own warnings

Vitals stay here, as in ARC and Helldivers 2 ([ARC wiki](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Game_UI)). Low health shows on the hp bar as a slow pulse under 3 a second, not as a text line ([Siege.gg](https://siege.gg/news/apex-legends-season-23-patch-notes); [XAG 118](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/118)). Lasting states sit as icons beside the "standing" chip: "location revealed", and "concealed · in cover", which today sits bottom right next to the pickup stack **(inference)**. The medal row stays above the vitals (his ruling), one medal at a time, chaining kills within 4 s ([Halo wiki](https://halo.fandom.com/wiki/Halo_3_medals)). The folded "f1 controls" chip stays mid-bottom left (his ruling). When F1 opens the list, it should open toward the left edge, not out into the play space beside the character as it does in tonight's calm picture **(inference, per his "moves to the left" note)**.

### Bottom centre: hotbar only

The hotbar and the gun name shown once on a swap (v35.36) stay. An empty or low slot shows on the slot itself, not as a line **(inference from [Siege.gg](https://siege.gg/news/apex-legends-season-23-patch-notes))**.

### Bottom right: ammo warns on the counter

The ammo count pulses red when low, and a small "r" key cap appears on the panel only when the gun is empty ([Siege.gg](https://siege.gg/news/apex-legends-season-23-patch-notes) for the pattern; the key cap is inference). The "concealed · in cover" chip moves to the left with the stance chip.

### Right pickup stack: unchanged

By his ruling, the stack, its six rows, the 4 s life, the loot sounds, the money and SP rows and the item labels stay exactly as they are. The research agrees with what it already does: one row per item, repeats counted ("scrap metal x3"), a short fixed life, newest at the bottom ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/using-hud-message-devices-in-fortnite-creative) on merging repeats). The only rule here is that no other message may draw in its column **(inference)**.

## Build jobs, most impact first

1. Route every say() line by the five kinds, so the 27 lines with no kind and the 78 info lines go to the run log by default, each of the 40 warnings is sorted into "danger to you now" or the log, and the log can be read on the pause screen so nothing is lost.
2. Reshape the left feed to 3 lines that live 5 s and fade fully out, with one new line per 2.5 s, merged repeats, a 4 s wait limit with a re-check, and goal-done lines held until the fight is over.
3. Move the danger banner down to about 27% of screen height, show one at a time, merge repeats by source with a 10 s cooldown, give teammate-down the same top rank, drop stale dangers instead of sending them to the feed, and shrink the downed card into this slot.
4. Move the left cluster flush left, as he ordered: the feed, the medal row, the f1 chip and the opened controls list, so nothing stands in the play space beside the character.
5. Turn lasting states into icons beside the stance chip ("location revealed", "concealed", "in cover"), and show low health on the hp bar and low ammo on the ammo counter as a slow pulse instead of text.
6. Put extraction state on the top-centre pill (colour and last-minute countdown) and make the edge marker an arrow with "c 152m", using the danger slot only for open, closing and extracting.
7. Add the small answer line under the character for actions that did not work, once per press for 2 s, while "backpack full" stays in the pickup stack untouched.
8. Give each of the five kinds its own short sound family, fired once by the message system with a cooldown per kind, and pad rumble for top-rank danger only, with no loot sound touched.
9. Raise text to the Xbox floors at 1080p (feed and answer line at least 20 px, controls list at least 18 px, danger slot about 30 px) and check contrast over the lightest and darkest ground.

## Conclusion

The clutter is not a wording problem, and it is not the 338 call sites. Every one of those lines gets a slot on screen, and one feed holds them long after they matter. The research points to sending lines to the right place, not to rewriting them. Once each kind has its own home and a short life, most lines never need to be drawn at all. That also settles the apparent clash between his "move it all left" order and the eye-line rule: the left edge is a fine home for a quiet log, as long as nothing urgent lives there.

Nobody has measured how these numbers feel in a top-down game, so the 27% slot, the 3-line feed and the 2.5 s rate are starting points for his playtest. He should watch for two things on Friday: whether he ever misses something that now lives only in the log, and whether the lower danger slot covers enemies coming from above the character. If either happens, the slot height and the list of kinds are the levers to change, not the number of lines.
