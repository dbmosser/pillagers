PILLAGERS already does most of what the genre learned the hard way. ## Friday's real risk is the rig the tests cannot see

Your top party fears are stutter or harsh sound, and the second pad or window failing. Both live where the test robots never go. Hidden test browsers draw in software by default ([Chromium source](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/chrome/browser/headless/headless_mode_switches.h)), so the robots catch crashes but cannot see what the graphics card spends. **The 4K numbers so far come from a hidden test browser running at 30 frames a second.** Nobody has measured the smooth look, now the default, at 4K on a real card with two windows open. The smooth look still paints some glows and the full-screen edge shading fresh every frame. Drawing them once and reusing them is the safest frame win the research found. The queued 2-player frame job covers part of it, but only at 1080p, so it needs the 4K two-window number to know if it is enough. The helpers can take that number on the party PC while you are away. Watch for stutter in a big fight on the TVs.

The pads are the same story. Chrome's gamepad engineer Matt Reynolds wrote in September 2024 that on some systems the input paths "have focus requirements that prevent background access" and that behavior **"can differ between different gamepads on the same system"** ([W3C list, Sept 2024](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-webapps-github/2024Sep/0476.html)). The game is already built for this. The window in front passes the other window's pad along, each pad belongs to one player, a pad that drops in a raid pauses the game and says so, and your 10-18 test showed both fullscreen windows getting pad data. What has never been seen is the real rig in its awkward cases: focus on another app, a pad unplugged and plugged back in, a pad that went to sleep, and two identical pads. Those need real hands on real pads, and you play blind by your own call, so let Wednesday's rehearsal (10/28, you alone with two pads on both TVs) do it: the run report notes every pad drop, return and window switch, and the helpers read it the next morning, with Thursday and Friday left for fixes. Watch for a pad that stops answering after it sleeps or after a click on another app.

Two smaller checks round it out. Guns and impacts have no limit on how many play at once; the newer noises and the siren do. A cap per sound kind, where the oldest copy stops first, is cheap and aims straight at "harsh sound". The party runs about three hours, and the game counts each of its picture stores but keeps no total. One store grew once before and was fixed. A three-hour soak with a running total settles it. One thing needs nothing: on Windows, Chrome stops drawing any window that is fully covered ([Chromium blog](https://blog.chromium.org/2021/12/chrome-windows-performance-improvements-native-window-occlusion.html)), and the game **already keeps the host window's raid running** when that happens.

## A revive under fire is the one co-op call to make this week

Today a downed pillager has **17 seconds**. A partner's revive is a 3.2 second hold. Each enemy hit on the downed pillager takes 3 seconds off, and the clock keeps running during the revive. So a partner who starts a revive while a pillager bot is shooting often watches it run out. According to its fan wiki (not rechecked by the fact check), Project Lazarus, a co-op zombie shooter, pauses its 45 second bleed-out while a revive is in progress, so a player being revived cannot die from the timer ([Project Lazarus wiki](https://project-lazarus.fandom.com/wiki/Downed_Players)). That is a new rule, so it waits for your yes; the base numbers stay your call. If you do not answer, it stays as it is. The cuts to demo and easy damage on a downed pillager are already queued. One correction: the first pass said shipped games use a 25 to 50 second bleed-out. The fact check found that Delta Force's 50, 40 and 25 second timers are only a first stage, followed by a **three-minute final stage** in which a teammate can still revive ([HiTech Gazette](https://hitechgazette.com/hidden-mechanics-for-medics-in-delta-force/)). Your 17 seconds is shorter than every example found, but there is no industry band to match.

Extraction is the other co-op moment strangers remember. Helldivers 2 calls the shuttle, holds a defend phase of about two minutes, and **gives the rest of the squad 20 seconds once the first player boards** ([Helldivers wiki](https://helldivers.wiki.gg/wiki/Extraction)). PILLAGERS already has the defend-then-board shape: the ship call starts a 30 second wait and a siege that grows with the haul, then a short boarding hold with a last call. The gap is the partner. When the host extracts first, the partner's ship is cleared. The fix is queued; it should make Friday afternoon's party copy, because losing the ride after a good raid is the ending a guest takes home.

## Strangers play the defaults and the first two minutes

Defaults decide what a walk-up crowd feels. Ubisoft's accessibility team reported that **95 to 97% of players kept subtitles on when they started on**, while only about 60% switched them on when they started off ([Shazoo](https://shazoo.ru/2019/06/27/81229/ubisoft-bolshinstvo-gejmerov-igrayut-s-subtitrami); [Ubisoft](https://news.ubisoft.com/es-es/article/5PU9CjjhOINxTzEQOtQJI0/accesibilidad-en-ubisoft-creando-juegos-para-todos)). At the party, whatever is on is what everyone plays. Screen shake and pad rumble still start at full; halving both, your own "about half" answer, is queued. A 2024 study of 1,699 players found that **feedback tied to what the player did raised motivation, while feedback that was simply bigger lowered it** ([Kao and others, CHI 2024](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/253c32f5-d124-4a23-88b5-9aaaf114608d)). So half is a safe default, and the big jolts belong on kills, blasts and extractions. At the rehearsal, watch whether fights still feel punchy at half.

The first two minutes carry the pitch. A solo developer who launched a canvas browser game in 2026 found that its first two minutes looked generic, and most players left before what made the game different showed up, around the second run; the lesson was "fix your first session before the traffic arrives" ([itch.io forum postmortem](https://itch.io/t/6645181/launch-week-postmortem-solo-browser-td-product-hunt-a-portal-rejection-and-a-flagged-show-hn-numbers-inside)). The often repeated "90 seconds to decide" figure has no source behind it, so this postmortem is the better guide. Your round 2 answer put the first fight at 15 to 20 seconds. The game records first contact, but nobody has measured it in demo raids. Measure it this week. If it runs well past 20 seconds, your parked pacing answers (first fight in 15 to 20 seconds, a planned enemy order, a final rush) can come back, but only on your word, since you parked them on 10-19. Show developers also say keep it easier than you think ([Bearmask](https://bearmask.itch.io/lofi-room/devlog/133337/g-star-2019-postmortem-big-indie-pitch-win)); the demo already offers easy at the start of each run.

How you watch matters too. The party runs itself and you check in now and then, by your own call. Show developers agree that **watching silently** gave the most useful feedback, and that when a guest asks what to do, **one spoken objective** is enough ([McRae, Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/5-expo-tips-for-indies)). That is you talking, not a prompt on screen, so it does not touch your no-tutorial rule, and the screen recordings you asked for cover the times you are away. Deaths bunched in one spot point to a design problem, not difficulty ([Articos guide, 2026](https://www.articos.com/blog/games-user-research)). Party run reports already save seat, why the run ended, score, kills and frame stats. Adding time to first kill, where each death happened, pad or keyboard and the preset, demo downs and revives, and the last screen turns Friday into data, as your process answer 66 asked. Last, the noise marks already show sounds you can hear but not see. A mark for a heavy attack coming from off screen is the logged next step. ARC Raiders' early machines were hated because players could not read their attacks, and opinion improved sharply after the reworks ([Shazoo](https://shazoo.ru/2026/03/14/180994/pervaia-versiia-vragov-v-arc-raiders-byla-sliskom-xardkornoi)). It needs your yes.

## Thanksgiving hinges on saves that survive itch's shared storage

On itch's default game domain, every browser game shares one storage area. An itch admin confirmed that storage is tied to that domain ([itch.io notice](https://itch.io/post/8952630/view-in-topic)), and developers report games there **seeing each other's saved data** ([itch.io forum](https://itch.io/t/1158456/html5-local-storage-seems-to-be-shared-between-multiple-games)). Browsers treat that area as one unit: when space runs out it is all deleted together, and Safari with tracking prevention on **deletes it after seven days without a click or tap** ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)). One developer on Brave saw saves vanish about every half hour inside the itch frame ([itch.io forum](https://itch.io/t/5464689/web-browser-game-randomly-losing-localstorage-data)). PILLAGERS already keeps three rolling daily copies, an undo for restores and a save file export, and it survives a full store. What is missing: the save is written over itself in one step with no read-back, there is no second copy in a separate store, and the game never asks the browser to keep its data. Players never see the fix unless it saves them. The itch save stays separate from your own save at home; the publish notes already tell friends that.

The first pass warned that Firefox players might get no pad input inside itch's frame. The fact check overturned that. Firefox's first strict rule broke sites and was relaxed, and **the gamepad standard now lets embedded games read pads by default** ([Chromium notice](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/B1QUuICApvc/m/RRxZLqG4HQAJ); [W3C standard](https://w3c.github.io/gamepad/)). The leftover risk is in the game: the pad read has no guard, so if any browser ever blocks it, every frame stops. A guard is a few minutes of work. Then the helpers check a private upload in Chrome, Edge and Firefox; real pads on the real page come with the couch check below. So far, links between windows were tested only in Chrome and Edge, never on the real itch page and never with pads.

The page gets one shot. itch's guidelines say a published page "can not be put on the top of the Most Recent page again," and that draft and restricted pages are not eligible for browse ([itch.io guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines)), so the private friends and family link should not use up that slot. In a survey of 169 developers, **browser-playable pages turned about 37% of views into plays, against about 7% for downloads**, the median game had **1,582 lifetime views**, and most games get almost no traffic after the first month ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/05/12/benchmark-itch-io-traffic/)). The publish plan already sets an embedded game at 1920x1080 with the fullscreen button and bare page text. Still open: the mobile flag (off unless tested), suggested tags only, tested browsers only, a still cover image, and a two-pad couch check on the real page. Nothing on Valve's calendar lands on Thanksgiving week; the Steam Winter Sale starts Dec 17 ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events)). No data on itch traffic during Thanksgiving week was found.

For the friends and family link, testers who know you tend to say what they think you want to hear ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/ux-walkthrough-anatomy-of-a-usability-test-in-video-games-part-1-)). Read their runs for behavior: did they start a second raid, where did they die, did they extract. A twin-stick veteran's rule is to treat a complaint as real when **three or more people raise it on their own** ([Venturelli, Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/everything-i-learned-about-dual-stick-shooter-controls)). In the 1,699-player study, curiosity was the only thing that predicted how long people kept playing ([Kao and others](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/253c32f5-d124-4a23-88b5-9aaaf114608d)), so "did they want one more raid" beats "did they like it".

The design rows for the release build on systems already in place. Marathon fell **more than 96% below its Steam peak** ([The Game Post](https://thegamepost.com/marathon-player-count-96-launch-bungie-season-3/)) amid reviews about confusing onboarding ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-reviews-praise-Marathon-while-Reddit-warns-of-punishing-gameplay.1247580.0.html)). Teaching through the first room's layout is the version that fits "no tutorial prompts". ARC reworked its Fireball so its weak point opens during its attack ([Shazoo](https://shazoo.ru/2026/03/14/180994/pervaia-versiia-vragov-v-arc-raiders-byla-sliskom-xardkornoi)). Your Overseer already overheats after its sweep, and any vent hit stuns a machine, but the bulwark and warden have no such beat. ARC's PvE test lets machines find a downed player who crawls but ignore one who lies still ([ARC Raiders](https://arcraiders.com/news/pve-toggle-beta-test)); in PILLAGERS machines never see a downed pillager at all, and only pillager bots do. Arrowhead's rule that a great co-op game "makes players want to play together" ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2024/03/12/helldivers-2-how-arrowhead-turned-the-twin-stick-shooter-original-into-a-bombastic-ps5-sequel/)) points at a shared resupply or a two-player objective. ARC added a machine that stalks the edge of vision in October 2026 ([IDC Games](https://idcgames.com/en/arc-raiders/news/arc-raiders-opens-the-studio-doors-again-to-explain-how-its-first-year-changed-the-game-2026-10-02-09-00-14009)). Each of those is a new rule or feature and waits for your order. The reduce-motion link and the hit jolt fix are small fixes that need no new rule.

## Half the genre's hard lessons are already in the game

The market shows your corner is open. Escape from Duckov sold **3 million in about three weeks** with a Steam peak over 300,000 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_from_Duckov)); it is single-player and keyboard and mouse only ([Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2025/12/17/reviews/148515-pc-review-escape-from-duckov/)). Zero Sievert passed 600,000 copies ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1782120/ZERO_Sievert/)) from a solo developer ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/01/23/zero-sievert-1-million-revenue-cabo-studio-extraction-shooter-steam)) and, correcting the first pass, **has had full pad support since February 2024** ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/02/single-player-extraction-shooter-zero-sievert-adds-controller-support/)). Its sequel gathered about 100,000 wishlists in two weeks ([Rutab](https://rutab.net/b/video-games/2026/05/18/zero-sievert-2-sobrala-100-000-zhelayuschih-v-steam-za-dve-nedeli.html)). So the open gap in top-down extraction is co-op on one PC, not pads alone. ARC Raiders' studio surveyed its players: **37% play friendly co-op, 27% mixed, 34% PvP**, and 51% showed interest in a PvE option; its PvE test lowered loot value to make up for the added safety ([ARC Raiders](https://arcraiders.com/news/pve-toggle-beta-test)). A co-op raid against machines and pillager bots sits where most players lean. No browser or itch top-down extraction hit was found, so there is no direct comparison.

The genre's biggest lessons are already in. ARC's first sentry had no aiming cone and shot in any direction, like an aimbot, in a translated report ([Shazoo](https://shazoo.ru/2026/03/14/180994/pervaia-versiia-vragov-v-arc-raiders-byla-sliskom-xardkornoi)); yours shows its sight cone, is blind behind and paints a red aim lane before it fires. ARC found the real cause of enemy hate was unclear counters; each of your machines is built around one answer. ARC slowed everything by about 60% ([OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/news/27964/the-first-thing-embark-did-after-making-arc-raiders-an-extraction-shooter-was-making-it-slower)); you have the stamina bar and a held sprint. Duckov reviewers say you hear an enemy before you see it ([GameGrin](https://www.gamegrin.com/reviews/escape-from-duckov-review/)); your noise marks and edge arrows show those sounds, and your fog hides enemies but not ground, tighter in rain, fog and storms. ARC lets a downed player crawl into the elevator and still get out ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/arc-raiders-extraction-elevators-hatches-and-timers-explained/)); so does PILLAGERS. Tarkov's lack of map, compass and ammo counter is a known newcomer killer ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/escape-from-tarkov-pc/)); you have the big map with every extract marked and a live ammo count. The free and random kits match ARC's free loadout, and your 10-minute raids sit between Fortnite's five-minute Blitz matches ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/144783/fortnite-blitz-royale-mode)) and ARC's 30-minute raids.

Co-op, pad and browser basics are in too: one round dead zone that keeps diagonals smooth ([Venturelli](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/everything-i-learned-about-dual-stick-shooter-controls)), rumble tied to events with an off switch, a seat ring with the player number beside amber and cyan (close to a colour-blind-safe orange and sky blue pair, [NYU Siegal Lab](https://siegal.bio.nyu.edu/color-palette/)), a teammate edge arrow that pulses when the partner goes down, a dead partner back beside the living one in the demo, an idle countdown with a live fight behind the title, hold or toggle for every held action, key remapping, and separate volumes. Frame caps that hold their number, a resolution that drops effects before sharpness, lighting drawn at reduced size, recovery after a graphics reset, a sound limiter and frame records in the flight recorder are all in. Your 60 ms hit pause on heavy rounds sits inside the 35 to 90 ms range one 2026 guide suggests, though that guide cites no studio for its numbers ([Sword Arcade](https://swordarcade.xyz/guides/game-feel-hitstop-and-screen-shake/)). The research's do-not-adopt list (friendly fire, getting your body back, gun wear, any voice lines) matches vetoes already in force; ARC's computer voices were its most cited complaint ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARC_Raiders)).

## Conclusion

The research moves the question from "what should PILLAGERS add" to "what can the tests not see". The studios paid dearly for lessons like ARC's sentry rework, its 60% slowdown and its readable counters, and those are already in your game. The fresh risks are in hardware and browser plumbing: real pads on the real rig, a real graphics card at 4K, and a shared storage area on itch. Those need hands-on runs, not features, and they cost little next to a P2 pad dying in front of guests.

With no trailer, no devlog and a bare page by your own calls, the first two minutes of play are the whole pitch on itch, just as they are at the party. The browser postmortem and itch's one-time newest-list slot point the same way: the first raid has to show what sets PILLAGERS apart, such as the forge and a two-player extraction, early, and it has to be right before the page goes public. That makes Friday more than a party. What strangers do in their first two minutes is the best preview available of what itch visitors will do at Thanksgiving, which is why the party run reports deserve their missing fields this week.
