## v11.01 - HIS NOTE: THE FEEDBACK BUTTONS AT THE END OF A RAID ARE OLD

"change the feedback buttons at the end of a raid to something more current",
2026-09-04.

**REPRODUCED BY READING THE LIST, and its own comment dates it.** Twenty-four
tags, with this written above them at v5.72: "the two music tags went where the
music went. These two are the live unknowns instead: the Listener teaching was
rewritten at v5.60 and nobody has tested it on a human, and the Choir has never
been fought at all."

Eight of the twenty-four were one-machine questions from that era: Listener
beatable, Listener unfair, Pillbox worth it, Pillbox a chore, Bulwark great,
Bulwark unfair, Howler great, Howler unfair. Every one of those machines still
exists, and he has logged dozens of runs against them since, so those questions
are answered by his own log rather than open. Meanwhile **nothing from the last
thirty builds had a tag at all**: not the grid backpack, not the hotbar, not
being downed and crawling, not the map screen, not the Undercroft, not the
weather he now chooses, and above all not "this crashed" or "this looked wrong",
which are the two things a friend cannot tell him any other way.

**WHY THIS ONE MATTERS MORE THAN IT LOOKS.** His friends are about to play. A tag
is the only feedback most people will ever give, and the run report is the only
channel back. A tag row full of questions about a machine somebody has not met
yet, with no way to say "it broke", wastes the alpha.

**THE NEW SET, thirty tags, every one judgeable in a single raid.** It opens with
the three he cannot get any other way, Something crashed, Something looked wrong,
Sound was off, then readability, which is where most of his own notes come from,
then feel, then what he carried and the room he keeps it in, then who was up
there, then the three newest systems, then the place and his oldest complaint,
then getting out. Old records keep their old strings, because a tag is just text
on a run row.

**NEW CHECK v11.01** requires a way to say it crashed, that something looked
wrong, that the sound was off and that text was hard to read; requires no more
than two of the eight v5.72 one-machine questions to survive; requires no
duplicates, nothing too long for the row, and a count between twelve and forty.
Then it drives the real thing: deploy, end the raid, press two buttons on the
real card, press a third twice, press the real way out, and require the two to
be on the logged run and the third, which was pressed twice, not to be. Controls:
the card must have drawn a button for every tag in the list, there must be a way
out of the card, and the run must have reached the log at all.

Fails on a v11.00 fixture with all five findings, including all eight stale
buttons named.

Not verified: whether he agrees with the eight I removed, which is a judgement
about what is still open and is his to overrule; and how thirty tags wrap on his
1440p and 4K screens, since the check counts them and does not measure the row.
