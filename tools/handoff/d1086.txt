## v10.86 - HIS NOTE, THE OTHER HALF: THE HUM ALSO SURVIVES A PAUSE

His note said "random humms that last way too long, like after you die, etc".
v10.85 took the death. This is the etc, and it is the one most people will
actually meet.

THE FAULT. The entire raid update sits inside a branch gated on !G.paused, and
tickAmbience, the only thing in the file that writes the ambient bed's gains,
lives inside it. Open the pause box and those gains are never written again, so
the bed holds its last level for as long as the menu is open. Pausing happens
many times in a raid. Dying happens once.

And it holds at whatever the room was. The level follows the nearest hunting
body, so pausing while something is on top of you leaves the loudest version of
the bed running underneath a completely still menu, which is the worst version
of the effect he described.

THE FIX. Duck on the frame the pause opens, once, not every frame, using the
same cut v10.85 added. Then let tickAmbience bring it back by itself when play
resumes: it already ramps toward its live target with a 0.45 time constant, so
the room returns over about a second rather than snapping back on.

Ducking once rather than every frame matters for two reasons. It avoids
cancelling and rewriting the same ramp sixty times a second under a menu that is
not moving, and it keeps v10.85's own assertion true, that the bed is never cut
during ordinary play.

NO DIAL MOVES. Nothing in this build changes a balance number, an entity count,
a spawn rate or a map's contents, per his instruction of 2026-09-04.

THE CHECK steps a real raid and asks six things: no duck across twenty frames of
ordinary play; exactly one duck on the frame the pause opens; no further ducks
across forty more paused frames; no duck on resume; the duck flag cleared so the
next pause is armed; and a SECOND pause ducking again, because a build that
ducked once per raid and never again would pass every other line. Its control is
that a build with no way to cut the bed reports that fact rather than skipping.

Not verified: this is measured as a fact about the code path, not as a fall in
volume, because the fixture makes AudioContext throw so probe runs stay silent.
Nobody has heard it. The death beat, the second or so of slow motion before a
losing raid ends, still freezes the bed rather than driving it, and endRaid cuts
it immediately afterwards, so it is under two seconds and was left alone. The
music is not ducked by a pause and I have not decided whether it should be.
