pillagers · season zero · building from 11-01, m0 first

progression plan

Where we are headed: a lineage of pillagers on one account. Each pillager grows its own Pillager Survival Level in raids and keeps it until it dies. When it dies it goes to the hall of the fallen with everything it wore and carried, and you choose the next one from three heirs, auto-named, like Rogue Legacy. What lasts is the account: the cash in your stash, your lifetime earnings and the rank they give you, the skills you bought, and the hall itself.

There is no XP and no cash in raids. You bring home gear and items, and most of your money comes from selling them, plus a small extraction bonus. Every dollar you earn adds to lifetime earnings, which sets your rank, and spending never lowers it. Cash buys everything: gear, skills, starter kits, body options, rerolls and dyes. What you wear makes you stronger, is the only way your pillager looks, and is lost when it dies.

Built on your 11-01 calls (no XP; "most the money should come from selling gear"; "lifetime earnings"; skills are bought; keep the level "until they die"; auto-names with a rename; account and pillager names separate; looks only from worn gear; the backpack is a helper piece with no loot cap) and your 100 answers and notes.

the loop

what a session feels like when this is done

a new pillagerChoose 1 of 3 heirs, each with small traits. Rename it, set its face and hair, pick a starter kit.
before you ascendDress it from the stash, sell what you do not need, buy a skill at the Mainframe, sign this week's law or none.
in the raidEverything that earns SP fills the survival level bar. Gear is in the loot boxes, 4 or 5 pieces a raid. Forge your gun and take an overclock.
getting homeThe extraction bonus lands on your account. Choose a pick for each level gained. Pieces you wore home earn a pip. Sell, and watch lifetime earnings climb.
dyingThe pillager is gone, with all it wore and carried, and takes its place in the hall. Stash cash, lifetime earnings, rank, skills and the account stay.
each weekAn optional hub law and a seeded challenge raid with a preset kit.

The pillager is the risk, the account is the safety net, the hall is the story. Gone on your word: XP and the XP ladder, cash found in raids, picking upgrades during a raid, the cloak, a wardrobe of kept looks, wearing one piece's look over another, pillager classes, two-player combo moves.

the lineage and the hall of the fallen

core
what it is
Your account holds one living pillager at a time (plus a roster of prestiged ones, see the next sheet). When it dies, the account carries on with a new one.
heirs
  • On death (or retiring), three heirs line up. Each rolls one or two small traits, good or bad: light step (quieter), nearsighted (shorter sight in fog), heavy hands (slower reload, harder melee), steady (less sway), and about a dozen more. You take one.
  • A worse trait comes with no $ bonus. You pick the draw you like.
names
  • Auto-named as name and generation: brakka iv. The generation count shows too ("your 14th pillager").
  • Rename it at birth, and any time at the Mainframe.
  • A pillager earns an epithet from how it plays (the quiet, the greedy, the lucky), shown in the hall.
  • The account name is separate. It shows in menus and the two-player lobby.
birth moment
A short scene in the Undercroft: the name card, its traits, a rename button, the body editor (random to start, edit what you like) and the starter kit choice.
starter kits
Three tiers, chosen at each birth: nothing, basic (a plain gun and bandages) and privileged (a better gun, a sound armor piece, a medkit). Your note: this is where the game can make money later. For now the player picks one each time, paid in game $ (see the open questions).
the hall
  • Both: a wall in the Undercroft with a portrait of each pillager in what it last wore, and a tab at the Mainframe with full stats.
  • It keeps every pillager, forever.
  • Each entry shows how and where it died (district, killed by), its level, epithet, raids lived, best haul and kills.
  • Lineage records on top: longest life, richest haul, most kills, highest level.
retire
A living pillager can retire into the hall. Its worn gear goes to the stash and it pays a bonus by level: $250 a level, so $5,000 at level 20. Then three heirs line up as on a death.
death rules
As today, quitting a raid early counts as a death. In two-player a downed pillager revived by the partner lives on.

pillager survival level and picks

core
what a pick is
You asked, so plainly: a pick is a small upgrade that belongs to this one pillager. Each time it gains a level you see three cards, for example "8% faster reload", "kills heal 2" or "a third grenade", and you keep one. Twenty levels means twenty picks. They are who this pillager is, and they die with it.
the bar
  • Called Pillager Survival Level, shown next to health.
  • Tied to SP: anything that earns score points fills it.
  • No picking in a raid. The bar keeps filling through the raid, level after level, and you choose your picks on the raid-end screen after you extract. Die and the unspent picks die too.
numbers
  • Cap: level 20. Pace: 2 or 3 levels a raid early, slowing later, so a pillager that lives reaches 20 in about 15 raids.
  • A pool of about 40 picks at launch. Taking the same card again strengthens it, up to three times. Two maxed picks that belong together combine into a stronger one.
  • Picks show as small badges by the pillager's name.
  • Change a pick for $ at the Mainframe.
  • Level 20 gives a capstone pick and a title in the hall.
challenge
No scaling: a high-level pillager has it easier, and that is the reward. Your note: "easier, not easy". Picks top out near +30% across all stats at level 20, and no single stat climbs past a fixed ceiling, so a level 20 still dies to carelessness.
two-player
Each player has their own pillager and their own picks. When one player levels, both get a short boost: 10 seconds of quicker reload and movement.
prestige roster
  • Your note: only a pillager that reaches level 20 can prestige. It gets a prestige star and goes onto a small roster, like Darkest Dungeon, and a new heir is born for you to play.
  • Take a prestiged pillager out any time and play it again. If it dies, it is gone and the prestige with it.
  • The roster holds three.

money and selling

core
no cash in raids
Your words: "keep it like a credit card". Cash only ever lives on the account. A raid never holds money, so a death never takes any.
income
  • Mostly selling: at the Peddler, or straight from the stash screen.
  • An extraction bonus of about 10% of the haul's value, paid when you get home.
  • Contracts pay in gear and items to sell, never cash.
  • Racks pay materials instead of $ (and get rethought, see stations).
  • No daily login bonus.
prices
  • Your note: prices "should move around based on....something". The plan: a market. Each item's price drifts day to day. What you sold a lot of this week sells for less; what nobody sold creeps up. Three wanted items a day pay 50% more. (Open question at the bottom.)
  • Buy back anything you sold today at the same price.
  • The seller's prices never rise with lifetime earnings.
numbers
A brand-new account starts with $1,000. Net worth (stash cash plus what the stash would sell for) shows on the account card.
wirt
The gamble station stays as it is, with worn gear added to what it can pay out.
hostile pillagers
They drop what they wear into the loot they leave behind, so killing a geared one pays.

lifetime earnings and ranks

core
what it is
Lifetime earnings counts every dollar earned: sales, the extraction bonus, retire bonuses. Spending never lowers it, like Halo Reach. It shows at the Mainframe and on the account card.
ranks
  • 30 named ranks, six names of five steps: scrounger, cutpurse, sacker, reaver, marauder, warlord (warlord v is the top).
  • The first rank-up comes in about two good raids; each rank needs a bit more than the last. Paced so a regular player reaches warlord in about six weeks of season zero, tuned from the run logs.
  • The rank does not show over your head in raids: there it is the pillager's name and level only.
  • Ranks never open districts or gate anything.
rewards
A reward at every rank: a gear crate, a relic, a dye, a body option, or $. This replaces the XP reward ladder and never expires; season one adds ranks on top.
old saves
Any XP ladder rewards earned but never claimed are paid as $ on first load, and the starting rank comes from lifetime earnings. Nobody loses anything.

risk
Dress at the stash before you ascend. Die and every worn piece is gone with the pillager. No insurance. The choice is the game: wear the relic and play scared, or leave it home and play loose.
where it drops
In loot boxes, like everything else (nothing lies on the ground, so no ground glow). Also machine wrecks (bigger machines, better odds), hostile pillagers, keycard rooms, contract rewards, the seller (up to rare) and Wirt. A raid averages 4 or 5 pieces. You see a piece's traits before you take it, with a tip saying better or worse than what you wear.
strength
Like Destiny 2: every piece has a power number, and drops roll near the best piece you own in that slot (worn or in the stash), now and then a step above. Better gear chases better gear, and after a death the stash keeps you from starting at zero. Total power from gear caps near +25%.
growth
Extract wearing a piece and it earns a pip, up to 3; each pip strengthens its traits by a third. Gear never wears out. Any pillager can wear any piece.
workbench
Reroll one trait for $. Salvage unwanted pieces into materials.
stash
No limit on room for gear. Saved outfits: one tap to dress.
two-player
Hand a piece to your partner in a raid (the first real trading), and the partner sees what you wear.
later
Set bonuses for 2 and 4 pieces.
demo mode
Both players get the same preset kit, nothing saved, no lineage, as today.

looks and the body

core
gear is the look
  • Your pillager looks like what it wears, and only while it wears it. Your note: no permanent cosmetics from gear. No wardrobe of kept looks, and no wearing one piece's stats with another's look.
  • Dyes, bought with $, color a piece. The dye stays on that piece and goes with it.
  • Relic pieces get a small glow.
  • Hats over hair depend on the pair: each hat and hair style is set to hide, tuck or show, so a helmet hides all hair but a cap lets a ponytail show.
  • Your partner sees your gear and dyes over the network.
the body
  • Gender, face, hair, face tattoos, skin, eyes and beard, set at birth. Each new pillager starts random and you edit what you like.
  • Some choices cost $: the basics are free; extra hair styles, beards and face tattoos are bought once and stay on the account for every future pillager.
  • Changing hair or face later costs a small $ at the bar.
the depot
The Discount Fashion Depot is frozen. Body options you already own stay yours.
new art
Headgear, armor, pants, boots, gloves and backpacks on the pillager, at least a few per slot and rarity. Rings and amulets do not show.

skills, bought on the account

core
shape
  • Bought with $ at the Mainframe. Every pillager has them, and death never takes one.
  • Three branches of 6 skills, each skill 2 steps, then a capstone where you pick one of two. Each step in a branch costs more than the last. The whole tree costs more than a season of play, so every skill competes with gear for the same cash.
  • scavenger: quieter searching, keycards that last longer, more materials from salvage. Capstone: loot boxes holding a rare or better piece show on your map, or a fourth card at every level-up.
  • gunhand: steadier aim on the move, faster swaps, cheaper Forge. Capstone: a third overclock choice, or a free MK II once per raid.
  • iron: faster bandages, longer downed time, more stamina, faster revives. Capstone: get up once per raid by yourself, or armor pieces take a hit for you before breaking.
  • Bought skills also add new picks to the pool, so the account widens what heirs can roll (about 4 per branch).
rules
Respec costs $. No free starter skills on a new account. No pillager classes.

forge overclocks

you chose
how it plays
At MK II and MK III the Forge offers two overclocks and you take one. Each gives and takes: heavy rounds (more damage, slower reload), hair trigger (faster fire, more spread), deep mag (bigger magazine, slower aim), quiet barrel (no noise ring, less range).
keeps
An overclocked gun goes home with you on extract, like a forged gun today. Remove an overclock for $ at the workbench.

weekly challenge raid

you chose
how it plays
  • One raid a week from the lift, the same map seed for every player that week.
  • Three modifiers stacked on it, for example double machines, no map, half ammo, fog all raid.
  • Played with a preset kit, so your pillager is never at risk and scores compare fairly.
  • Your best SP is kept for the week. Friends compare by trading a short score code (no server).
  • Finishing it once a week sends a rare gear piece to your stash.
today
A daily seeded raid already exists in the code, switched off. This turns it weekly and adds modifiers.

weekly hub law

you chose
how it plays
Each week the Mainframe offers three laws. Signing is optional: sign one, or none. A signed law holds until the week ends. Laws never apply in demo mode. Each gives and takes:
examples
  • contracts pay double, but machines hit harder
  • relics drop twice as often, but machines spot you sooner
  • the peddler pays a quarter more, but the seller charges double
  • the survival level fills faster, but no Forge
  • wardens roam every district, and drop rare gear
  • dying keeps your rings, but costs $1,000

build plan

order, size, what you will see

Your build order: the lineage, the level and the gear together, not one after the other. So m2 and m3 run side by side on two helper lanes and land in the same week. Each milestone ships as several small builds through the usual pipeline: a job file, the gate with a new check that fails before and passes after, pictures of every screen, then on your PC. Old saves keep loading and the save key never changes. Seed 4242 stays the same raid, because gear drops use a side dice roll. You playtest at the end of each milestone, so we change direction early if it does not feel right.
