progression plan

Where we are headed: a career where every raid pays toward a named rank, what you wear makes you stronger and is lost when you die, how it looked stays yours forever, and each week brings a new law and a new challenge raid. This page has the design for each piece, then the build order.

the loop

what a session feels like when this is done

before you ascendPick what to wear and what to leave home. Spend $ at the seller, upgrade a station, check the week's law.
in the raidMachines fill your surge bar: pick 1 of 3 upgrades. Forge your gun and pick an overclock. Find gear and maybe a relic.
getting homeEvery bar that moved on one screen: rank, pass, new looks, gear pips, station materials.
dyingEverything worn and carried is gone. Your rank, skill points, looks and stations stay.
each weekA new hub law and a new seeded challenge raid with its own high score.

Gear is the risk, rank and skills are the safety net, looks are the trophies. All ten ideas you liked fit that shape. Two-player combo moves are cut on your word.

ranks and $ to spend

you liked
ranks
  • 30 ranks in six names of five steps: scrounger, cutpurse, sacker, reaver, marauder, warlord (warlord v is the top).
  • XP comes from what already pays it: kills, loot, doors, selling, extracting. Dying still pays half.
  • Paced so a regular player reaches warlord in about six weeks of season zero.
  • The rank shows on your name over your head, at the lift, and on the raid-end screen.
each rank-up
A $ payout, one skill point, and every fifth rank a deeper stock at the gear seller.
what $ buys
  • Gear at the seller (stock rotates each day, deepens with rank).
  • Consumables and guns, as today.
  • Station upgrades (with materials), and rerolling one trait on a piece at the workbench.
  • Losing gear on death keeps $ meaningful: there is always something to replace.
today
The level exists but does nothing and is hidden. XP already flows. The shop has 8 consumables and 2 guns.

worn gear, lost on death

core
hatawareness: sight range in fog, ping range, threat warning
cloakstealth: footstep noise, how far machines spot you
armortoughness. Your armor rigs today become this slot, with rarity and traits added.
pantsmovement: speed, stamina, crouch speed
amuletone big effect; relic amulets break a rule
ringgun handling: reload, recoil, magazine
ringsecond ring; the same effect twice does not stack
scrap1 small trait. Most drops.
sound2 traits.
rare2 stronger traits.
relic2 traits and a rule-breaker.
risk
  • You dress at the stash before you ascend. Die and every worn piece is gone, like the rest of what you carry. No insurance, no keepsake pocket (the old safe pocket was removed in v12.35).
  • The choice is the game: wear the relic and play scared, or leave it home and play loose.
growth
Extract wearing a piece and it earns a pip, up to 3. Each pip strengthens its traits by a third. Losing a 3-pip relic should hurt.
drops
Machine wrecks (bigger machines, better odds), keycard rooms, wardens (always rare or better), contract rewards, the seller. A raid averages two or three pieces.
traits
About 20 to start, each a small step: 8% faster reload, 10% less machine damage, quieter footsteps, 6% move speed, faster bandages, longer downed time, faster revives. Total gear power caps near +25%, so a fresh player is never helpless.
salvage
Unwanted pieces break into the materials you already carry home (scrap, wire, cell, board), which build stations.
demo mode
Both players get the same preset kit, so walk-up play stays equal. Nothing is saved, as today.

cosmetics become the wardrobe

rethink

Your point: if gear drops on death, cosmetics need a rethink. The answer: death takes the stats, never the look.

how it plays
  • What you wear shows on your pillager: hat, cloak, armor and pants.
  • The first time you extract wearing a piece, its look joins your wardrobe for good.
  • At the stash any slot can show a wardrobe look over the real piece. Stats come from what you wear; the look is your choice.
  • Today's cosmetics from the Discount Fashion Depot move into the same wardrobe, so nothing earned is lost. Hair, face, skin and the rest stay as they are.
  • A relic look is a trophy: it says you got one home at least once. Your partner sees your looks over the network, as with cosmetics today.
new art
There is no cloak look today, so cloaks are new art. Hats, vests and pads have looks already to build from.

rule-breaking relics

you liked
examples
  • reloading drops a mine at your feet
  • every 5th shot costs no ammo
  • bandaging heals your partner too
  • opening a container pings every machine nearby on your map
  • while crouched your shots are silent
  • a kill at full health gives a short sprint
rules
Only relic rings and amulets carry one, so at most three run at once. About 12 at first, growing to 20.

level-up picks in raid

you liked
how it plays
  • Machine kills, containers and doors fill a surge bar. When it fills, three small cards slide in at the bottom of the screen.
  • No pause. Press 1, 2 or 3 (d-pad on a pad), or it picks for you after 8 seconds.
  • About 6 to 8 picks a raid, gone when the raid ends.
  • Max two related picks and they combine into a stronger one, so each raid has a build to chase.
  • In two-player each player picks their own.
examples
faster reload · kills heal 2 · a third grenade · shots ricochet once · pickups pull from further · machines show through walls for 3 s after a kill
watch for
Screen clutter, and overlap with the Forge. The cards stay small and low; the Forge stays about your gun.

forge overclocks

you liked
how it plays
At MK II and MK III the Forge offers two overclocks and you take one. Each gives and takes: heavy rounds (more damage, slower reload), hair trigger (faster fire, more spread), deep mag (bigger magazine, slower aim), quiet barrel (no noise ring, less range).
keeps
An overclocked gun goes home with you on extract, like a forged gun today.

skill tree

you liked
shape
  • Spent at the Mainframe. One point per rank, 30 in season zero. Points never leave.
  • Three branches of 6 skills, each skill up to 2 points, then a capstone where you pick one of two. That is 39 points of tree for 30 points, so you cannot take everything.
  • scavenger: quieter searching, keycards that last longer, more materials from salvage. Capstone: see gear rarity through walls, or one extra pick per raid.
  • gunhand: steadier aim on the move, faster swaps, cheaper Forge. Capstone: a third overclock choice, or a free MK II once per raid.
  • iron: faster bandages, longer downed time, more stamina, faster revives. Capstone: get up once per raid by yourself, or armor pieces take a hit for you before breaking.
  • Free respec at the Mainframe.

build the undercroft

you liked
stations
  • workbench: more recipes; reroll a gear trait; at level 3, add a pip.
  • stash: more room for gear you are keeping home.
  • rack hall: more racks and a better payout (today's racks move here).
  • peddler: sells at 55%, then 62%, then 70%.
  • seller's counter: one more gear slot in the daily stock per level.
cost
Three levels each, paid in carried-home materials plus $. Each level changes how the station looks in the hub, so you can see it grow.

season zero pass, never expires

you liked
how it plays
  • The 100-step ladder you already have (top 1.2 million XP today) is retuned so a regular player finishes in about six weeks.
  • Rewards become gear crates, $, wardrobe looks and a few relics.
  • It never expires. When season one comes, season zero stays claimable.

weekly challenge raid

you liked
how it plays
  • One raid a week from the lift, the same map seed for every player that week.
  • Three modifiers stacked on it, for example double machines, no map, half ammo, fog all raid.
  • Played with a preset kit, so your gear is not at risk and scores compare fairly.
  • Your best SP is kept for the week. Friends compare by trading a short score code (no server).
  • Finishing it once pays a weekly look.
today
A daily seeded raid already exists in the code, switched off. This turns it weekly and adds modifiers.

weekly hub law

you liked
how it plays
Each week the Mainframe offers three laws; you sign one and it holds until the week ends. Each gives and takes, so the week plays differently:
examples
  • double contract pay, but machines hit harder
  • relics drop twice as often, but you carry less
  • the peddler pays 80%, but the seller charges double
  • surge bar fills faster, but no Forge
  • wardens roam every district, and pay in rare gear
  • dying keeps your rings, but costs $1,000

build plan

order, size, what you will see

Each milestone ships as several small builds through the usual pipeline: a job file, the gate with a new check that fails before and passes after, pictures of every screen, then on your PC. Old saves keep loading; the save key never changes. The raid layout on seed 4242 stays identical, because gear drops draw from a side dice roll. You playtest at the end of each milestone before the next starts, so we change direction early if it does not feel right.

m0foundation

first
  • Today an item is just a name in the stash. Gear needs pieces that remember their slot, rarity, traits and pips, so the save gains a gear list beside the stash, added the first time an old save loads.
  • One gear lookup that speed, stamina, reload, spread, noise, detection, bandage, downed and revive times all read from. With nothing worn it changes nothing, which the gate proves on every old check.
  • Gear drops roll on a side dice roll, so seed 4242 stays the same raid.

Watch for: nothing; this milestone is invisible on purpose.

m1ranks and $

  • Rank names and curve, rank-up moment, the name tag, the raid-end line, seller stock by rank.

Watch for: whether the first ranks come fast enough to feel good.

m2worn gear

the big one
