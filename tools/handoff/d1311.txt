## v13.11 - THE WAY OUT NOW HAS A TEST, AND THE RAID CLOCK BUG WAS MINE

NOTHING IN THE GAME CHANGED. This is a guard build: the extraction path was
opened up, driven end to end for the first time, found correct, and given the
check it has never had. Saying that plainly is the point of the entry.

I OWN THE FALSE ALARM. v13.10 shipped a note claiming a deployed raid driven by
the loop does not run the raid clock and that the extraction pull never starts.
Both halves were wrong and both were my instrument. The clock does advance:
measured 0.1335 seconds over six frames, which is the first frame clamped to
0.05 plus five at 16.7ms, exactly what the loop promises. The pull never started
because calling the ship is a 1.6 second hold and I held E for six frames, which
is a tenth of a second. The note has been rewritten rather than deleted, so the
measurement that refutes it sits where the wrong one sat.

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY UNGUARDED. Every other check in the corpus ends a raid by
calling the harness, which jumps to the payout and never touches the two-stage
pull. So the call, the inbound wait, the landing, the cancel on release and the
board had run in front of a player hundreds of times and in front of a test zero
times. That is the single most important thirty seconds in the game and it was
the least tested code in the file.

WHAT THE DRIVE FOUND, each measured on a live raid at seed 4242 with real frames
and the real key. Hold E for 2.5 seconds on an open point: called, inbound 24.1
seconds, and the game pointer for the way out moved onto that point. Wind the
inbound down and run frames: the ship lands with a 29.7 second window. Hold E
again: the board fills at real time and the raid ends as an extraction at 1.4028
of 1.4. Release mid-board: the hold is gone on the next frame, it does not bank.
Get knocked down mid-board: the standing hold is lost as its comment promises,
the hold restarts from the floor, and the board still completes, which is the
hauled-aboard rule he chose at v0.90 and it holds. Search a body with X while
the ship is down and hold E at the same time: both run, the container opens, and
the extraction still fires. Put the raid clock at 0.30 seconds with the board at
1.30: the extraction wins the race by one frame rather than the timer killing a
player who did everything right.

ONE HYPOTHESIS RAISED AND REFUTED BEFORE IT COST A BUILD. I thought the 1.6
second call might be silent, which would have made a tap read as a dead key.
It is not: the call bar is drawn in three places, on the pad marker, on the
extraction row and on the downed overlay, all reading the same value. Recorded
so nobody builds it twice.

THE CHECK. Five arms, all on a live raid. A hold of well under 1.6 seconds must
not call, and the bar must be filling while it is held. The full hold must call
and must move the pointer. The landing must open a window. Releasing must clear
the board. Holding again must end the raid as an extraction.

NO FAILING CONTROL EXISTS FOR THIS BUILD AND I AM NOT GOING TO INVENT ONE. The
rule is that a fix is proved by a control that fails on the previous fixture.
This build fixes nothing, so all five arms pass on v13.10 as they should. What
was run instead is the negative arm, which is the honest version of the same
question: the same staging with E never pressed, which fires four of the five.
That is what proves the check has teeth.

Not verified: the inbound countdown itself, which is wound down rather than
waited out because it is minutes long; the siege that spawns during it; the
departure when the window is ignored; and the second and later extraction points
on a landing, since every arm runs on the first one.
