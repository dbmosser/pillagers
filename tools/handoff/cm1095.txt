v10.95: one family on the canvas, the loot pop included

His note: "the loot text that pops on screen upon looting that tells you what item
you got is still too bolded -- use the entire font across the entire game". His
second time asking.

Reproduced by counting. Every text draw in a raid frame, its HUD, the map screen
and four Undercroft frames was traced and its font recorded. Almost all of it was
already the game family in one of five roles, at three sizes because the HUD
scale multiplies them. Three were not: the item name on the loot pop at 17.2px
Titan One, PICKED UP and every other world label at 15.6px, and the faint
district numbers baked into the ground between 114 and 244px.

Titan One is the toybox display face the title wordmark is set in. At fifteen
pixels it does not read as a bold word, it reads as a slab. It was never a
weight, it was a second typeface.

It survived the August pass because the world label function measures and draws
with a font string of its own, so it never went through a role and the sweep
never reached it.

The label now measures and draws in the same roles as everything else. The small
one lands at exactly the 13 rendered pixels it already had. The large one goes
from 14.3 at weight 400 in a heavy display face to 15.6 at weight 600 in the game
face. The ground numbers and the placeholder dash on an item with no portrait
come across too. The only Titan One left is the game's own name.

New check v10.95 traces every word drawn while the map is built, a raid frame and
its HUD are drawn, the map is opened and the Undercroft runs, and requires one
family; it asks about the family and never the size. Controls: the loot pop must
actually have been drawn, found by a marker string; at least sixty words must
have been drawn; and the tracer draws one word in a foreign face and must catch
itself. The map is built inside the trace because the ground is baked once.

Fails on a v10.94 fixture with thirty words in a second typeface.

Not verified: how the lighter loot pop reads in a real raid, and the DOM, which
this check does not trace.
