# LLM coding agents for game art: code-drawn 2D art, shaders, art tooling and visual QA (2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-20. Older results (2023-2024) are marked BACKGROUND. Separator between a claim and its source is a plain hyphen (project rule: no em or en dashes).

## 1. Evidence on LLMs writing visual art as code (SVG, Canvas 2D, pixel grids, shaders) and how quality changed

### Takeaway
Frontier LLMs write recognisable art as code, but untrained "draw it in one shot" output stays crude; quality jumps when the output format is constrained (palette-indexed grid, primitives a rasteriser turns into pixels) and when rendering feedback closes the loop. Research models trained with rendered-image rewards (RLRF, VectorGym) now beat general frontier models on image-to-SVG fidelity by a wide margin, which shows the render-and-compare loop is the thing that works.

### Cited Findings
- BACKGROUND (2023): GPT-4 drawing a unicorn in TikZ was the early "LLMs can draw in code" demonstration in Microsoft's Sparks of AGI paper. - [Bubeck et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12712)
- BACKGROUND (2024): VGBench (EMNLP 2024; 4279 multiple-choice QA pairs, 5845 vector-graphic caption pairs) found LLMs strong on high-level formats such as TikZ and Graphviz but weaker on low-level SVG; chain-of-thought helped on low-level formats; generation was scored with CLIP Score and FID. - [VGBench, arXiv 2407.10972](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10972v2); [review summary](https://themoonlight.io/review/vgbench-evaluating-large-language-models-on-vector-graphics-understanding-and-generation)
- BACKGROUND then ongoing: Simon Willison's "pelican riding a bicycle" SVG test began 25 Oct 2024 with 16 models and continued into 2026 (April 2026: GLM-5.1 SVG called "excellent", but its animated version broke and needed a follow-up prompt). - [Willison 2024](https://feeds.simonwillison.net/2024/Oct/25/pelicans-on-a-bicycle/); [Willison Apr 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/7/glm-51/)
- Willison is quoted (secondhand) saying the correlation between pelican performance and actual model quality "has been mostly severed now", i.e. labs may now tune for the meme. - [OpenEnv pelican_svg docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/openenv/environments/pelican_svg) (secondary; original wording not verified)
- RLRF (NeurIPS 2025, ServiceNow + Mila): SFT on 1.7M image-SVG pairs, then GRPO where each rollout SVG is rendered (CairoSVG) and scored against the target. On image-to-SVG (SVG-Stack-Hard), frontier models scored SSIM 66.91 (GPT-4o), 69.33 (Claude 3.7 Sonnet), 60.75 (Gemini 1.5 Pro); Qwen2.5-VL-7B + RLRF reached SSIM 95.10, DINO 98.70, LPIPS 3.08. Note the paper's own tables disagree on some 7B numbers (Table 1 MSE 1.03 vs Table 2 MSE 4.42). - [RLRF, arXiv 2505.20793](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20793v2); [NeurIPS 2025 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/120143)
- RLRF reward findings: L2 pixel loss alone improved reconstruction but lowered semantic (DINO) similarity; pixel + edge (Canny) + DreamSim semantic reward + a code-length penalty worked best; RL without prior SFT did poorly. Named reward hacks: "Small ViewBox Hack", "SVG Length Collapse", "Text-in-Image Hack". Text-to-SVG output still relied on basic primitives and struggled with precise Bezier curves. - [RLRF, arXiv 2505.20793](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20793v2)
- VectorGym (2026): multitask benchmark for SVG generation, sketching and editing with human-annotated gold data; a Qwen3-VL 8B trained with GRPO plus rendering-based rewards beat Qwen3-VL 235B and matched GPT-4o; it also proposes a VLM-as-judge metric for SVG. - [VectorGym, arXiv 2603.29852](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.29852)
- Pixel Art Bench: models return a 2-10 colour hex palette plus a 24x24 grid of palette indices, rendered to an image; subjects include Mario, a rainbow, Saturn, a maze, the Mona Lisa and "hope". No leaderboard numbers were visible to me. - [Pixel Art Bench](https://emergentmind.com/pixel-art-bench)
- Hacker News (2026, thread on models "drawing" the Mona Lisa with draw/smudge tools and a canvas view): one commenter reported that about a year earlier, LLMs asked for pixel art as 2D colour-code arrays at 16x16 to 64x64 "all failed at anything beyond a basic shape"; GPT-5.6 drew the most-praised results; Claude's cat was singled out as strong; one model spent about 14.6M tokens (about $161) on the task. - [HN thread 48998404](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/48998404)
- Case study (Aug 2026, DEV Community, game COMPASSO built with Claude, called "Opus 5" in the post): procedural Canvas 2D textures "plateaued"; what worked was a palette-indexed pixel grid at native 320x224 with integer nearest-neighbour upscaling, where the model describes ellipsoids and boxes and a rasteriser casts one orthographic ray per pixel to palette indices; the locked palette is the hard constraint that "does the drawing"; gradients only via 4x4 Bayer dither; rotations are baked frames, not runtime rotation. The author's summary: a "high, cheap floor, not a high ceiling." - [jucelinux, DEV Community](https://dev.to/jucelinux/i-taught-claude-opus-5-to-draw-with-no-image-model-just-code-then-i-asked-for-a-self-portrait-2894)
- Reddit report (secondhand): a developer had the model write a custom renderer, then "draw" sprites as ASCII text that the renderer converted to pixel art; about 120 hours of work. - [search summary of Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1v00q2p/snapshots/2026-07-20T20%3A24%3A21.6759Z) (not verified)
- July 2026 developer post (anecdotal): LLMs emitting pixel art through HTML produced usable tilesets, while diffusion models gave "beautiful individual sprites, but completely broken tilesets". - [search result summary, HN/dev posts](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/48998404) (anecdotal, original post not fetched)
- Shaders: ShaderMatch (LLM4Code at ICSE 2025) has 467 real Shadertoy GLSL function-completion tasks scored by static comparison and then by comparing rendered frames; across 20+ open-source code models, even the best fail to produce working code in 31% of cases, attributed to GLSL being low-resource in pretraining. No frontier chat-model results were found. - [ICSE 2025 LLM4Code paper page](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2025/llm4code-2025-papers/13/Evaluating-Language-Models-for-Computer-Graphics-Code-Completion); [ShaderMatch space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/Vipitis/shadermatch/blob/64f1463f305f1b6d845841e8a52e41f7c47672e8/README.md)
- An informal agency test (14islands) of OpenAI, Claude, Mistral and DeepSeek writing GLSL from text found recurring syntax errors. - [14islands](https://www.14islands.com/journal/ai-generated-glsl-shaders)
- Claude itself cannot generate or edit images; it only understands them, so all Claude "art" is code or calls to another tool. - [Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)

### Inferences
- PILLAGERS' plan (agent writes code that draws at art-pixel resolution into a locked grid) matches the format that the strongest 2026 case study found works best; the Canvas 2D vector-ish drawing used today is the format that case study says plateaus.
- Rendering feedback is what moves quality in every serious result (RLRF, VectorGym, ShaderMatch's render check). An agent loop that renders and compares is the inference-time version of the same idea, so the approach is sound in principle; the open question is the quality of the critic, not the loop.
- Expect output near "competent indie floor" rather than art-director ceiling without human picks.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2025-2026 benchmark scoring frontier models (Claude 4.x/5.x, GPT-5.x, Gemini 3) on pixel-art grids or Canvas 2D sprites was found; Pixel Art Bench scores were not visible.
- StarVector's own numbers were not retrieved beyond its RLRF row (StarVector-1B + RLRF: SSIM 88.00, DINO 98.00).
- No benchmark specifically for LLM-written Canvas 2D drawing code was found.

## 2. Vision-in-the-loop: render, critique, reference images, multi-agent critics, rubric design, VLM weaknesses and mitigations

### Takeaway
Vendors now officially recommend "screenshot, compare to the design, list differences, fix" plus a separate fresh-context reviewer. But VLM judges are measurably bad at exact geometry and counting, easily fooled by brightness and other presentation changes, often lean on text over the image, and reviewers asked to find problems will invent some. The working pattern is a ladder: cheap numeric checks first, vision only for what numbers cannot see, a human last.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic's Claude Code guidance: "Give Claude a check it can run: tests, a build, a screenshot to compare"; example prompt: "implement this design. take a screenshot of the result and compare it to the original. list differences and fix them." - [Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)
- Same doc: use a verification subagent so "the agent doing the work isn't the one grading it"; a fresh-context reviewer "won't be biased toward code it just wrote." - [Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)
- Same doc, critic calibration: "A reviewer prompted to find gaps will usually report some, even when the work is sound"; chasing every finding leads to over-engineering; tell the reviewer to flag only gaps that affect the stated requirements. - [Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)
- Same doc, loop hygiene: after two failed corrections on the same issue, clear context and restart with a better prompt rather than keep correcting. Deterministic gates (Stop hooks) can block a turn from ending until a check passes. - [Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)
- Claude vision mechanics: images are seen as 28x28-pixel patches (one visual token each, cost = ceil(w/28) x ceil(h/28)); Claude 4.7 and later accept up to 2576 px long edge and 4784 visual tokens, older models 1568 px and 1568 tokens; larger images are downscaled. Images under 200 px risk hallucination; counting is approximate "especially with large numbers of small objects"; spatial outputs are approximate. - [Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)
- Claude vision prompting: images before text work best; label multiple images "Image 1:", "Image 2:" for comparison; lossy JPEG/WebP compression can introduce artifacts that hurt performance; above 20 images per request a stricter per-image size limit applies (keep each under 2000 px). - [Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)
- BACKGROUND (2024, ACCV): "Vision language models are blind" (BlindTest) found four frontier VLMs averaged 58.57% on trivially simple geometry (line intersections, touching circles, counting nested squares, grid rows and columns); best was Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 74.01% vs humans 100%. Linear probes on the vision encoders hit at least 99.47% on some tasks, so the loss happens turning vision into language, not in seeing. - [arXiv 2407.06581](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06581v4); [ACCV 2024 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ACCV2024/papers/Rahmanzadehgervi_Vision_language_models_are_blind_ACCV_2024_paper.pdf)
- "Fooling the LVLM Judges" (EMNLP 2025): every tested VLM judge inflated scores for images edited with brightness, gamma, text overlays, black padding, beauty filters or bounding boxes, in every domain; combined edits compounded; pairwise comparison was also vulnerable; prompt-based mitigation did not remove the effect. - [arXiv 2505.15249](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15249v2)
- Informativeness bias (Apr 2026): VLM judges often pay limited attention to the image and favour the more detailed answer even when it conflicts with the image. - [arXiv 2604.17768](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17768)
- Leniency: a secondary write-up reports frontier VLM judges systematically mark failed computer-use runs as successes (OSReward). - [aiweekly summary](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/osreward-frontier-vlm-judges-overrate-agent-success) (original not verified)
- Case study oracle ladder (COMPASSO): (1) compiler and build, (2) 290 numeric locks under vitest that never look at an image (distinct palette indices per frame, frame-to-frame difference, palette membership, unused baked layers), (3) a ~100-token text summary such as a colour ramp census, (4) a PNG rendered without a browser at about 200 to 1,200 tokens per sheet, (5) the human. Numeric locks caught a walk cycle whose frames were nearly identical. - [jucelinux, DEV Community](https://dev.to/jucelinux/i-taught-claude-opus-5-to-draw-with-no-image-model-just-code-then-i-asked-for-a-self-portrait-2894)
- Same case study, what slipped through: eyes shrunk below one pixel gave a blank face while all 290 locks stayed green; a rotation bug (rotating position instead of the anchor) recurred four times and the model never found it, the human did by looking; the self-portrait had 11 defects found only later. The author also found a PNG cheaper and more accurate for the model than an ASCII dump. - [jucelinux, DEV Community](https://dev.to/jucelinux/i-taught-claude-opus-5-to-draw-with-no-image-model-just-code-then-i-asked-for-a-self-portrait-2894)
- HN practitioners on drawing loops: models only move "forward" and pile on strokes or code to fix earlier mistakes, so add an undo/revert tool; optimising toward SSIM/RMSE feedback degraded drawings, one suggested embedding similarity (DINOv2) instead; crop and zoom to inspect details; draft on a scratch layer; models draw "symbols" (icon eyes) like beginners rather than from value and shape. Single runs and untuned prompts make such comparisons noisy. - [HN thread 48998404](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/48998404)

### Inferences
- Pixel art is the worst case for VLM perception: a 32x32 sprite at 1x is barely more than one 28 px patch wide. For critique images, upscale with nearest neighbour so one art pixel is large (for example 16 to 28 screen px, so a 32x32 sprite becomes 512 to 896 px), crop one sprite per image, and keep images under the 2576 px long edge. Do not hand the critic a 4K full-screen shot and ask about pixel details.
- Present reference and candidate identically (same scale, same background, same lighting/brightness, same crop, PNG not JPEG) and label them Image 1 and Image 2; otherwise presentation differences alone can swing the judge's score.
- Do not let the critic count pixels, measure outline width or check palette membership by eye; those are cheap exact checks in code (see section 4). Use the critic for silhouette, read at play size, mood, proportion and "does this look like the reference".
- Keep a best-so-far snapshot and let the loop revert, so a polish pass that makes things worse is discarded instead of patched on top.
- Give the critic a fixed rubric with anchored scores and a "no change needed" option, tell it to report only issues that matter for the stated goal, and cap polish rounds; otherwise it will always find something and the drawing will drift into over-detail.
- Consider blind pairwise "A or B, which is closer to the reference" between the last two versions instead of absolute 1-10 scores, randomising which side is which; absolute scores are where leniency bites. (Pairwise is still vulnerable per the EMNLP 2025 paper, so randomise and keep presentation identical.)

### Gaps
- No published study measured VLM accuracy on judging pixel art specifically (outline breaks, banding, pillow shading, dithering quality).
- No 2025-2026 BlindTest rerun numbers on the newest Claude or GPT models were retrieved; current models are likely better but I have no source.
- No source quantified how many critique rounds help before quality plateaus or degrades for code-drawn art.

## 3. How studios and indie devs use LLMs for art tooling (Aseprite scripts, palettes, sprite sheets, generators, shaders, pipelines, image generator plus agent)

### Takeaway
In 2025-2026 the common pattern is the LLM as tool-builder and glue: MCP servers that drive Aseprite through generated Lua, skills that replace placeholder shapes with code-built sprites, and pipelines where an image model (Nano Banana / Gemini image, GPT Image) makes frames and code slices, aligns and cleans them. Independent evidence on frame-to-frame consistency of image generators is thin and mostly vendor-supplied.

### Cited Findings
- rkdfx/aseprite-mcp: FastMCP server exposing Aseprite to Claude Desktop and other MCP clients (canvas, pixels, lines, rectangles, circles, fill, export PNG/GIF) by generating and running Aseprite Lua scripts. - [GitHub rkdfx/aseprite-mcp](https://github.com/rkdfx/aseprite-mcp)
- ext-sakamoro/AsepriteMCP v2.0 adds palette tools, batch processing over file sets and a Lua script builder; needs a local Aseprite install and Python 3.13+. - [mcpservers.org listing](https://mcpservers.org/en/servers/ext-sakamoro/AsepriteMCP); [Glama listing](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/ext-sakamoro/AsepriteMCP)
- Claude Code skills marketplaces list an "add-assets" skill that swaps placeholder shapes in browser games for pixel-art sprites built entirely in code (no image files), a "game asset generation" skill that calls an external image API for sprites and animation sheets, and skills covering sprite-sheet structure, tilesets and palette constraints. - [add-assets skill](https://claudemarketplaces.com/skills/opusgamelabs/game-creator/add-assets); [game-asset-generation skill](https://claudemarketplaces.com/skills/eachlabs/skills/game-asset-generation); [pixel-art-sprites skill](https://openskillindex.com/skills/absolutelyskilled-absolutelyskilled-pixel-art-sprites)
- Image-generator sprite-sheet guides (Nano Banana / Nano Banana Pro): keep an approved reference image beside every prompt, name a few distinctive features to hold constant, ask for frames on one baseline with constant lighting, start with a simple side-view walk cycle, treat output as a rough animation to check in playback before polishing, export PNG to keep transparency, check the grid is evenly spaced. - [Geek Vibes Nation](https://geekvibesnation.com/nano-banana-pro-sprite-sheets-make-the-walk-cycle-work/); [Rosebud guide](https://rosebud.ai/blog/how-to-create-a-sprite-sheet-with-ai-using-google-gemini-and-nano-banana-easy-guide)
- Tools let users drag grid dividers to fix AI-generated frame boundaries without regenerating; one tool offers GPT Image 2 as an alternative backend and renders animations as 2x2 grids to keep character identity; ComfyUI templates exist for single-image-to-sprite-sheet and style transfer with alpha-preserving save nodes. - [sprite sheet creator](https://openapps.pro/apps/sprite-sheet-creator); [ComfyUI sprite sheet workflow](https://comfy.org/workflows/use-cases/sprite-sheet-generator/); [ComfyUI NB2 template](https://www.comfy.org/workflows/template_purz_nb2_single_image_sprite_sheet-065f4992f6e8)
- Anecdote: diffusion models gave good single sprites but broken tilesets, while LLM-written HTML pixel art gave usable tilesets. - [search summary, 2026 developer post](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/48998404) (anecdotal)
- Shader tooling: GLSL is low-resource for models (31% failure for best open models on ShaderMatch), so shader work needs a compile-and-render check in the loop. - [ICSE 2025 LLM4Code](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2025/llm4code-2025-papers/13/Evaluating-Language-Models-for-Computer-Graphics-Code-Completion)

### Inferences
- For PILLAGERS the useful pieces are the tool-builder ones that keep the single-file, no-external-library rule: a palette file, a sprite-sheet baker, a "contact sheet" renderer for critique, and numeric linters. An Aseprite MCP is only worth it if the owner wants hand-editable source files; it adds a dependency the project does not have today.
- Mixing an image generator in would conflict with "code-drawn" sprites and add consistency problems the sources do not show solved; if tried, use it only to make reference or mood images for the owner to pick from, not shipped sprites.

### Gaps
- No talks or postmortems from named studios on LLM-built art validators were found in this pass.

## 4. Automated 2D art checks an agent can run, and perceptual metrics with their limits

### Takeaway
Exact checks (palette membership, integer grid, per-frame distinctness, outline and silhouette rules) are cheap, deterministic and catch real defects, and should gate before any vision critique. Perceptual metrics help as trend signals but each has known blind spots: pixel metrics (MSE, SSIM) punish one-pixel shifts and push toward blur; LPIPS is low-level and blind to pose and layout; DreamSim and DINO are tolerant of small shifts and were not trained on pixel art; optimising any single metric invites reward hacking.

### Cited Findings
- Numeric locks used in a shipped 2026 Claude-built pixel pipeline: palette membership, distinct indices per frame, frame-to-frame difference (caught a near-static walk cycle), no baked-but-unused layers, plus a colour ramp census as a ~100-token text summary. - [jucelinux, DEV Community](https://dev.to/jucelinux/i-taught-claude-opus-5-to-draw-with-no-image-model-just-code-then-i-asked-for-a-self-portrait-2894)
- Same source, limits of locks: sub-pixel features (eyes) vanished with every lock green, so locks need explicit "feature still present at native size" checks, and a human look is still needed. - [jucelinux, DEV Community](https://dev.to/jucelinux/i-taught-claude-opus-5-to-draw-with-no-image-model-just-code-then-i-asked-for-a-self-portrait-2894)
- Translucency inside a locked palette: a blend table in index space simulates transparency without adding colours. - [jucelinux, DEV Community](https://dev.to/jucelinux/i-taught-claude-opus-5-to-draw-with-no-image-model-just-code-then-i-asked-for-a-self-portrait-2894)
- RLRF used MSE, SSIM, DINO and LPIPS for evaluation and L2, L2-on-Canny-edges, DreamSim and CLIP as rewards; L2-only improved MSE but hurt DINO semantic similarity; edges plus DreamSim converged faster; named reward hacks included shrinking the viewBox, collapsing length and writing text into the image. - [RLRF, arXiv 2505.20793](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20793v2)
- Pixel-level metrics (SSIM, PSNR) have trouble separating minor pixel changes from real corruption; LPIPS, SSIM and PSNR are classed as low-level patch metrics. - [Benchmarking image similarity metrics, arXiv 2506.12563](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12563v1)
- LPIPS is not sensitive to pose, layout, shape or semantic changes. - [CVPR 2026 supplement, Jeong et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Jeong_Training-free_Perceptually_Consistent_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf)
- In a Gaussian-splatting study, PSNR and SSIM fell sharply with a few pixels of offset while DreamSim stayed almost flat. - [MS-GS, arXiv 2509.15548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.15548)
- DreamSim was tuned on human judgments of diffusion-generated image triplets; higher-resolution variants are listed as future work. - [DreamSim GitHub](https://github.com/ssnl/dreamsim); [Voxel51 explainer](https://voxel51.com/blog/teaching-androids-to-dream-of-sheep)
- Practitioners found optimising drawings toward SSIM/RMSE made them worse and suggested DINOv2 embedding similarity instead. - [HN thread 48998404](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/48998404)
- VLM judges can be swayed by brightness, gamma and padding changes, so any VLM score needs normalised presentation. - [arXiv 2505.15249](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15249v2)

### Inferences
Checks that fit PILLAGERS' sprite pipeline (all exact, all runnable in headless Chrome on the art-pixel buffer before upscaling):
- Palette conformance: every opaque pixel is in the locked palette (zero tolerance); count colours per sprite against a cap.
- Grid alignment: art drawn at native resolution and scaled by an integer factor with smoothing off; check that every NxN block in the upscaled image is one colour (catches accidental anti-aliasing or fractional offsets).
- Outline continuity: the outer silhouette edge is fully covered by outline-colour pixels (flood-fill the transparent background, then check every opaque pixel touching it); flag gaps and doubled (2-pixel) outlines; flag orphan single pixels.
- Silhouette readability at play size: render the sprite as a solid black mask at actual on-screen size and compare masks across the roster (pairwise mask IoU); too-similar silhouettes between classes (P1 vs P2, enemy kinds) are a readability failure.
- Contrast: luminance contrast between sprite and the actual floor tiles it stands on, measured at play size; also check P1 amber and P2 cyan stay distinguishable under the fog and lighting pass, not only on a flat background.
- Feature survival: named features (eyes, gun muzzle, backpack) each keep at least one pixel of their colour at native size in every facing and frame.
- Animation sanity: each frame differs from its neighbours by more than a floor and less than a ceiling; the foot baseline does not drift.
- Use SSIM or DINO/DreamSim against the reference only as a trend line across rounds and as a "did this round make it worse" revert trigger, never as the sole target; combine a structural signal with a semantic one, as RLRF did.

### Gaps
- No source evaluated SSIM, LPIPS, DreamSim, CLIP or DINO on pixel art or nearest-neighbour-scaled sprites specifically.
- No published standard set of pixel-art lint rules (outline, banding, pillow shading, jaggies) for automated use was found; the rules above are inferences from pixel-art practice and the case study.

## 5. Human art direction still required, and how a non-artist owner can steer

### Takeaway
Every credible 2026 source keeps a human as the final, scarce judge: agents reach a cheap competent floor, but taste calls, recurring "looks wrong" bugs and style choices came from the human looking at the screen. The effective owner moves are binary or pick-one-of-N choices, a locked reference and palette, and approval gates, not written critique.

### Cited Findings
- In the COMPASSO case study the human was the top of the oracle ladder and "the scarce input"; the decisive style moment was Claude asking one binary question (real pixel art or smooth SNES-style rendering) and the author picking; an append-only decision log of failures and successes was kept; recommendation: let constraints (locked palette, integer positions, baked facings) replace taste decisions, and reserve visual review for what automated checks miss. - [jucelinux, DEV Community](https://dev.to/jucelinux/i-taught-claude-opus-5-to-draw-with-no-image-model-just-code-then-i-asked-for-a-self-portrait-2894)
- The same project's model never found a rotation bug that recurred four times; the human found it by looking at the screen. - [jucelinux, DEV Community](https://dev.to/jucelinux/i-taught-claude-opus-5-to-draw-with-no-image-model-just-code-then-i-asked-for-a-self-portrait-2894)
- Anthropic recommends having the agent show evidence (screenshots, outputs) rather than assert success, because reviewing evidence is faster for the human. - [Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)
- Image-generator sprite guides centre an "approved reference image" kept beside every prompt as the consistency anchor. - [Geek Vibes Nation](https://geekvibesnation.com/nano-banana-pro-sprite-sheets-make-the-walk-cycle-work/)
- Practitioner note: models draw "symbols" (icon eyes and heads) the way beginners do; drawing from value and shape gives better results, which is a direction a human has to set. - [HN thread 48998404](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/48998404)

### Inferences
- For a non-artist owner: (a) pick one reference image per asset class and a locked palette, and treat both as law; (b) choose between 2 to 4 side-by-side variants at real play size, on the real floor, rather than describing what is wrong; (c) approve at gates (silhouette and palette first, then shading, then animation) so detail work is not wasted on a rejected silhouette; (d) keep a short style sheet of his picks with the reason in his words, which the critic agent then scores against.
- The separate critic agent is useful for catching regressions and obvious mismatch, but its approval should never replace his pick; vision judges are lenient and biased (section 2).
- The PILLAGERS menu and art survey pages (pick-one-of-N picture questions) already match the pattern the sources recommend.

### Gaps
- No studio talk or postmortem was found describing art-direction gates for AI-coded procedural sprites specifically; the steering advice above is inference from the case study, vendor guidance and general practice.
- No source measured how well a non-artist's picks hold up versus a trained art director's in AI-assisted pipelines.
