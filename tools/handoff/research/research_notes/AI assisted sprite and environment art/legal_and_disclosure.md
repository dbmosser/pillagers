# Legal, licensing, store-policy and player-reception rules for AI-assisted game art (itch.io release Thanksgiving 2026, possible later Steam release)

Checked 2026-10-20. This is research, not legal advice. Where a primary page could not be fetched (OpenAI and Midjourney terms returned HTTP 403), that is said in the bullet and in Gaps.

## 1. itch.io: current AI policy, disclosure fields, tags, visibility

### Takeaway
Since 20 Nov 2024 every itch.io project edit page has a "Generative AI Disclosure" field. It is required for asset pages and optional (but encouraged) for games. Answering yes adds an "AI Generated" tag, optional sub-tags (Graphics, Sound, Text & Dialog, Code), and a listing on the "AI Assisted" browse page. Answering no adds "No AI". Leaving it blank (allowed for games) gives neither tag. Untagged AI asset pages lose browse indexing, and lying in the tags can make any page ineligible for discovery. Staff guidance says code written by an LLM counts as AI content, while using ChatGPT only to inform yourself does not.

### Cited Findings
- Announcement "Generative AI Disclosure tagging" by leafo (itch.io Admin), topic created 20 Nov 2024: "This field is now _required_ for all asset creators on itch.io." and "This field asks if your project contains the results of generative AI." and "If you select "yes," then your page will automatically receive the AI Generated tag." The four content types are Graphics, Sound, Text & Dialog and Code, each with its own sub-tag; "no" gives the No AI tag. ([itch.io announcement](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging))
- Same post on enforcement for assets: "Assets comprised of generative AI (even if modified afterwards) that are not tagged will no longer be eligible for indexing on our browse pages." A grace period was promised, after which "we'll likely use user reports" for pages not addressed. Creators with public asset pages got "a blocking dialog instructing you to classify your pages" plus a bulk tagging tool. ([itch.io announcement](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging); [80.lv, 21 Nov 2024](https://80.lv/articles/asset-creators-on-itch-io-now-have-to-disclose-the-use-of-generative-ai))
- Games versus assets, admin reply in the same thread: "It is only mandatory for asset creators, but we encourage everyone to fill it out at their convenience." ([itch.io announcement thread](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging)). Note: early press said the rule covered "all games and asset packs" ([GamesMarket, 21 Nov 2024](https://www.gamesmarket.global/itchio-now-requires-gen-ai-content-to-be-tagged-in-all-games-and-asset-packs-711e824843d410eab51fcd9436836294/)); that is contradicted by the admin reply and by [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/asset-creators-on-itch-io-now-have-to-disclose-the-use-of-generative-ai), which says the announcement refers only to assets.
- Admin replies on scope (same thread): for code, the test is "If your project contains code synthesized by a Large Language Model" with "Use your best judgment"; "If the output of Gen AI is something you put into your project, then tag it."; a dependency needs tagging only if chosen because of its generative AI use, otherwise "I wouldn't really worry about it."; asking ChatGPT something "solely to inform yourself" does not make the project contain generative AI content. ([itch.io announcement thread](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging))
- Admin replies on the No AI tag and filtering: "The `no-ai` tag is only applied to pages where the creator explicitly marked their page as not containing generative AI." and "Negative filtering is coming soon." (Nov 2024). On penalties: itch.io relies on creator tags, handles misuse through reports, "there's no top-level filtering or penalizing going on with any pages", but abusing tags to lie would make a page ineligible for indexing on discovery pages. ([itch.io announcement thread](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging))
- Current Content creator quality guidelines (page footer "Copyright 2026 itch corp", no revision date), heading "Accurately tag your use of generative AI": "We ask that you accurately tag your project if it contains materials produced by generative AI"; generative AI "includes large language models like ChatGPT and image generation models like DALL-E, Midjourney"; "Projects using self-contained algorithms without external large datasets don't require the use of generative AI tags", for example "traditional game AI like NPC pathfinding, enemy behavior patterns, procedural level generation"; "Projects that indicate that they use generative AI will appear on the AI Assisted browse page." Boxed note: itch.io is "strictly enforcing disclosure for all game asset pages due to legal ambiguity around rights" and "Failure to tag your asset page may result in delisting." ([itch.io quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines))
- Same guidelines, heading "Avoid uploading excessive amounts of automatically generated or AI-generated content": discouraged are "Games or projects that are predominantly created by algorithms or AI with minimal human intervention", "Multiple iterations of essentially the same content that are generated by AI or algorithms" and "Content that is randomly generated with no or minimal curated design"; "If your project involves automatic or AI generation, make sure it's clearly stated in your project description". ([itch.io quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines))
- Forum users report the disclosure answers act as browse filters and do not show in the project page info box; the admin did not confirm a change. User complaint threads about AI visibility exist. ([itch.io announcement thread](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging); [itch.io forum, "dismayed" thread](https://itch.io/t/4527500/im-dismayed-that-itchio-decided-to-hide-which-gamesassets-are-ai-and-which-arent))
- itch.io does not verify the answers; it is up to the creator. ([itch.io forum post](https://itch.io/post/14981185), user post, not staff)

### Inferences
- For PILLAGERS (a game, not an asset pack) the field is optional. If he answers, the honest answer under staff guidance is "Yes" with the Code sub-tag, because much of the shipped code was written by Claude. Answering "No AI" would conflict with the admin's code test and the "lying" warning, so it is the one answer to avoid.
- Code-drawn art: the canvas drawing code runs no model and uses no external dataset at runtime, which matches the guideline's exemption for "self-contained algorithms". The code itself was LLM-written, so Code applies; Graphics arguably does not, unless AI image-generator pixels (even repainted) ship. This line is a judgment call; staff said "use your best judgment".
- An AI reference picture used only for style direction and never shipped fits the "solely to inform yourself" reading, so no Graphics tag. Sprites traced from or painted over AI output fall under "(even if modified afterwards)" and would need the Graphics tag.
- Tagging Yes puts the game on the "AI Assisted" browse page and in front of any "AI Generated" filter. No data was found on how much traffic that costs. Leaving the field blank is allowed for a game but forfeits the "No AI" badge and may read as hiding if players later learn of AI use.
- The private friends-and-family link (draft or restricted page) is not affected by browse indexing.
- The guideline "make sure it's clearly stated in your project description" means a short written AI note on the page is the expected practice even beyond the tag.

### Gaps
- No itch.io statement found on whether the AI Generated tag changes search ranking beyond the AI Assisted page and filters, and no traffic data.
- Not confirmed whether the promised negative filtering ("exclude AI") shipped, or whether the asset grace period has formally ended.
- No 2025 or 2026 itch.io policy revision found; the quality guidelines page carries no revision date.

## 2. Steam: AI content disclosure survey, the January 2026 rewrite, what must be disclosed

### Takeaway
Steam's Content Survey still requires disclosure of AI-made content that ships with the game and is consumed by players (Pre-Generated), and a guardrail description for content AI makes while the game runs (Live-Generated). A mid-January 2026 rewrite (spotted 16-17 Jan 2026) says AI tools used for "efficiency gains", such as code helpers, are not the focus, so AI coding help no longer needs disclosure. Disclosures show on the store page under "AI Generated Content Disclosure". Valve reviews AI output like any other content, and the developer promises it is not infringing.

### Cited Findings
- Current Steamworks Content Survey text: "The third part of the survey deals with generative artificial intelligence in your development or product." "We are aware that many modern game development environments have AI powered tools built into them. Efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus of this section. Instead, it is concerned with the use of AI in creating content that ships with your game, and is consumed by players. This includes content such as artwork, sound, narrative, localization, etc." "If your game used AI services during development or incorporates AI services as a part of the product, this section will require you to describe that implementation in detail". ([Steamworks Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey))
- Pre-Generated: "Any kind of content that ships with your game and is consumed by players that is created with the help of AI tools"; "you promise Valve that your game will not include illegal or infringing content, and that your game will be consistent with your marketing materials"; "we will evaluate the output of AI generated content in your game the same way we evaluate all non-AI content". ([Steamworks Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey))
- Live-Generated: "Any kind of content created with the help of AI tools while the game is running." It follows the same rules plus "you'll need to tell us what kind of guardrails you're putting on your AI to ensure it's not generating illegal content." Valve does not "want to ship Live-Generated AI Adult Only Sexual Content at this time." ([Steamworks Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey))
- The rewrite was reported 17 Jan 2026 and spotted by Simon Carless (GameDiscoverCo). "AI-powered tools" such as code helpers do not need disclosure. Still disclosed: AI used to generate content for the game, its store page, or marketing materials; and content generated during gameplay. Disclosures appear on the store page in a section called "AI Generated Content Disclosure". ([VGC, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use); [Game*Spark, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2026/01/17/161698.html) calls it a rewrite, not a deletion; [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/steam-makes-major-change-to-ai-rules-for-games-3306274))
- No formal Valve blog post on the rewrite was found; it was a quiet edit to the documentation. ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use))
- Disclosure share (Sulka Haro census, via Cinevva, 53,597 Steam releases mid-2023 to mid-2026): about 7% at launch of the field in early 2024; 10.9% of 2024 releases, 19.9% of 2025 releases, 30.8% of 2026 releases so far. AI-disclosed games were about a third of new releases but only 10 to 27% of estimated sales in the latest period. Among AI-flagged games that failed to find an audience, 72% used AI for visuals; successful AI-flagged games used AI for voice (24% vs 8%) and localization (18% vs 6%) more than failures did. The flag is binary and does not show how much AI was used. ([Cinevva, 20 Jul 2026](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-07-20-steam-ai-disclosure-study); I did not read Haro's original)
- Other estimates of the disclosure share are lower (about one in five); methodology differs. ([Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/steam-ai-disclosure-2026/))
- Epic's Tim Sweeney has criticised the label as a "Scarlet Letter of AI". ([Papogames](https://papogames.com/en/blog/steam-ai-disclosure-2026), secondary)

### Inferences
- For a later Steam port: any AI image-generator output that ships (sprites, tiles, UI art) or appears on the store page (capsule, screenshots of that art, trailer) must be disclosed as Pre-Generated, even if repainted ("created with the help of AI tools").
- Claude-written code is now outside the survey's focus (an "efficiency" development tool), so it does not need disclosure on Steam.
- Code-drawn procedural art sits in the gap: the art a player sees is drawn by code that an AI wrote. Read literally, it is "content ... created with the help of AI tools". A short, specific Pre-Generated note (for example "visuals are drawn by game code written with an ai coding assistant; no ai image generator output ships") costs little and removes the risk of a later "hidden AI" accusation. This is a judgment call, not settled policy.
- PILLAGERS has no Live-Generated AI, so the guardrail section would not apply.
- AI used only for a private style reference that never ships is arguably out of scope (not "content that ships"), but players treated even concept-stage AI as news in the Larian case (section 6).

### Gaps
- The exact pre-January 2026 survey wording (the before/after images were posted by GameDiscoverCo but not retrieved).
- No evidence either way that the Steam disclosure lowers algorithmic visibility; Valve says it reviews AI output the same as other content.
- Whether Valve treats AI-written code that draws art as "AI generated content" has no official answer.

## 3. Copyright: US Copyright Office position, Thaler, what stays protectable

### Takeaway
US copyright needs a human author. Thaler v. Perlmutter (D.C. Cir., 18 Mar 2025) held that, and the Supreme Court denied review on 2 Mar 2026. The Copyright Office's Part 2 report (29 Jan 2025) says prompts alone are not enough. What is protectable: perceptible human expression used as input, creative human selection and arrangement, and human modifications of AI output; the AI-generated material itself stays unprotected. The Office registered "A Single Piece of American Cheese" (30 Jan 2025) on selection, arrangement and coordination after about 35 inpainting edits, backed by a timelapse video.

### Cited Findings
- Report parts: Part 1 Digital Replicas, 31 Jul 2024; Part 2 Copyrightability, 29 Jan 2025; Part 3 Generative AI Training, pre-publication version 9 May 2025. The registration guidance page still links the 16 Mar 2023 Federal Register policy statement; no 2025-2026 registration guidance update is listed. ([US Copyright Office, Copyright and AI](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/))
- Part 2 on prompts: "a human cannot control the way an idea is expressed and executed through prompting"; repeated prompt revision is "re-rolling the dice"; the Office left an opening: "a different conclusion may be called for" if future tools let prompts closely control output. "the issue is the degree of human control, rather than the predictability of the outcome." ([Copyright Alliance summary of Part 2](https://copyrightalliance.org/ai-report-part-2-copyrightability/); [IPWatchdog, 29 Jan 2025](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/))
- Part 2 on what is protectable: expressive inputs (Rose Enigma example): "copyright in this kind of AI-generated output would cover the perceptible human expression"; modifications: copyright protects "original (minimally creative) human expression that modifies or arranges AI-generated output"; selection and arrangement (Zarya of the Dawn): registration limited to "the human authored original selection and arrangement of AI-generated images" plus human text. In Rose Enigma the applicant disclaimed the AI-generated elements. ([Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/ai-report-part-2-copyrightability/))
- Part 2 found existing law sufficient, no legislative change and no sui generis right for AI output; it drew on more than 10,000 public comments. ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/us-copyright-office-issues-report-on-copyrightability-of-ai-assisted-and-generated-works); [Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/ai-report-part-2-copyrightability/))
- Thaler v. Perlmutter: D.C. Circuit decision 18 Mar 2025 (130 F.4th 1039) held copyright requires human authorship; Supreme Court denied certiorari 2 Mar 2026 (No. 25-449) without comment; the government urged denial. A cert denial is not a merits ruling. ([Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/ip-updates/supreme-court-declines-to-hear-thaler-v-perlmutter-leaving-human-authorship-requirement-intact.html); [SCOTUSblog docket](https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/thaler-v-perlmutter/); [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/supreme-court-denies-certiorari-in-thaler-v-perlmutter-ai-cannot-be-an-author-under-the-copyright-act))
- Note on Thaler's narrowness: Thaler listed the AI itself as sole author, so the case decides only that a machine cannot be the author; AI-assisted works are governed by the Office's Part 2 analysis. ([Art at Law](https://artatlaw.substack.com/p/thaler-v-perlmutter-much-ado-about), commentary; title and framing seen in search, page not fully read)
- Invoke "A Single Piece of American Cheese": first refused Aug 2024; re-submitted with a timelapse video and an explanation of the creator's process; registered 30 Jan 2025 for "a sufficient amount of human original authorship in the selection, arrangement, and coordination of the AI-generated material"; about 35 inpainting edits; the individual AI-generated elements are not protected. Commentators question how far this stretches the report's test, and no court has ruled on such a work. ([ChatGPT Is Eating the World](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2025/02/11/ai-generated-image-received-copyright-registration-based-on-selection-coordination-and-arrangement-yes-in-the-united-states-how/); [IP Law Group](https://www.iplawgroup.com/a-landmark-ruling-u-s-copyright-office-grants-protection-to-ai-assisted-artwork/); [IPKat](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2025/02/a-single-piece-of-us-copyright-are-ai.html))
- Contract ownership is not copyright: Anthropic's consumer terms assign "all of our right, title, and interest, if any, in Outputs", which passes whatever rights exist but does not create them. ([terms.law on Claude outputs](https://www.terms.law/2024/08/24/who-owns-claudes-outputs-and-how-can-they-be-used/))

### Inferences
- In a game like PILLAGERS, the parts most clearly protectable are human-made: hand-drawn or substantially hand-painted sprites, human-written text, the human selection and arrangement of assets into maps, menus and screens, and any code a human wrote or materially rewrote. Raw image-generator output, and code an LLM wrote with only prompting from him, are likely unprotected on their own, so a copycat could lift those individual pieces.
- "Sufficient human authorship" in practice: (1) start from your own drawing or sketch so it is perceptible in the result; (2) make many targeted, creative edits (inpainting, repainting, recomposing) rather than re-rolling prompts; (3) select and arrange outputs into a composition; (4) keep evidence (source files with layers, before/after images, timelapse, prompts, git history). The Invoke case shows the Office weighs that evidence.
- For a pay-what-you-want itch release the practical stakes of weaker copyright are low (copycat reuse of individual sprites); the higher stakes are store policy and player trust.
- If he ever registers the game's copyright, the 2023 guidance (from prior knowledge, not re-fetched in this session) asks applicants to disclose AI-generated material that is more than de minimis and disclaim it, describing the human contribution. Flag for a lawyer if registration is planned.

### Gaps
- No court ruling yet on the copyrightability of AI-assisted (as opposed to AI-authored) images or of LLM-written code.
- No published threshold for how much paintover is "enough"; it stays case by case.
- Part 3 final version and any 2026 Compendium update were not found.

## 4. Tool terms: ownership, commercial use, indemnities, training-data claims

### Takeaway
Every tool checked says, in some form, that the user owns outputs "to the extent permitted by law" and may use them commercially, but none of the image tools checked offers an indemnity to a small, consumer or self-serve user; the infringement risk stays with the developer. Revenue thresholds ($1M a year at Midjourney and Stability) are far above a PWYW itch game. Free tiers can make images public (Midjourney lower plans, Leonardo free). Retro Diffusion is the only tool found that claims a consented training set.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI (GPT-image in ChatGPT and API): terms revisions of Oct and Dec 2024 say "as between you and OpenAI, and to the extent permitted by applicable law, you (a) retain your ownership rights in Input and (b) own the Output"; output "may not be unique" and others may receive similar output; the user is responsible for Content. The current terms page returned 403, so these may be superseded. ([OpenAI terms revision 2024-12-11](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/revisions/2024-12-11/))
- OpenAI Copyright Shield: API business terms (Jan 2024 version quoted by a user) indemnify against third-party claims that use or distribution of Output infringes IP, excluding cases where the customer "knew or should have known the Output was infringing or likely to infringe". Whether it covers consumer ChatGPT plans is unconfirmed (one forum source says business/API only). ([OpenAI community thread](https://community.openai.com/t/terms-of-use-for-copyright-shield/554183))
- OpenAI provenance: images from ChatGPT, Codex and the API carry C2PA Content Credentials and a SynthID watermark; openai.com/verify checks for either. Metadata can be stripped, SynthID is meant to survive that. ([OpenAI Help, C2PA in ChatGPT images](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8912793-c2pa-in-chatgpt-images); [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/openai-synthid-c2pa-verification-portal))
- OpenAI style rule (stated March 2025 at the 4o image launch): OpenAI will "continue to prevent generations in the style of individual living artists, but we do permit broader studio styles". The rule dates back to the DALL-E 3 system card (2023). ([AI Magazine](https://aimagazine.com/articles/how-openais-new-image-model-sparked-the-studio-ghibli-trend); [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/ai/ai-art/the-openai-studio-ghibli-controversy-will-be-test-for-art-copyright-in-the-ai-era))
- Midjourney: official plans page: "You must purchase the Pro or Mega plan if you are a company making more than $1,000,000 USD in gross revenue per year." ([Midjourney plans](https://docs.midjourney.com/docs/plans)). Secondary readings of the ToS: subscribers own assets "to the fullest extent" permitted by law; ownership persists after cancelling; Midjourney's license to your images survives; no indemnity for users; Stealth (private) mode only on Pro ($60/mo) and Mega ($120/mo), so lower-plan images are public; free-trial images non-commercial. The ToS page itself returned 403. ([terms.law Midjourney 2026 guide](https://terms.law/2026/01/15/midjourney-commercial-use-rights-complete-2026-guide/))
- PixelLab: Terms of Service last updated 2025-11-23. Section 1.3: you own the copyrights to your creations and may use them commercially or non-commercially without asking permission. Section 3.3: you keep ownership and may use, modify and distribute outputs for any purpose except training other models without PixelLab's permission; full legal responsibility for compliance and non-infringement is on you. Section 1.2 bars training your own models on outputs without written permission. Section 2.1 allows PixelLab to change limits, features and pricing. ([PixelLab ToS](https://www.pixellab.ai/termsofservice))
- Retro Diffusion: says its pixel model was "trained on licensed assets from Astropulse and other pixel artists with their consent" (self-published claim, not independently verified); sold as credits on the web or a one-time desktop/Aseprite purchase; the website and extension use different models. Output rights: only a community comment says users own generated images; no ToS found. ([Retro Diffusion on itch.io](https://astropulse.itch.io/retrodiffusion); [comments](https://astropulse.itch.io/retrodiffusion/comments))
- Scenario: Terms and Conditions dated 30 Apr 2025 govern website, models, tools and APIs; privacy policy: "Self-serve: you own your work; we may use your content and anonymized insights to improve our Services and AI"; enterprise customers get an MSA under which content is not used for training without written consent; commercial rights depend on the underlying model (for example Ideogram models: users hold rights and may use commercially); Scenario says compliance is the user's responsibility. No indemnity clause found. ([Scenario terms](https://www.scenario.com/terms-and-conditions); [Scenario privacy](https://scenario.com/privacy); [Scenario help, model licenses](https://help.scenario.com/en/articles/commercial-use-licenses-of-scenario-platform-models))
- Leonardo.ai (help article dated 25 Aug 2026): users "can indeed use your images for commercial purposes!" on free and paid plans; "when generating privately using a paid subscription, users retain full ownership, copyright, and all other intellectual property rights to their images"; free-plan generations are publicly viewable for others to copy and remix; for public images "Leonardo.Ai and its successors retain worldwide perpetual rights". ([Leonardo help, commercial usage](https://intercom.help/leonardo-ai/en/articles/8044018-commercial-usage))
- Stability AI Community License (last updated 5 Jul 2024): free commercial use with registration until you and affiliates "generate more than USD $1,000,000 in annual revenue", then "You must request a license from Stability AI"; "You own any outputs generated from the Models or Derivative Works to the extent permitted by applicable law"; distributing the Materials or Derivative Works needs a Notice file and "Powered by Stability AI" display. ([Stability AI Community License](https://stability.ai/community-license-agreement))
- Anthropic (Claude, relevant to code-drawn art): consumer terms assign Anthropic's rights in Outputs "if any"; commercial terms state the customer owns outputs; since 1 Jan 2024 Anthropic indemnifies commercial/API customers against copyright claims over "their authorized use of our services or their outputs", excluding modifications by the customer, combination with non-Anthropic content, and violations of the terms or law; the indemnity does not extend the same way to consumer plans. ([terms.law](https://www.terms.law/2024/08/24/who-owns-claudes-outputs-and-how-can-they-be-used/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/392234/anthropic-has-an-ai-copyright-indemnity-clause.html); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/anthropic-joins-party-offers-copyright-shield-enterprise-ai-customers))

### Inferences
- "You own the output" is a contract promise between user and vendor; it cannot create copyright the law does not grant (section 3) and does not protect against a third party's claim.
- With no consumer-plan indemnity anywhere in this list, the developer carries the infringement risk for any image-generator output he ships, which is also what Valve's survey makes him promise.
- Use private modes (Midjourney Pro/Mega Stealth, Leonardo paid private) if the look should not be public before release; on Leonardo free, public images carry a perpetual license to Leonardo and remix access for others.
- Images made in ChatGPT or the OpenAI API carry C2PA and SynthID, so their origin can be detected even after export. Disclosure should assume AI origin can be discovered.
- PixelLab's no-training clause matters only if he later wants to fine-tune a model on its outputs.
- For training-data reputational risk, Retro Diffusion's consent claim is the strongest among those checked, but it is unverified.

### Gaps
- Current verbatim OpenAI terms (403) and whether Copyright Shield applies to ChatGPT Plus users.
- Midjourney ToS verbatim (403).
- Retro Diffusion's written terms for output ownership.
- Scenario's indemnity terms (enterprise MSA not public).
- Adobe Firefly and other "commercially safe" indemnified tools were not researched.

## 5. Infringing output risk and the image-model lawsuits (2025-2026 status)

### Takeaway
The training lawsuits target model makers, not game developers, and none had reached a final judgment by 20 Oct 2026. The practical risk for a small game is shipping a specific output that reproduces a recognisable character, a logo or watermark, or a living artist's distinctive work. Andersen v. Stability AI is set for a jury trial on 1 Jun 2027. Getty lost almost all of its UK case in Nov 2025 and was granted permission to appeal in Dec 2025. The Disney, Universal and Warner Bros. v. Midjourney case is in motion practice.

### Cited Findings
- Andersen v. Stability AI (N.D. Cal. No. 3:23-cv-00201, Judge Orrick; defendants Stability AI, Midjourney, DeviantArt, Runway): direct and induced copyright infringement and Lanham Act claims survived; DMCA 1202(a)/(b) claims were dismissed with prejudice in Aug 2024; Third Amended Complaint filed 27 Feb 2026. ([AI Lawsuit Tracker](https://ailawsuittracker.com/cases/andersen-v-stability-ai-ltd-3-23-cv-00201/); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/california-federal-court-trims-lawsuit-against-stability-ai-midjourney-and-deviantart-in-generative-ai-artwork-case))
- Andersen schedule: a 2 Feb 2026 order moved the jury trial from 5 Apr 2027 to 1 Jun 2027 (pretrial conference 26 Apr 2027) and the summary judgment hearing from 4 Nov 2026 to 17 Feb 2027; expert discovery was set to close 18 Oct 2026. ([ChatGPT Is Eating the World, 3 Feb 2026](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/02/03/sarah-andersens-copyright-lawsuit-the-1st-filed-v-ai-companies-gets-pushed-back-again-at-her-request/); [CourtListener docket document](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.407208/gov.uscourts.cand.407208.409.0.pdf)). Contradicted by a law-firm blog claiming a jury trial began 8 Sep 2026 ([Sigma Law Group](https://sigmalawgroup.com/blog/2026-09-06-andersen-stability-ai-jury-trial/)); no docket reporting supports that, so treat it as unreliable.
- A 19 May 2026 order directed Midjourney to answer further discovery questions; an Aug 2026 letter brief alleged Stability failed to preserve evidence. ([AI Lawsuit Tracker](https://ailawsuittracker.com/cases/andersen-v-stability-ai-ltd-3-23-cv-00201/))
- Getty Images v. Stability AI (UK High Court, judgment Nov 2025): Getty dropped its primary training and output copyright claims during the June 2025 trial ([WIPR](https://www.worldipreview.com/artificial-intelligence/getty-drops-primary-copyright-claims-against-stability-ai)); the court rejected the secondary infringement claim: a model can in principle be an "article", but Stable Diffusion did not embody infringing copies; Getty won "in part" on trade marks for Getty watermarks in user outputs, findings "both historic and extremely limited in scope". ([CMS case tracker](https://cms.law/en/aut/publication/artificial-intelligence-and-copyright-case-tracker/united-kingdom-getty-images-us-inc-ors-v-stability-ai-ltd3); [judiciary.uk appendix, Nov 2025](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Getty-v-Stability-AI-Appendix-B.pdf))
- Getty appeal: permission to appeal the secondary infringement point granted 17 Dec 2025; the core question is whether a model must store a copy of a work to be an "infringing copy". Sources conflict on wording of who appealed, but [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102lydc/getty-images-v-stability-ai-getty-granted-permission-to-appeal) and [Law360](https://www.law360.com/commercial-litigation-uk/articles/2422431/getty-wins-shot-to-revive-stability-ai-copyright-case) say Getty. No Court of Appeal judgment found.
- Disney and Universal v. Midjourney: filed June 2025 in C.D. Cal. (2:25-cv-05275), citing characters such as Darth Vader and Elsa, after cease-and-desist letters; Warner Bros. Discovery sued in Sep 2025 and the cases were combined in Nov 2025. Midjourney moved for judgment on the copyright claims; the studios opposed on 11 Aug 2026 and Midjourney replied on 25 Aug 2026; no ruling found. ([Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/disney-universal-midjourney-1236262563/); [Graphic Policy, 28 Mar 2026](https://graphicpolicy.com/2026/03/28/midjourney-files-a-motion-to-compel-against-disney-and-warner-bros-in-ai-fight); [Graphic Policy, 11 May 2026](https://graphicpolicy.com/2026/05/11/disney-warner-bros-universal-and-midjourney-discovery-dispute/); [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2517986))
- Valve makes the developer promise "your game will not include illegal or infringing content" for AI content. ([Steamworks Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey))

### Inferences
- Avoid: prompts naming living artists, studios, franchises or characters; any output resembling a known character, logo, brand mark or watermark (the Getty watermark finding shows outputs can carry marks); image-to-image from someone else's art; shipping an output you have not inspected for near-copies.
- Do: prompt for generic style traits (palette, line weight, lighting, camera angle) rather than names; use his own reference picture and sketches as inputs; repaint and recompose; keep a simple asset log (tool, date, prompt, edits) so any claim can be answered.
- Style mimicry of a living artist is not itself clearly illegal under US copyright (style is not protected), but it is the subject of the surviving Lanham Act claims in Andersen and is the fastest route to public backlash. Treat it as off limits.
- None of these cases is likely to make a developer liable for having used a lawfully licensed tool, but a loss for Midjourney or Stability could change tool terms or availability; this is speculative.

### Gaps
- Status of Getty's separate US case against Stability not checked.
- No confirmed Court of Appeal hearing date in Getty, no ruling on Midjourney's motion in the studio case.

## 6. Player and press reception, backlash cases, disclosure practices that earned trust

### Takeaway
In 2025-2026, players react far more to AI in what they see and hear than to AI in tooling. The biggest backlash cases were triggered by visible AI art or by statements that later proved incomplete (Clair Obscur lost its Indie Game Awards; Larian had to promise no generative AI art in Divinity). Vague disclosures were mocked (Arc Raiders). Honest devs also get falsely accused. The trust pattern that holds up is a specific, plain disclosure that matches what players find, with no AI pixels in the visible art.

### Cited Findings
- Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 won Indie Game of the Year and Best Debut at the Indie Game Awards on 18 Dec 2025; two days later the IGA rescinded both under its rule against games developed with generative AI, because placeholder AI textures (newspaper-clipping-like backgrounds) had shipped at launch (patched within days) and Sandfall representatives had confirmed no generative AI was used; awards went to Blue Prince and Sorry We're Closed. ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/clair-obscur-genai-iga-awards-rescinded); [Malay Mail, 21 Dec 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/news/tech-gadgets/2025/12/21/indie-game-awards-rescinds-honours-from-clair-obscur-expedition-33-for-genai-use/202718); [Blue's News](https://www.bluesnews.com/s/292738/iga-rescinds-awards-for-clair-obscur-expedition-33-and-gortyn-code))
- Larian (Divinity, Dec 2025 to Jan 2026): Swen Vincke told Bloomberg the studio used AI "to explore ideas, flesh out PowerPoint presentations, develop concept art and write placeholder text" while no AI content would ship; after backlash he said the studio had 72 artists including 23 concept artists; in a Jan 2026 Reddit AMA he wrote "There is not going to be any generative AI art in Divinity" and said the team would refrain from genAI tools in concept art; the writing director said no text generation touches dialogue or journals. ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/holy-f-guys-were-not-replacing-artists-larian-boss-responds-to-ai-backlash); [Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/news/larian-confirms-that-it-will-no-longer-use-generative-ai-for-divinity-concept-art-and-writing-following-backlash-1615314); [CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/larian-generative-ai-divinity-reddit))
- Arc Raiders (Embark, extraction shooter, late 2025): AI text-to-speech voices drew criticism as flat, and boycott calls circulated on Bluesky; its Steam AI disclosure was described as "calibrated to convey the least amount of meaning possible"; the game was still a commercial hit; Embark later re-recorded some lines with actors and keeps licensed TTS for minor lines such as pings. ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/arc-raiders-ai-voice-controversy-nexon-ceo-every-video-game); [Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/arc-raiders-is-facing-boycotts-for-its-use-of-ai-content); [Top Gear](https://www.topgear.com/car-news/gaming/extraction-shooter-arc-raiders-already-a-hit-its-ai-voice-acting-has-ruffled-some); [ScreenHub](https://www.screenhub.com.au/news/games/arc-raiders-ai-voice-actors-replaced-2695984/))
- Steam Next Fest (Feb 2026): players and devs navigating a flood of generative AI demos; dozens of developers on r/IndieDev and r/gamedev report being wrongly accused of AI use. ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-next-fest-feb-2026-gen-ai-art-demos-2000673280))
- False accusations: the indie RPG Shrine's Legacy was review-bombed with claims it used AI; the developer pushed back. ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/steam-reviews-have-a-serious-ai-problem-that-needs-to-be-fixed/))
- Vapor World: Over The Mind replaced in-engine cutscenes with AI versions, players spotted inconsistent character models, and reviews went mostly negative; the outlet argues players object to AI in the visible parts of games. ([IndieForGames](https://indieforgames.com/steam-ai-art-backlash/), lower-reliability outlet)
- Market signal: AI-flagged games were about a third of 2026 Steam releases but 10 to 27% of estimated sales, and 72% of AI-flagged games that failed to find an audience used AI for visuals. ([Cinevva on the Haro census](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-07-20-steam-ai-disclosure-study))

### Inferences
- The visible art is where AI is least forgiven; AI in code or tooling draws much less fire. This favours his code-drawn plus hand-cleanup route over shipping image-generator sprites.
- The Clair Obscur lesson: a claim of "no AI" must be literally true, including placeholders. Audit for leftover AI placeholder images before release, and check any festival or award rules (some bar any generative AI use in development).
- Vague boilerplate ("AI tools may be used") gets mocked; a specific statement that matches what players find earns more trust. Larian's case shows that even concept-stage AI use can become a story once disclosed by surprise, so state it up front and briefly.
- A sample itch.io page note for the current plan (lower case to match his voice): "ai use: the game code was written with an ai coding assistant (claude), directed and tested by me. every picture in the game is drawn by that code or by hand. no ai image generator output ships in the game; one ai image was used privately as a style reference." If AI-generated sprites do ship after repainting, say so with the tool name: "some sprites and tiles started as ai images (tool) and were repainted by hand."
- Being specific also defends against false accusations, which are now common.

### Gaps

- No itch.io-specific reception data (most coverage is Steam).

## 7. Code written with AI help, and a short EU AI Act note

### Takeaway
Steam no longer asks about AI coding help (Jan 2026). itch.io staff say LLM-written code in a project is AI content (the Code sub-tag), but the disclosure is optional for games. US copyright treats AI-written code like any other AI output: protectable only where human authorship is shown. The EU AI Act transparency duties (Art. 50, applying from 2 Aug 2026) mainly bind AI providers and publishers of realistic deepfakes; for evidently artistic or fictional works the duty is a light, non-intrusive disclosure, so a stylised top-down game is at most lightly touched.

### Cited Findings
- Steam: "Efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus of this section"; code helpers do not require disclosure since the Jan 2026 rewrite. ([Steamworks Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use))
- itch.io: tag Code "If your project contains code synthesized by a Large Language Model"; disclosure mandatory only for asset pages. ([itch.io announcement thread](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging))
- Copyright: Thaler and Part 2 apply to all work types; contract assignment of Claude outputs passes rights "if any". ([Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/ip-updates/supreme-court-declines-to-hear-thaler-v-perlmutter-leaving-human-authorship-requirement-intact.html); [terms.law](https://www.terms.law/2024/08/24/who-owns-claudes-outputs-and-how-can-they-be-used/))
- Anthropic's copyright indemnity covers commercial/API customers' authorized use of outputs, with exclusions for customer modifications and combinations with non-Anthropic content. ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/392234/anthropic-has-an-ai-copyright-indemnity-clause.html))
- EU AI Act Article 50 obligations started 2 Aug 2026. Deployers publishing AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that resembles real people, objects, places or events (deepfakes) must disclose it; for evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional works the duty is limited to disclosing the existence of such content "in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work". Providers of generative systems must mark outputs in machine-readable form. Commission guidelines dated 20 Jul 2026 are non-binding; a voluntary Code of Practice exists. ([AI Act Article 50 guide](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/?p=6131); [McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/technology-and-innovation/one-month-to-go-eu-ai-act-transparency-compliance); [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/guidelines-on-transparency-obligations-under-the-eu-ai-act); [AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-rules-enter-force/))

### Inferences
- For code: no store or legal rule forbids shipping AI-written code. On itch.io, an honest disclosure (if answered) includes the Code sub-tag; on Steam, nothing is needed for code alone.
- EU: PILLAGERS' stylised fantasy art is not a deepfake of real people or places, so the deployer deepfake duty is unlikely to apply; any residual "artistic work" duty is met by the short page note in section 6. Whether a US hobby developer on itch.io is even in scope is uncertain.

### Gaps
- No source found on the copyrightability of LLM-written code specifically, or on open-source license contamination from AI code suggestions (not researched).
- Sources differ on a short extension (to 2 Dec 2026) for providers' machine-readable marking duty; not verified.
- No source addresses how Art. 50 applies to in-game assets in interactive works.
