# PILLAGERS: 100 suggestions (research only, nothing built), 2026-10-14

Order from the owner (2026-10-14): research what the game does not have yet, run it against the top extraction shooters and top-down/2.5D games, adapt it to 2.5D, and make 100 suggestions to go through together.

**How to read this.** Each entry has a bold title, one sentence on what it is in 2.5D, a "Why" naming the game that does it well, a size (S = one small build, M = a few builds, L = a multi-build feature), and a flag. NEW = nothing like it in the game. PARTIAL = the game has a piece of it (named in the entry). Flags come from the git log, the memory notes and a keyword scan of dark_raiders.html, not a full code audit, so treat PARTIAL/NEW as 90% right. Entries are ranked within each group, most impactful first.

**Rules respected (nothing below breaks them):** no voices or spoken sounds of any kind (so no voice chat, no announcer), no hum ambience, no music during raids, no hills or verticality, no second map, no Halloween, no headshots or weak spots on pillagers (machine weak spots already exist and stay machine-only), no gun wear or jamming, no level-up unlocks, no daily login rewards or dailies, no loadout presets, no loot thieves, no aim assist, no emotes, no team damage, no start countdown, no pillager words, no siege count. Keyboard and mouse first. DEMO and CAREER play identically.

**The benchmark in one line.** His pitch is "ARC Raiders with Zero Sievert graphics and Fortnite-level arcadey pick-up-and-play fun". The research says the games he names win on four things the game can still sharpen: pressure that rises and falls instead of only ramping (ARC Raiders, Left 4 Dead), impact you can feel on every shot (Vlambeer, Nuclear Throne), loot that announces itself (Fortnite, Dark and Darker), and a reason to run it again that is not a daily (Hades heat, Hotline Miami scoring, arcade high-score tables).

## 1. CORE LOOP AND RAID FLOW

1. **Raid Director** [M, PARTIAL]
   What: A hidden pacing system watches recent stress (damage taken, enemies alive, seconds since the last fight) and schedules waves, ambushes and breathers so pressure swells and ebbs, spawning from just outside the camera on the side you are facing away from, behind the existing spawn telegraph.
   Why: Left 4 Dead's AI Director; ARC Raiders' quiet-loot-then-spike rhythm inside a 30-minute cap. The game has waves that grow with time, but that is a ramp, not a rhythm.

2. **Raid events: supply flare, EM storm, blackout** [M, PARTIAL]
   What: Once or twice a raid a timed event fires with a map marker and a countdown ring: a flare drops a crate every pillager converges on, a storm shuts one extraction ring and lightning punishes open ground, or a blackout kills the lamps for 60 seconds.
   Why: ARC Raiders Night Raids and Electromagnetic Storm (fewer extraction points, lightning in the open, richer loot); DMZ dynamic operations; Fortnite supply drops. The game has weather, lightning and night, but no scheduled event layer.

3. **Golden-enemy clue hunt** [M, PARTIAL]
   What: Three clue props around the map (a dropped scanner, a trail of gold dust, a chirping beacon) shrink a search circle on the map toward where this raid's golden enemy walks, turning the every-raid golden kill into a small hunt.
   Why: Hunt: Showdown, where clues narrow down a boss and the reward is contested; Fortnite boss NPCs that drop a medallion. The golden enemy exists every raid; the hunt around it does not.

4. **Strongholds** [M, PARTIAL]
   What: One or two fortified compounds per raid are flagged on the map at deploy, each with a locked gate (keycard or breach), two defending waves, a guaranteed purple-or-better cache and a score bonus on clear.
   Why: DMZ strongholds; Hunt boss lairs; Delta Force Hazard Operations bosses and contracts. Vaults, keycards and interiors exist; a defended, advertised objective does not.

5. **Call-in beacons** [L, NEW]
   What: Spend raid score to throw a beacon, then enter a short arrow-key code to call an ammo crate, an airstrike, an auto-turret or a shield dome onto it; strikes never hurt allies.
   Why: Helldivers 1 stratagems (a timed directional input that makes the call itself a skill test, and a powerful payload you must place well); Warzone loadout drops. Top-down is the ideal view for placing area effects.

6. **Run goals** [M, PARTIAL]
   What: Three tiny goals show at deploy (kill a warden, open a vault, extract with a gold item) and each pays a score chip, identical in DEMO and CAREER, so every raid has a plan.
   Why: DMZ contracts that take only minutes; Delta Force Hazard Operations contracts and quests; Zero Sievert quests. CAREER contracts exist; DEMO has none.

7. **Creeping burn front** [M, PARTIAL]
   What: In the last two minutes flame advances visibly from the map edges inward, so the clock becomes a place you can see, and the site burns at zero as it already does.
   Why: Dark and Darker's ring of death; Fortnite's storm; ARC Raiders' orbiter crashing at the time cap. The final-minute sting and red clock exist; the visible advance does not.

8. **Extraction deals** [M, PARTIAL]
   What: Each extraction ring shows its terms on the map before you commit: the near ring is fast and loud (bigger siege), the far ring is quiet, one hot ring pays +25% on the doubled score.
   Why: DMZ's few contested exfils; Dark and Darker's three late portals where only one player can use each. Three rings and the siege exist; differing terms do not.

9. **Reveal-before-you-commit vending cabinets** [S, PARTIAL]
   What: Rare coin-op cabinets show the item inside through the glass, sell it for raid score, then go dark after one sale.
   Why: Fortnite vending machines, which show the item before you take it and are used up once. The peddler exists; this is a random one-shot that fits the Forge's score economy.

## 2. COMBAT FEEL AND WEAPONS

10. **Impact pack: hit-stop, gun kick, knockback** [M, NEW]
    What: A 2-4 frame freeze on heavy hits and kills, a small camera kick opposite each shot, shotgun and heavy rounds shoving enemies back, scaled per gun so a pistol stays light and the LMG feels heavy.
    Why: Vlambeer's Art of Screenshake and Nuclear Throne (hit-pause, camera kick, enemy and player knockback, shells and smoke as permanence). The game has trauma shake, muzzle flash, casings and decals, but no hit-stop, kick or knockback.

11. **Active reload, bonus only** [M, NEW]
    What: A second tap inside a marked window mid-reload completes it instantly and gives the next magazine +15% damage; miss it and the reload simply continues at normal speed, with no jam and no penalty.
    Why: Synthetik, where the timing window differs per gun and the whole mechanic can be switched off. Reloads and draw times exist; this adds mastery without breaking "guns never wear or jam".

12. **Camera look-ahead toward the cursor** [S, PARTIAL]
    What: A soft, eased camera offset toward the aim point (longer for sniper-class guns) so you see farther in the direction you shoot, on top of the sprint zoom-out setting.
    Why: Standard top-down camera practice (lead the action, ease in and out, clamp to bounds); Zero Sievert and Nuclear Throne both show more world toward the cursor. Only the right-mouse steady-aim lean exists.

13. **Gun signature quirks** [M, PARTIAL]
    What: Each of the 11 guns gets one readable trait beyond its stats: shotgun pellets shove, the Longshot pierces one enemy, the Lance shows a heat ring on the cursor and a vent window, the LMG spins up, the Whisper leaves no tracer. Rarity still changes damage only.
    Why: Enter the Gungeon, where every gun plays differently; Helldivers weapon identity. Big guns, draw times and Forge elements exist.

14. **Elemental combos** [M, PARTIAL]
    What: Forge elements interact with the world: cryo-frozen enemies shatter for blast damage, shock chains through wet floors, fire ignites oil and the red barrels, acid strips a plate then a spark finishes the machine.
    Why: Enter the Gungeon status effects; Zero Sievert environmental hazards. The four elements and the barrel order exist; interactions between them do not.

15. **Dodge-through reward** [S, NEW]
    What: Rolling through a bullet inside the i-frame window refunds the stamina, pops a CLOSE CALL score tick and a tiny hit-stop, turning the roll into an attack-adjacent skill.
    Why: Enter the Gungeon and Hades dash-dodging; Hotline Miami's style rewards. The roll with i-frames exists; nothing rewards a perfect one.

16. **Finisher on downed pillagers** [S, NEW]
    What: A hold-to-finish with the crowbar executes a downed pillager before its one-time self-revive, for a small score bonus and a heavy hit-stop.
    Why: Halo-style melee as the close-combat answer he asked for; Gears-style finishers; it makes the existing self-revive a decision.

17. **Grenade landing marker** [S, PARTIAL]
    What: Holding a throw draws the arc and a landing ring sized to the blast, with the cook countdown ticking inside it.
    Why: Helldivers 1 and every top-down grenade game, where the view makes aiming a thrown blast a placement puzzle. Cooking and countdowns exist; a landing preview is not confirmed.

18. **Ricochet off steel** [S, NEW]
    What: Rounds can bounce once off metal walls with a spark and a ping, making angled shots and corridors a little more playful.
    Why: Enter the Gungeon bouncing bullets; Tarkov penetration and ballistics as the extraction-genre cousin.

19. **Bloom ring that tells the truth** [S, PARTIAL]
    What: The cursor's outer ring widens with movement, firing and sprint and tightens when still or crouched, so the player reads accuracy without a number.
    Why: Fortnite and CoD reticle bloom, adapted so the eye stays on the cursor (his 2.5D rule). Steady aim and the inner-reticle hit marker exist.

## 3. ENEMIES AND AI

20. **Attack telegraph pass** [M, PARTIAL]
    What: Every area or heavy attack gets a ground marker that fills over its windup (mortar circle, bulwark charge lane, warden slam cone), and sniper-class machines draw a thin red aim line for 0.6 seconds before firing.
    Why: Brotato (chunky red projectiles and charging enemies that warn first); Hotline Miami's learnable vision arcs. Windups and a lock-on siren exist; consistent ground telegraphs do not.

21. **Pillager roles** [M, PARTIAL]
    What: Crews mix distinct roles shown by colour and silhouette (rusher, suppressor, flanker, medic who revives mates, shield carrier, marksman), so each crew needs a different answer. No words.
    Why: Helldivers 1 factions that each demand different loadouts and tactics; ARC Raiders' mixed roster (Wasp, Rocketeer, Bastion). He asked for more distinct pillager colours; roles give the colours meaning.

22. **Suppression and pinning** [M, NEW]
    What: Sustained fire near a pillager makes it duck behind cover and stop advancing, and the same happens to your side, so LMGs matter and pushing has a rhythm.
    Why: Tactical extraction shooters such as Gray Zone Warfare and Delta Force, where AI reacts to fire. Near-miss whizzes exist; AI reaction to them does not.

23. **Three-way brawls** [M, PARTIAL]
    What: Pillager crews and machines also fight each other on contact, so a decoy or a noise ring between them starts a brawl you can loot afterward.
    Why: Escape from Duckov, where factions bicker and fight each other; Zero Sievert 2 events that pull factions into open conflict. Rival crews already shoot each other.

24. **Overseer phases** [M, PARTIAL]
    What: The Overseer, present every raid, gets three readable phases with arena changes: a sweeping laser lane, summoned crawlers, an exposed core after a charge, and a large score payout.
    Why: ARC Raiders' Queen (homing rockets, laser sweeps, an exposed core during attacks). The Overseer exists and sees farther; phase structure is not confirmed.

25. **Elite affixes** [M, NEW]
    What: Any machine can roll one visible affix (shielded, explodes on death, fast, repairs neighbours) shown by a coloured ring so danger reads at a glance, with a score bonus for each.
    Why: Enter the Gungeon's Jammed enemies; Brotato elite waves. Adds variety from the machines already built.

26. **Search patterns instead of snapping back** [M, PARTIAL]
    What: Enemies that lose you after a noise sweep the last known point in a widening search with visible scan cones; crouching and smoke visibly break it.
    Why: Hunt: Showdown's audio-driven hunting; Hotline Miami's predictable arcs and mediocre hearing. Noise rings and alarm/hunt states exist.

27. **Mimic containers** [S, NEW]
    What: Rarely a crate or locker holds a dormant crawler that wakes when searched, with a faint tick for careful players, so searching carries a small risk.
    Why: Dark and Darker mimic chests; the Gungeon's trapped rooms.

28. **Armoured fronts, soft backs** [M, PARTIAL]
    What: Bulwarks and wardens shrug off frontal fire but have exposed joints at the back or sides, so flanking and smoke pay off.
    Why: ARC Raiders' Bastion, with weak joints at the legs and rear that reward a flank. Machine weak spots exist; facing-based armour does not.
