# Lock the scale before polishing the pixels

PILLAGERS is mostly on the professional path, but it is tuning the wrong picture. In 2025 and 2026, the practice that held up kept AI images in the reference and exploration lane. Rovio's in-house models, Larian's retreat and the indie record all point the same way: hero characters stay under human control (here, hand-authored code), every generated asset gets a cleanup pass, placeholders are tracked so none ship, and AI use is disclosed in specific words. For art that Claude builds, the strongest evidence says quality comes from a hard format plus code checks. The format is a locked palette on a native-resolution pixel grid, scaled up by whole numbers. A ladder of exact code checks runs before any vision critic, and the human judges last. The current style test already does the core of this. It uses whole-pixel shapes on a small canvas, a near-black auto outline, 4-tone ramps lit from the top left, nearest-neighbour blits, a cache per look and a separate critic, and the AI lineup is used only as a reference. Three gaps make it unsound as it stands:

- **Scale.** The test pillager is 52 by 66 art pixels, so at true raid size it draws one screen pixel per art pixel. The chunky look of the lineup only appears at 2x or more. Meanwhile his newest notes push the camera further out, and the game's zoom glides freely between non-whole values.
- **Colour.** Colours come from any RGB value and from passes that scale brightness, so there is no locked palette.
- **Coverage.** Only a front-facing still pose exists. The hard parts are untested: eight facings, a free-aim gun layer, walk frames and outfit recolours.

The fix starts with deciding the art-pixel scale and the palette. After that, the character becomes a paper doll of code-drawn parts baked into a cache, numeric checks run ahead of the critic, no image-generator pixels ship, and the restyle lands after the 10/30 party, aimed at the Thanksgiving release.

## Studios keep generated images in the reference lane, and players punish leaks

The industry numbers split cleanly between using AI and shipping AI art. **36% of game professionals use generative AI at work** (30% at game studios), but **52% now say it harms the industry, up from 30% in 2025 and 18% the year before**. Visual and technical artists are the most negative group at 64%, and the top uses are research and brainstorming (81%), not asset production ([GDC 2026](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)). Players are harsher still: in an opt-in survey of 1,799 gamers, **85% were negative about generative AI in games** ([Games Market on Quantic Foundry](https://www.gamesmarket.global/quantic-foundry-most-gamers-are-negative-about-the-use-of-gen-ai-in-video-games)). A census of Steam releases found AI-disclosed games were **about a third of 2026 releases but only 10 to 27% of estimated sales**, and **72% of AI-flagged games that failed to find an audience used AI for visuals** ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-07-20-steam-ai-disclosure-study)). That census flag is binary and the link is correlation, but the direction is consistent with everything else.

The most detailed production pipeline on record is Rovio's. It fine-tuned one model per game on **only 20 to 30 of its own artist-curated, artist-captioned images**. Artists generated in batches, picked the best, cleaned every image by hand, and nothing shipped without an artist's review. Season-pass background time fell from **20 days to 4**. Rovio's artists rejected the model's renderings of its main character for defects in gesture, proportion and style, because "pretty good is not good enough" for brand-critical assets ([ZenML summary of Rovio's talk](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/accelerating-game-asset-creation-with-fine-tuned-diffusion-models)). EA's partnership with Stability points at the same safe class, textures and previsualisation ([EA](https://www.ea.com/news/ea-partners-with-stability-ai)). Larian used AI images only as photo-search-style reference, then dropped even that from Divinity concept art "so that there can be no discussion about the origin of the art" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/larian-generative-ai-divinity-reddit)). The professional rule across these cases: AI may explore, background and vary, and the characters players identify with stay human-made.

The backlash record shows that discovery hurts more than use, and that small leaks are enough. Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 lost its Indie Game Awards when AI placeholder textures turned out to have shipped at launch, even though they were patched within days ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/tech-gadgets/2025/12/21/indie-game-awards-rescinds-honours-from-clair-obscur-expedition-33-for-genai-use/202718)). Ubisoft's Anno 117 loading screen "slipped through our review process" ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/ubisoft-says-ai-generated-art-in-anno-117-was-a-placeholder-which-slipped-through-our-review-process)). Vapor World's top complaint was that AI cutscenes could not keep the main character consistent ([Everything Edinburgh](https://everythingedinburgh.com/games/news/vapor-world-steam-ai-cutscene-backlash/)). Postal: Bullet Paradise was cancelled over AI accusations its developer denied ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/postal-game-developer-shuts-down-after-its-project-was-cancelled-due-to-allegations-of-generative-ai-use)). On the indie side, the cleanest story is The Roottrees are Dead. Its jam version used AI images, an illustrator redrew them for the commercial release, and it became a hit ([The Verge via repost](https://rss.boorghani.com/?p=320625)). A 2026 practitioner guide for 2D and pixel art says the same thing in working terms. Use AI for "working references", not finals. Pixelling down a larger image by hand beats asking a model for pixel resolution. Keep characters and interactive elements hand-made, give every asset a human pass, treat AI animation as prototype only, and be open with players ([Relish Games](https://relishgames.com/journal/generative-ai-for-2d-game-art-tools-and-techniques/)).

### Disclosure is cheap if no generator pixels ship

On itch.io the Generative AI Disclosure field is **required for asset pages and optional for games**. Staff say a project containing "code synthesized by a Large Language Model" should take the Code sub-tag. For graphics, assets made with generative AI "(even if modified afterwards)" count as AI content ([itch.io announcement](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging)). The creator guidelines exempt "self-contained algorithms without external large datasets" and ask that any AI use be stated in the project description ([itch.io guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines)). Steam's January 2026 rewrite dropped AI coding tools from the survey's focus. It still requires disclosure of AI-made content that ships or appears on the store page, even after repainting ([Steamworks Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use)). On copyright, the US Copyright Office holds that prompts alone are not authorship, but human selection and modification are protectable ([Copyright Office](https://blogs.loc.gov/copyright/2025/02/inside-the-copyright-offices-report-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence-part-2-copyrightability/)). The Supreme Court left the human-authorship rule intact on 2 Mar 2026 ([Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/ip-updates/supreme-court-declines-to-hear-thaler-v-perlmutter-leaving-human-authorship-requirement-intact.html)).

For PILLAGERS this means the AI lineup used as a private style target sits in the most accepted lane, as long as it never appears in the build, on the store page or in a trailer. Code-drawn visuals let him say truthfully that no image-generator output ships. He cannot honestly tick "No AI", because the code is Claude-written, and that choice is his. A specific note in his own voice holds up better than vague boilerplate, which was mocked in the Arc Raiders case ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/arc-raiders-ai-voice-controversy-nexon-ceo-every-video-game)). For example: "ai use: the game code was written with an ai coding assistant (claude), directed and tested by me. every picture in the game is drawn by that code. no ai image generator output ships; one ai image was used privately as a style reference." Being specific also protects against false accusations, which are now common ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-next-fest-feb-2026-gen-ai-art-demos-2000673280)).

## Pixel generators now rotate and tile well, but still drift in motion

The tools have improved a lot since 2024, and two specialists lead. **PixelLab** generates **8 rotational views from one character image** on canvases from 16 to 128 px, with a target-palette option and a documented recipe. You start from a south-facing reference, fix the easiest result by hand, regenerate with it as the init image, and inpaint to lock parts ([PixelLab Rotate docs](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/rotate)). Its API adds skeleton animation, outfit transfer, Wang tilesets and cleanup tools such as palette reduction ([PixelLab API v2](https://api.pixellab.ai/v2/llms.txt)). Its hosted MCP server lists Claude Code as a supported client ([mcpservers.org](https://mcpservers.org/servers/pixellab-code/pixellab-mcp)). **Retro Diffusion** charges per image, **$0.03 to $0.25**. It accepts an `input_palette`, offers 8-direction rotation, tilesets at $0.10 and seamless tiling flags ([Retro Diffusion API](https://github.com/Retro-Diffusion/api-examples)). It is the only tool found claiming a model "trained on licensed assets... with their consent", and nobody has verified that claim ([Retro Diffusion on itch.io](https://astropulse.itch.io/retrodiffusion)). General image models produce "pixel style", not pixel art. Their grid is uneven, edges are soft and palettes explode into hundreds of colours, so each frame needs grid detection, downscaling and quantisation before hand cleanup ([DEV, unfake pipeline](https://dev.to/jenissimo/how-to-tame-your-ai-pixel-art-3pk5); [Pixel Snapper](https://www.spritefusion.com/pixel-snapper)). His reference lineup shows exactly these signs: soft glows, gradients and uneven pixel sizes.

Consistency is where generators still cost work. In a hands-on PixelLab test, the 8-direction rotation "held up well". A 6-frame walk was a different story: the shoulder pad's silhouette disappeared on frame 4, and the sword tip thinned by 1 to 2 px over frames 3 to 5. That left **about 20% of the work manual** ([Techsy](https://techsy.io/en/blog/meshy-vs-higgsfield-vs-pixellab)). A reviewer who otherwise rates PixelLab the standout found it **weaker at 16x16 and below** and complained of burning credits learning tools that change fast ([Jonathan Yu](https://www.jonathanyu.xyz/2025/12/31/pixellab-review-the-best-ai-tool-for-2d-pixel-art-games/)). That size weakness matters here, because a pillager at a true 4x look is only about 20 art pixels tall. For environments, PixelLab builds connectable Wang tilesets of 16 or 25 tiles at 16 to 64 px ([PixelLab tileset docs](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/create-tileset)). No tool was found that makes a full 47-tile blob set end to end. On terms, PixelLab lets you own outputs and use them commercially, but puts legal responsibility for non-infringement on you ([PixelLab ToS](https://www.pixellab.ai/termsofservice)). None of the image tools checked offers indemnity to a self-serve user.

These tools do not fit PILLAGERS' characters. Players choose hood, pads, suit colours, hats and hair, the body has 8 facings, there are walk frames, and the gun aims freely. Generating every combination is impossible. Even with a generator, the working structure would be a neutral base body plus palette swap plus a separate gun layer, which is the same structure code can draw without a disclosure cost. The defensible uses for a generator are two. One is private reference, such as asking a rotation tool what the back of the lineup's red hood looks like, which the lineup never shows. The other, if he ever wants it, is environment variation (rocks, tufts) forced through the project palette, cleaned and disclosed. Given the code-drawn ground test already exists, and given the reputational cost, the soundest choice for Thanksgiving is that no generator pixels ship.

## Claude draws well only inside a locked grid, checked by code before critics

Claude cannot generate or edit images, only read them, so every Claude-made picture is code ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). The research on code-drawn art points one way: rendering feedback moves quality, and the output format sets the ceiling. In the RLRF study, frontier models converting images to SVG scored around SSIM 67 to 69, with Claude 3.7 Sonnet at 69.33. A 7B model trained on rendered-image rewards scored **95.10** ([RLRF](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20793v2)). An agent's render-and-compare loop is the inference-time version of that idea.

The closest public case study is COMPASSO, a 2026 game drawn by Claude with no image model. Its author found procedural Canvas 2D textures "plateaued". What worked was a palette-indexed grid at native resolution with whole-number nearest-neighbour upscaling, where the locked palette "does the drawing", gradients come only from a 4x4 Bayer dither, and rotations are baked frames. His verdict was "a high, cheap floor, not a high ceiling". The same author ran 290 numeric checks that never look at an image, covering palette membership, distinct colours per frame and frame-to-frame difference. They caught a walk cycle whose frames were nearly identical. They missed eyes that shrank below one pixel and left a blank face, and a rotation bug came back four times before the human found it by looking at the screen ([jucelinux, DEV](https://dev.to/jucelinux/i-taught-claude-opus-5-to-draw-with-no-image-model-just-code-then-i-asked-for-a-self-portrait-2894)). PILLAGERS' choice to draw at art-pixel size with whole-pixel shapes is exactly the format that worked there. The current flat vector look is the format that plateaued.

Vision critics are weakest exactly where pixel art lives. Claude sees images in 28-pixel patches, images under 200 px risk hallucination, and counting is approximate ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). On trivially simple geometry, four frontier vision models averaged **58.57%** against humans' 100%. Those results date from 2024, and newer models are likely better, but no rerun was found ([BlindTest](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06581v4)). Vision judges also inflate scores when brightness, gamma or padding change, and prompt fixes did not remove the effect ([Fooling the LVLM Judges](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15249v2)). Anthropic's own guidance supports PILLAGERS' separate critic: the agent doing the work should not grade it. The same guidance warns that "a reviewer prompted to find gaps will usually report some, even when the work is sound", and says to restart with a better prompt after two failed corrections ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). Practitioners report that drawing models pile strokes on top of mistakes, and that optimising toward SSIM made drawings worse ([HN thread](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/48998404)).

Put together, the critic loop is sound in shape. What decides whether it helps is what the critic is shown and asked. Palette membership, the pixel grid, outline gaps and pixel counts belong to code, which is exact. The critic gets silhouette, readability, proportion and "closer to the reference or not". It should see one sprite per image, upscaled by nearest neighbour so an art pixel is large, on the same background, scale and brightness as the reference, saved as PNG. A blind A-or-B choice beats a 1 to 10 score, a "no change needed" answer must be allowed, and a best-so-far copy lets a worse round be thrown away. No published study measures vision models judging pixel art specifically, so these rules come from the general findings above. The owner stays the final judge, picking between 2 to 4 variants at real play size. His survey pages already work this way.

## Pixel craft comes down to one grid, one palette and one light

Professional pixel art looks consistent because a few hard rules apply everywhere. All art shares one pixel grid. "Mixels", parts whose pixels are a different size, read as unprofessional ([Flowlab community](https://community.flowlab.io/t/mini-rant-stop-using-mixels/38287)). One small master palette is built from ramps that shift hue as they brighten. Slynyrd says a fixed shared palette "will ensure everything in your game has consistent colors" ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 1](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/1/10/pixelblog-1-color-palettes)). An outline "should always increase contrast": it is darker than both the object and the background, and dark outlines are the safe choice when backgrounds change ([Lospec](https://lospec.com/articles/pixel-art-outlines-part-2-using-color)). On top-down characters, the head takes "a third to half of the total sprite", 16x32 is a common frame, and Slynyrd's own 3/4 top-down mockup ran at **480x270 native, scaled 4x**, which is exactly a 1080p screen at 4 screen pixels per art pixel ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 22](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2019/10/21/pixelblog-22-top-down-character-sprites)). One projection per scene keeps the 3/4 view coherent ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 3](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/3/14/pixelblog-3-graphical-projections-1)).

Canvas 2D adds its own rules. Smoothing must be off on every scaled draw, and non-integer device pixel ratios make some pixels draw larger than others ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Games/Techniques/Crisp_pixel_art_look)). Path fills can never turn off anti-aliasing ([WHATWG list](https://lists.whatwg.org/pipermail/whatwg-whatwg.org/2007-May/011263.html)), so procedural art has to use whole-pixel rectangles or pixel plotting, which the test already does. The clean pipeline draws the world into one small buffer at whole art-pixel positions and upscales it once. The camera hides its stepping by rendering one extra pixel of border and offsetting the final blit by its fractional part ([yal.cc](https://yal.cc/gamemaker-smooth-pixel-perfect-camera/)). Expensive work belongs in one-time bakes. Asking for fast pixel reads (`willReadFrequently`) "will force the use of a software" canvas ([MDN getContext](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLCanvasElement/getContext)), so outline and recolour passes must run on scratch canvases at bake time, never per frame. A further design note: 480x270 scales by 4 at 1080p but by 5.33 at 1440p, while 640x360 scales by exactly 3, 4 and 6 at 1080p, 1440p and 4K.

Customisation and free aim have standard answers. The paper doll is a base body with every layer on same-size frames and a shared origin, plus palette swaps for colour ([Solarus forum](https://forum.solarus-games.org/en/index.php/topic,1324.0.html)). Without shaders, Canvas recolours by looping over the pixels in JavaScript once per palette and caching the result, the same pattern Phaser developers use ([HTML5GameDevs](https://html5gamedevs.com/topic/36547-solved-phaser-3-palette-swapping)). Guns are the trap. RotSprite exists because ordinary rotation of pixel art is either blurry or ugly ([Sonic Retro](https://info.sonicretro.org/RotSprite)), and Enter the Gungeon's weapons were praised as hand-crafted "down to the last pixel" ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/dd-meets-bullet-hell-shooter-in-enter-the-gungeon/)). Pre-baking 16 to 32 gun angles at art resolution, with an outline pass after rotation, is the Canvas-friendly version. Dead Cells' 3D-to-pixel pipeline accepted "the disappointing level of details" and flickering pixels at about 50 px tall ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)). At 20 art pixels the loss would be worse, and the project bans 3D anyway.

Lighting and ground follow the same logic. Normal-mapped pixel lighting is a GPU shader technique, and even there it blurs light edges ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/low-bit-pixel-art-light-texture-on-canvastexture-sprite-has-unexpected-smoothing-blurring/129394)). The fit for Canvas is shading baked into the sprites plus light and darkness layers drawn at screen resolution over the pixel world, the look Children of Morta used ([Jump Dash Roll](https://jumpdashroll.com/article/children-of-morta-review)). For ground, Slynyrd recommends 16x16 tiles, an even spread of visual weight, few colours, and "negative space is your friend" ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 20](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2019/8/27/pixelblog-20-top-down-tiles)). Drop shadows go on the light's far side, all the same length and never longer than a tile ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 43](https://slynyrd.com/blog/2023/3/26/pixelblog-43-top-down-tiles-part-2)). Dual-grid autotiling needs 16 tiles, or 6 with rotation ([Boris the Brave](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2023/05/31/quarter-tile-autotiling/)). That saving disappears with top-left light, because rotating a shaded tile flips its lighting.

## PILLAGERS has the right method but is tuning the wrong picture

Measured against all of this, the style test (`tools/handoff/art-survey/style-test/`) gets the hard engineering right:

- It builds sprites as whole-pixel masks at art resolution and runs a `harden` pass, so no anti-aliased edge survives.
- The outer outline is near-black (`#16121c`), the correct Lospec choice over busy grass.
- Its 4-tone ramps warm toward highlights and cool toward shadows, as Slynyrd advises, with the light fixed at the top left.
- It blits at whole scales with smoothing off, caches each look and seeds its randomness.
- A play-size comparison picture (`style-test-play.html`) exists, which is the right instinct.

With a separate critic, the method matches the COMPASSO format and Anthropic's verification advice. The problems are about what is being measured.

The first and largest problem is scale. The test pillager is drawn on a **52 by 66 art-pixel canvas** and the machine on 56 by 52. At true raid size, the play picture draws the pillager at 1x: about 66 screen pixels tall, the same as today's figure, with one screen pixel per art pixel and no visible pixel blocks. The chunky look appears at 2x, where the figure stands about 130 pixels tall, nearly double its raid size. The reference lineup is a close-up. Each figure is about a sixth of the picture's width, which on a 1920-pixel screen is over 300 pixels tall, roughly four times raid size. His newest play notes queue a camera move further out, which shrinks figures again. Getting about 4 screen pixels per art pixel at raid size means a pillager roughly 20 art pixels tall. At that size the face is a handful of pixels and the COMPASSO "eyes vanished" failure becomes the central risk. The other route is a closer camera, which contradicts his order. This is a design trade he has to see in pictures, not something the critic loop can polish its way out of.

The second problem is that the camera never sits on a whole-number scale. `dark_raiders.html` (v33.17) has an eased zoom dial from 0.85 to 9.0 with a default of 1.3. It also has eased scope zoom from 1.25 to 2.30, a 0.93 sprint zoom, and a screen factor `hudRes()` that is 1.33 at 1440p. With nearest-neighbour scaling, every one of these gives uneven pixel widths that shimmer while the zoom eases, which breaks the one-grid rule. There are three ways out, and all of them touch feel he ordered. The world zoom could snap between whole art scales, with scopes punching in by a whole step. The world buffer could be upscaled by the whole number just above the target and the small remaining step done with smoothing on; this is a common workaround the sources here did not cover, and it needs a picture test because it softens slightly. Or the game could accept mixed pixel widths.

The third problem is colour. `ramp()` turns any `#rrggbb` into four tones through HSL. Extra tones come from `PIX.shade`, and `pix.js` offers a rim pass, both of which scale RGB values. So the colour count has no ceiling and outfit colours land off any palette. Free RGB is what makes procedural art look "off palette". The fix is a master palette of about 32 to 64 colours in hue-shifted ramps: outfits pick a ramp, and the rim and specular passes move one step along that ramp instead of multiplying brightness.

The fourth problem is coverage. The test has one front-facing still pose holding a rifle across the body, copied from the lineup's poses. `pillager.js` has no facings, no aim layer and no walk frames, yet those are where the restyle succeeds or fails. Mirroring a sprite breaks top-left lighting, which Slynyrd warns about. Code that shades each facing correctly is therefore an advantage over hand sheets here, but only once it exists. The test picture also places a 1x pillager on ground drawn at scale 2. The ground code says its sizes "keep their screen size and get twice the art pixels" at scale 2. In the game that becomes mixed pixel density, the exact mixel error. When the ground moves into the game, its scatter must use a hash of cell coordinates, not the map's random stream, or the seed 4242 fingerprint (ents 85/374, containers 165/593) shifts.

Timing is the last issue. There are six days to the 10/26 feature cutoff. A full restyle is a rendering and feel change that the test corpus and robots cannot judge. Under his dates, it belongs off the party build and lands after the 10/30 party for Thanksgiving. Whether it counts as release work after the cutoff is his call.

## Recommended pipeline and the changes to make

The pipeline below keeps AI in the roles where the evidence says it works: Claude writes the drawing code and the tools, a separate critic checks readability, and image models only make private references. It keeps the human decisions few and visual.

| Step | What happens | Who decides or checks |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Scale decision | Render 2 or 3 candidates at true raid size with the new camera distance: for example a 640x360 world buffer at 3x (pillager about 24 to 27 art px) against 480x270 at 4x (about 20 art px). Pick one art-pixel size for everything. Pick the zoom policy: whole-step snapping, a smoothed final step, or mixed widths. | Owner picks from pictures; helpers log the reason |
| 2. Style sheet and palette | One page holds the master palette (32 to 64 colours in hue-shifted ramps, Slynyrd pattern), the outline colour, the light direction, head-to-body ratio, the tone count per material and the P1 amber and P2 cyan ramps. The lineup is a moodboard only and is never shipped. | Owner approves once; the checks enforce it |
| 3. Silhouette first | Solid-colour blocks for the pillager and each enemy and machine class in 8 facings at the chosen size, before any detail. | Mask checks, then the owner picks among 2 to 4 |
| 4. Paper-doll parts in code | Each part (body, legs, head, hood, pads, hat, hair, pack) is drawn as whole art pixels in palette-index colours, per facing. Legs get a 4-frame walk. Arms and gun are a separate layer. Machines are a hull plus a turret layer. | Claude writes; numeric checks run on every change |
| 5. Bake and cache | At load or on an outfit change: recolour indices to the chosen ramps, composite, run the outline pass, pre-rotate guns to 16 or 32 angles with an outline after rotation, and cache by outfit, facing, frame and angle with a size cap. Passes run on scratch canvases, never per frame. | Frame-cost check at 4K |
| 6. One world buffer | Draw ground, props, walls and figures at whole art positions into one buffer, upscale once, and offset the camera by its fraction. Fog, lights and HUD go on top at screen resolution. The simulation keeps exact positions and the exact aim angle (layer split). | Grid check on the upscaled frame |
| 7. Ground | 2 to 4 calm 16x16 base tiles, dual-grid transitions, then decals and scatter placed by a cell-coordinate hash. Bake into chunks once per raid. | Seed 4242 fingerprint unchanged |
| 8. Check ladder | Code first: palette membership, one grid, outline continuity and no doubled outlines, named features present in every facing, silhouette difference between classes, contrast on the real floor under fog, frame difference and foot baseline. Then the critic: one-sprite crops at a large whole scale, same presentation as the reference, blind A or B, fixed rubric, best-so-far revert, at most 2 rounds per issue. | Gate fails on any code check; critic advises only |
| 9. Ship gates | The build may contain only approved images (a hash list), the disclosure text is written, and his playtest notes on shimmer and readability are in. | Ship queue gate, then the owner |

The changes to the current approach, most important first:

| # | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Judge at true raid size and the planned camera distance, never at the lineup's close-up scale. Redesign the pillager at the chosen art height (about 20 to 27 px) instead of polishing the 52x66 figure. | At 1x the current test shows no pixel look, and at 2x it doubles the figure's size. |
| 2 | Ask him one picture question about the zoom policy before building it. | His eased dial, scope and sprint zoom put every frame on a non-whole scale, which shimmers. |
| 3 | Replace free RGB ramps and brightness-scaling passes with a locked master palette. Outfits choose ramps. | No palette means unbounded colours, and outfit choices land off the look. |
| 4 | Build the hard cases next: 8 facings, the gun layer at baked angles, a 4-frame walk, and P1 and P2 side by side on real ground. | They decide whether the restyle works at all, and none exists yet. |
| 5 | Put one world buffer and one grid in charge. Stop mixing scale-2 ground with 1x figures. | Mixed pixel density is the most visible professional tell. |
| 6 | Move exact checks ahead of the critic, and change what the critic sees and how it answers. | Vision judges miss pixels and inflate scores, and reviewers invent gaps. |
| 7 | Ship no generator pixels, keep the lineup out of all marketing, and write the itch note (Code yes, visuals drawn by code). | Discovery of hidden AI causes the backlash; a specific note builds trust. |
| 8 | Keep the restyle out of the 10/30 party build and land it for Thanksgiving. | It is a rendering and feel change the robots cannot judge. |

## Conclusion

The restyle will succeed or fail on one number, how many screen pixels each art pixel gets at the camera he actually plays with, and not on how good the drawing loop is. Once that number and the palette are fixed, most of the "taste" moves into constraints that code can enforce. That is where Claude is strongest: writing the bakers, recolourers, contact sheets and lint checks, not being the eye. The evidence also flips a common assumption. Procedural characters are not a stopgap until real sprites arrive. With paper-doll parts, per-facing shading and baked gun angles, they handle unlimited outfits and free aim, which generated sheets cannot do, and they let PILLAGERS say truthfully that no AI image ships. That is a real asset with a Western itch.io audience that is 85% hostile to generated art.

Some things remain unknown. No study measures vision models judging pixel art, and no independent test compares the generators' palette or grid accuracy. The frame cost of the bake cache and the full-frame upscale at 4K is unmeasured here, and nobody has tested how a smoothed final zoom step looks in this game. In playtests, he should watch for pixels shimmering while zooming or aiming down sights, faces that turn to mush at the new camera distance, and P1 amber and P2 cyan staying distinct on grass under fog.
