# PILLAGERS UI and HUD redesign: art direction, design system and build plan

- **Date:** 2026-10-12
- **Base:** HEAD v23.59 to v23.61. These are the builds in the screenshots. Line numbers are from today's read of `dark_raiders.html` (49,280 lines).
- **Orders:**
  - #50: "MENUS AND TEXT STILL LOOK LIKE SHIT ... TAKE SCREENSHOTS, MAKE IT LOOK LIKE A TRIPLE-A GAME".
  - #35: "redo the HUD entirely ... keep all the critical info".
  - #20: gritty and grounded, like ARC Raiders.
  - Keyboard and mouse first, controller fully supported.
- **Evidence:** the 60 screenshots in `tools/handoff/shots/ui-before/` (3840x2160). I looked at every one.
- **Status:** design only. Nothing in the game was edited.

## 0. Bottom line

The game does not look amateur because of one bad screen. It looks amateur because it has **no system**. Five root causes show up on almost every screenshot:

1. **The chrome has the wrong art direction.**
   - Every window is a "Chrono Trigger window palette" (CSS comment at line 25): saturated royal navy (`--win-top #1c2a63`, `--win-bot #101a42`) with periwinkle double borders (`--steel-hi #7f92d8`, `--steel #2f4394`, lines 19-31, 157-162, 586-591).
   - The wordmark is Titan One, called "the toybox display voice" in the comment at line 7.
   - That is a 16-bit JRPG menu. ARC Raiders, The Division and CoD use near-black graphite, hairline frames, one hot accent and a cream "paper" card.
   - Only one element already looks right: the cream item card in the shop (`.vdet`, line 437, `#f1ebdf`). The redesign grows from that card.
2. **There is no type system in the menus.**
   - Caps are tracked out to .22em to .42em: `.brand` .42em at line 122, `.modal h3` .24em at line 694, the title rows .22em at line 709, `.pausebox h3` .24em at line 627. Wide tracking is the single loudest "amateur" tell on these screenshots.
   - Families are mixed: Titan One for the wordmark, a heavy squared face for buttons, Rubik for body, Impact for the shirt number at line 213.
   - Sizes run ad hoc from 10.5px to 34px, and weights from 400 to 800 on the same screen.
   - The canvas HUD has a disciplined five-role scale (`TYPE`, line 7696). The DOM menus never got one.
3. **There is no layout system.**
   - Every window puts CLOSE somewhere else:
     - top centre: the Stash, 04
     - bottom right: the shop and the welcome pack, 08 and 02
     - bottom left: the Mainframe and Settings, 12 and 18
     - centred under the content: Wirt and the bar, 11 and 16
   - Content is centred in a full-screen frame and leaves half the frame empty: Welcome Pack 02, Racks 14, Craft detail 09, Terms 06, sector map card 05.
   - Nothing sits on a grid or a safe area.
4. **Too many text walls and dev data are shown to players.**
   - Explainer paragraphs: Welcome Pack, Party 07, P2 window 58, What's New 40.
   - Raw config JSON: SEND PLAY DATA, 31.
   - "the test robot extracts 39.2% of its raids": the sector page, 05.
   - Key soup in the pause box: 25, 42, 49, 59.
   - 29 feedback chips on every run card: 35, 38, 60.
5. **The HUD is nine panels in four styles, with duplicated information.**
   - The panels: CURRENT PILLAGERS, CONDITIONS/CONTRACTS, the controls legend, STANDING, the stamina line, HP, the belt, the gear corner, CREDITS/XP.
   - Credits and XP sit in the top right during a raid, where they mean nothing (19-37).
   - Extraction is announced three times at once: "EXTRACTION 1m" at the top, the ring badge, and "EXTRACT B INBOUND 15s" (37).
   - A 600x200 controls legend sits on the bottom-left quarter of the play space (19-21).

### His words are KEYS: the redesign never rewords them

`TXSHIP` (line 2708) is his baked text, and it applies by whole-sentence match. These lines stay **word for word**, typos included. The redesign may restyle them (family, size, colour, a quote style for vendor voice), but **never edits them**. Rewording one silently drops his edit.

- **Settings:** "So you are too good for default settings, eh?", "Are you scared?", "Too much time on your hands?", "Value is relative.", "Turn down for what?", "Since Claude can't seem to figure this out on its own...", "Clade came up with this.", "Don't use this unless you know what you are doing!"

## 2. The design system

One idea: **a field-issued tool, not a fantasy menu.**
- Near-black graphite surfaces and hairline frames.
- One hot accent (signal amber), and a cream "paper" card for anything you decide on (an item, a contract, a reward).
- Condensed industrial caps for anything you scan; a plain grotesque for anything you read.
- Sharp corners, real texture (grain), no glow.
- The reference read is ARC Raiders, with the discipline of The Division and CoD menus. Apex and Fortnite are the reference for HUD clarity.

- `--f-cond: "Barlow Condensed","Bahnschrift SemiCondensed","Arial Narrow","Roboto Condensed",sans-serif;`

**Why Barlow:**
- It is a DIN-like industrial grotesque. It reads at HUD sizes, and its condensed cut gives about 20% more characters per plate than Rubik.
- It has a full weight range in one superfamily, so menus and HUD share one voice.
- It ships tabular figures for clocks, ammo and prices (ui1's check measures it).
- It is the same genre of face as the condensed grotesques in the reference games, without imitating any one game's brand face.

**Embedding.** The game must render offline (line 6), so the fonts go in as `data:font/woff2` like Rubik today.
- Size: latin subsets are about 20-30 KB each. Eight files are about 200 KB, or about 270 KB as base64.
- **Removed:** Titan One (line 9; ui9 removes it), and Rubik (line 11) once the last user is gone (ui38).
- **Source:** the latin-subset woff2 files Google Fonts serves for these families, plus `OFL.txt` from the google/fonts repository. Store them under `tools/fonts/`.
- **Permission:** fetching them is a download. The orchestrator asks for approval once, before ui1.
- **Itch zip:** `tools/PUBLISH.md` gains a line that the zip carries `OFL.txt`.

## 3. The HUD redesign (his #35)

### 3.1 Principles

1. **Four corners, one centre, one top strip.** Every item of critical information lives in exactly one module, and each module has one style: a `--ui-panel` plate at .86 alpha, or no plate, with a 2px accent rail when it carries a kind.
2. **Nothing explains itself on the HUD.** Key names appear as keycaps only at the moment they apply.
3. **Career data stays in the Undercroft.** Credits and XP, the full pillager board and the full contract list leave the in-raid screen. A compact form stays where the information is tactical.
4. **The play space is sacred.** The ring around the player, from the centre out to about 40% of the height, carries only world-anchored prompts, the crosshair and the downed or extract module.
5. **Every module keeps its `HUDBOX` id**, its user drag and zoom (`hudZoomRect`, `hudGrip`, 31157-31300) and its `[-]/[+]` toggle, so his custom layouts survive. Only the defaults change.

## 6. Risks and how each is avoided

1. **Text width changes break layout checks.** The font swap (ui3, ui4) changes every measured width. Corpus checks that pin pixel positions, plate widths or wrap counts will move.
   - Run the full corpus on the ui3 and ui4 dry builds alone, in their own tab.
   - Re-baseline only the checks whose subject is a width, each with a note.
   - Barlow Condensed is narrower, so overflow risk falls. It does not rise.
2. **Canvas draws before the font loads.** Canvas text falls back silently and caches widths in `_fs`.
   - ui1's `UIFONTS.ready` gate clears `_fs` on load.
   - The first HUD frame after boot re-measures.
   - Checks `await document.fonts.ready`.
3. **His edits silently lost.** A restyle that rebuilds DOM text with new wording drops his `TXSHIP` match.
   - Rule: change markup, never source strings that are TXSHIP keys.
   - The per-build TXSHIP diff and a check that `TX(key)` still returns his value for 5 sample keys on the screens touched.
4. **User HUD layouts.** He can drag and zoom HUD panels (`HUDZ`, `hudUserZ`).
   - New anchors change defaults only. The stored offsets apply on top.
   - `HUDBOX` ids stay, so stored layouts map onto the new modules.
5. **Pad navigation.** Moving close buttons into footers and adding tabs changes focus order.
   - `padMenu` reads DOM order. Footers are appended last, so BACK stays last.
   - Q/E and LB/RB are bound only after checking that they are free.
   - ui6's check walks the focus order of each window with a stubbed pad.
6. **Frame cost.** Grain is a static background; `backdrop-filter` is only on while a window is open; no `shadowBlur` on the canvas (HUDC rule 7728); the vignette edge is an alpha term. ui10 and ui6 read `__gfxCount` and a 120-frame time before and after.
7. **Quirks mode.** The page has no doctype. Tables and some line-height rules behave in quirks mode, so new markup avoids `<table>` or sets `font:inherit` (ui22). Adding a doctype is **not** in this plan; it would move layout game-wide.
8. **Two windows.** The P2 window and the arcade P2 share CSS. Every menu check that opens a window also runs with `NET.same='p2'`, where the screen exists for P2.
9. **Overlap with the features and graphics programs.** Covered in "Order and overlap". Each UI build re-reads its line numbers at the time it is drafted.
