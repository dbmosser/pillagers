## v10.85 - HIS NOTE: RANDOM HUMS THAT LAST WAY TOO LONG, LIKE AFTER YOU DIE

FOUND BY COUNTING, before anything was played. The file creates 40 oscillators
and stops 38. The two that are never stopped are the ambient bed: a 54 Hz sine
and an 81.5 Hz sine, alongside a looping noise bed, a weather layer and a 36 Hz
dread sine that rises as the Warden closes.

That is deliberate and correct. A permanent bed is silenced by driving its GAIN
to zero, not by being stopped, and starting and stopping five voices per raid
would click.

THE FAULT IS THAT NOBODY DRIVES IT DOWN AT THE END. tickAmbience is the only
thing in the file that writes those gains, and it is called from exactly one
place: inside the raid branch of the frame loop. Nothing else in the file
touches AMB at all. The moment a raid ends that branch stops running, so the
gain is simply never written again. It freezes at its last value and those five
voices go on sounding at that level through the outcome card, through the
Undercroft, and until the next deploy.

AND IT FREEZES LOUD. The target is 0.16 + 0.30 * threat, where threat is the
nearest hunting body and reaches 1 when something is on top of you. The last
frame of a raid you LOSE is the frame something was on top of you, so the bed is
near its maximum at exactly the moment it stops being driven. That is his hum,
and dying is the way to get the loudest one.

THE FIX. One function, ambienceOff, cutting all three ambient gains. It calls
cancelScheduledValues first, because the ramp tickAmbience wrote on the last
raid frame is still in flight and would otherwise pull the gain back up after
the cut. Then a 0.12 fade rather than a hard stop, because a bed that vanishes
on a frame boundary clicks; the live bed uses 0.45, so this is gone in under half
a second instead of never. It is called from the top of endRaid, ahead of
everything below it that can throw.

NO DIAL IS TOUCHED. Nothing in this build changes a balance number, an entity
count or a map.

HIS RULE, AND THE CHECK TAKES IT. His words while this was being written:
there should not be any oscillator whose gain fails to fade to zero over a
reasonably short time. That is a better statement of the fault than the one
path I found, so the check enforces both halves. First, the bed is cut exactly
once on each of the three endings and never during ordinary play, because a cut
that fired every frame would stop the room ever tightening. Second, the file's
count of oscillators that are started and never stopped must stay at the 2 the
bed accounts for: a sixth voice with nothing to turn it down is the next hum,
and this catches it the day it is added. The needle for that count is assembled
at runtime rather than written out, or the check would find its own text in the
page and read it as the game's.

Not verified: this is measured as a fact about the code path, not as a fall in
volume, because the fixture makes AudioContext throw so probe runs stay silent.
Nobody has heard it. The same freeze may exist for other sounds that outlive
their moment, the music tail and anything scheduled ahead of the ending, and
this build only cuts the bed. And the pause box is untouched: pausing still
leaves the bed at its last level, which is the right behaviour for a pause and
has not been tested.
