## v15.80 - ONE SET OF ENEMIES, RUN BY THE HOST, SEEN AND FELT BY EVERYONE

His order of 2026-09-25: native multiplayer, co-op first. This is phase 2, build 2 of tools/multiplayer/plan.md with the corrections
in critique.md. Until v15.79 each window in a shared raid ran its OWN pillagers and machines, so two players up top on one seed
saw two different worlds. Now the host runs them all and the linked windows watch. His rulings hold: NO team damage of any kind;
numbers frozen (no dial, count, damage or table moved); coming back empty on death or abandon stays per player. Loot, searching,
revives, extraction, the raid clock, the weather and the bullets themselves stay with each window in this slice.

THE BUILD.
- NUMBERS. Every body gets a number (e.nid) at the build, in list order: netEntsInit(g) runs on the host from netUpAnnounce
  (right after buildRaid, before the raid word goes out) and on a linked window from netUpStart right after its fingerprint
  matched. The populations match by the fingerprint, so number 12 is the same crawler on every window and nothing about a
  body needs to be sent to name it. A body that arrives later on the host (a wave, a reinforcement, a siege arrival, anything
  pushed onto G.ents) is numbered on the next tick from G.nidN and told to the party with what the drawing needs. A solo raid
  numbers nobody (netEntsInit is only reached through the two party paths).
- THE HOST, ten times a second (netEntsTick, called from netUpTick on the clock that already sends where the host stands,
  behind NET.on and the host role, only while a raid is up on the party seed):
    {t:'en',sd,n,e:[[nid,kind,x,y,face,state,health,flags],...]}   on the fast channel, to every link that is in
  kind and state are indexes into NET_ENT_KINDS and NET_ENT_STATES, x and y whole units, face two decimals, health in
  twentieths of maxhp, flags a bit set of what the drawing, the nameplates and the pillager board read (downed, finished,
  alert, moving, hostile, friendly, hit, muzzle, seen you, windup, vent, crouched, rolling, steady, reviving, shooting,
  looting, healing, sprinting, helped, grudge, parley, blind). Only bodies within NET_ENT_RANGE (1600) of any player go in:
  the host player or one of the party up top on this seed, where his window last said he stood. At most NET_ENT_MAX (200)
  rows a packet; more than that and the rest go on the next tick, round robin. A body that left G.ents since the last tick is
  told to every link at once on the reliable channel, {t:'ent',op:'gone',id,how:'dead'|'out',n} (n is the snapshot that
  follows, so a late fast packet naming it is known for what it is); a dead body one of the party killed is credited to that
  seat, {t:'kill',seat,id,k,el}. A body numbered on a tick is announced first, {t:'ent',op:'new',id,k,x,y,n,r,mh,hp,el,ho} plus
  coat, crew, identity, gun, rig and hire for a pillager.
  MEASURED at seed 4242 in the MSHTML engine, JSON on the wire: sector 0 (85 bodies) with the host player and a stand-in
  friend 300 units apart, 21 bodies in reach, 635 characters a packet; every body (reach lifted) 85 rows, 2436 characters;
  sector 1 (374 bodies) 32 in reach, 958 characters, the cap of 200 rows 5838 characters. One row is about 28 characters.
  A new word 117 characters, a hit word 91, a shot word 76. The message cap is 16384. At ten a second the near packets are
  6 to 10 KB/s per link on either sector; a whole sector 0 packet ten times a second would be 24 KB/s.
- THE NEAREST PLAYER. In updateEnts every body picks its target afresh each frame, p=netTargetFor(e) (behind NET.on; with the
  party off p is G.player as it always was): the host player or one of the party up top on this seed, a standing man before
  a downed one, the nearest of those; a hired man keeps the host (netUpBody hands the AI a body with x, y, r 11, face, downed,

  the nearest player for its earshot rule, feudEngage for its engage-near gate, and the wave spawn stands its arrival far from
  every player; the 24 draws of that spawn and every other seeded draw are unchanged in count and order.
- A BLOW ON ONE OF THE PARTY: the host never touches his health. netHitPlayer(p,amt,src,name,x,y) takes the host player
  through damagePlayer exactly as before and tells one of the party {t:'hit',seat,dmg,kind,name,x,y} on the reliable channel;
  his window takes it through its own damagePlayer (netHitTake), so his armour, his downed rules and his death stay his own
  and his own How hard they hit dial applies. The three updateEnts sites (Listener, twice, and the crawler bite) go through
  it; enemy rounds test the party after the host player (netBulletPeer, in the enemy branch of updateBullets only); a
  pillager charge (never your own), a Howler shell and a bolt reach the party by the same radius, roof and wall rules
  (netAreaHitPeers). NO TEAM DAMAGE: player rounds, fists and charges test G.ents only, as they always did, and one of the
  party is never in G.ents; the host charge branch never reaches the peers test.
- A LINKED WINDOW UP TOP (netEntsPeer: role join, a raid in hand on the party seed, not over): updateEnts returns at its
  first line into netEntsEase(dt), which slides each body toward where the host last put it (1 - e^(-12 dt), the v15.75
  easing; shortest arc; the walk cycle while the host says it moves; the hit, muzzle, roll, windup, vent and shooting timers
  running down; crawler and Listener legs), takes off any body the host has said nothing about for NET_ENT_STALE (3) seconds,
  and any body this window made on its own (nothing the host numbered: a local siege arrival, say). tickRaiderWaves returns
  on such a window. netEntsApply files each row on its body by number (the first row, a row after three seconds of silence or
  a jump past 160 units puts the body there at once, as the floor does); an older snapshot than the last applied is dropped
  (the fast channel is unordered), one from another seed too; a row naming a body never seen makes it on the spot with what
  the drawing needs (netEntMake) and the new word fills it (name, health, coat, crew, gun, rig, the pillager look from
  raiderLook off the identity and the spawn spot, no seeded draw). A gone word takes the body off the list and the map with
  the spark, the puff and the label the host frame shows (netEntDeathFx; what it drops is the host world, later); an out word
  marks a pillager EXTRACTED on the board. A kill word counts the kill, the contract and the grudge on this window as its
  own kill would (netKillTake, saveProfile on its own save). Its rounds (updateBullets), fists (fireWeapon) and charges
  (explodeFrag) on a body send the host {t:'shot',id,dmg,x,y,a,wk} through netShotSend, with the damage worked out here
  (weak point, shield, armour and the back shot) and change nothing on the body themselves; the host applies it if the body
  is alive (netShotTake): the weak point effect, the damage, the seat mark by the v15.45 rule (a killing or downing blow
  marks the seat, a blow a standing man survives clears it; the enemy round, frag and bolt kill sites clear it too), the man
  turned hostile and onto the shooter as a host round turns him, the crier alarm (wound 3.5 seconds flat, where a host round
  draws it, so the net code never draws), the v3.94 dodge roll off the bearing the shooter sent. Not the host hire.
- NET gains entMap (every body by number), entGone (bodies lately gone, by number, with the snapshot they left before), entN
  (the snapshot count), entRot (the round robin) and entLast (the last snapshot a linked window applied); netReset and
  netUpEnd clear them. netOnMsg routes en, ent, hit, shot and kill. The ?netslot and ?p2 test handle window.__net gains
  ents(): whether this window runs its bodies for the party or watches the host run them, how many it holds and how many are
  numbered, and each by number, kind, place, state and health.
