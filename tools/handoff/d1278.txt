## v12.78 - THE PRICE OF WALKING OUT IS THE PRICE YOU ACTUALLY PAY

From the 2026-09-08 first-hour audit. Most of what that finding claimed was
wrong, the skeptic threw it out, and I am not building the refuted part. What
survives is small and true, and I confirmed it by reading.

WHAT I AM NOT BUILDING. The reader said the abandon fine is invisible to the
outcome card's accounting, so the card and the corner readout disagree. They do
not. The fine lands before the raid ends, and the card's total is built from the
profile afterwards, so the two agree. Thrown out.

WHAT IS REAL. The fine for walking out is at least a hundred XP, and it is taken
with a floor at zero so it can never push him negative. That floor is right and
it is not moving. But the button quoted the fine from BEFORE the floor, and the
line afterwards announced that same unfloored number.

So a first-time player, who has almost no XP, gets lost on his second run, opens
the pause box, and reads YES, ABANDON THIS RUN (costs 106 XP) with nothing like
that to his name. He presses it, is told XP -106, and loses nothing at all,
because there was nothing there to take. The one number the game shows him about
the consequences of quitting is fiction in both places it appears, and it is
exactly the kind of fiction that keeps a new player inside a run he wanted to
leave.

THE BUILD. Both places use the figure that will actually be charged. At zero the
button says there is no XP to lose and the line afterwards says the same. Nothing
about the fine itself moves: the amount, the sixty second grace, and the floor
are all exactly as they were.

MEASURED. Check 12.78 presses the real two-stage button in a real raid aged past
the grace, and reads the words he is shown and the line he is told. The control
is a character who HAS the XP: he must still read the full price and must still
pay exactly it, so a build that had simply stopped charging would fail there.

Not verified: it ages the raid by moving the clock rather than playing three
minutes; it reads the button's text and the message string rather than pixels;
and it does not change what the fine is worth, which is a dial and his to set.
