## v12.25 - THE RING YOU CALLED KEEPS THE POINTER

From the 2026-09-06 read-only in-raid audit (P2 WRONG STATE: standing in a
second open extraction ring hijacks the pointer), verified by reading at
v12.20.

WHAT IT WAS. tryExtractTick runs tickExtractPoints first. Its mirror block
(v8.25) finds the ring that carries the beacon, points G.active at it and
copies its seconds into the clock the HUD shows, so the name on the banner
and the number under it belong to the same ring. Then, on the same frame,
the standing-ring line ran: if you are inside an open ring, that ring is the
active one, with no beacon test at all. So while the extraction was inbound to
ring A, walking into open ring B made B the active ring. The banner named
B, the pointer swung to B, HOLD E TO EXTRACT hung over a circle no extraction was
coming to, and the seconds it showed were still A's, because the mirror had
just written them. Two rings open at once is the normal state of a map for
most of a raid, so this is a walk across the map with the wrong arrow.

THE BUILD. The ring you stand in wins the pointer only when it carries the
beacon, or when no ring does. With nothing called, the ring you are in is
still the one the game names, as it always has been; that is how you learn
which letter you are standing at. With an extraction inbound, the ring you called
keeps the pointer and its clock until you call another or it leaves. Four
lines at the one site, and one word at the call site: the ring you call
takes the pointer as the call lands, so the banner, the arrow and the clock
agree on the frame a second call goes in (the 2026-09-07 draft review found
the call writing the new clock under the old ring name for that frame).

MEASURED. Check 12.25 deploys on map 0, takes its first two rings and holds
both open, sets a beacon inbound at the first with the pointer on it, stands
the player in the second with E up, and runs one tick; it requires the first
ring to stay active and the clock shown to be that ring's. Then it clears
every beacon and ticks again, and requires the ring stood in to win, the old
rule, as the control. On the v12.24 fixture the second ring takes the
pointer.

Not verified: his own eye on the banner mid-raid; the bot, which targets
G.active and cannot stand in a second ring by accident in a way I have
measured; a second beacon called from the second ring, which the code lets
win because it then carries one.
