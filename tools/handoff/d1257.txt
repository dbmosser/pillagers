## v12.57 - WALKING THROUGH ANOTHER EXTRACTION POINT NO LONGER ABANDONS YOUR OWN

From the 2026-09-06 read-only in-raid audit (P2). This is the last finding of
that run still live. Verified by reading at v12.56.

WHAT IT WAS. The game keeps one pointer at "the extraction that matters", and
that pointer was set by standing in a ring. So walking into any other open ring
while your own extraction was inbound moved the pointer onto the one under your
feet, which had nothing coming.

From that moment everything followed the wrong door. The banner named the ring
you were standing in. The middle of the screen offered you a hold over a ring
with nothing arriving. The approach pings and the last-seconds warning went
quiet, because both watch the pointer. Holding E there started a fresh call,
with its own twenty five second wait, while the extraction you had already paid
for landed somewhere else and left without you. And a man on the floor had his
surrender refused against the wrong ring, which is the guard v12.47 gave words
to.

It needs no deliberate double call to happen. A pillager can open a second ring
himself, and you only have to walk across it.

THE BUILD. A ring with a live countdown or a window still running keeps the
pointer. Calling moves it, and that is a new line at the call itself, because
calling is a choice you made rather than a place you walked through. With
nothing running anywhere, the pointer still follows you into an open ring
exactly as it always has, which is what that line is for.

IT COSTS NOTHING ANYWHERE IT CAN BE MEASURED. The bot calls one ring and walks
to it, so it never has two live at once and no paired number can move. No dial
changes.

MEASURED. Check 12.57 needs two open points and skips honestly on a map that
builds only one. It puts a countdown on the first, stands the man in the
second, which is the exact shape of walking across a ring on the way somewhere,
and requires the pointer and his own countdown both to be untouched. The first
control has nothing running anywhere and requires the pointer to follow him, so
the build cannot pass by simply freezing that line. The second control has him
hold the key in the second point until it actually calls, and requires the
pointer to move, so a choice he made is not ignored.

Not verified: it drives the extraction tick directly rather than walking him
across the map; it reads the pointer and the countdown rather than the banner
and the warning that follow them; and it runs one map and one seed.
