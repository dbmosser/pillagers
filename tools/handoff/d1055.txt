## v10.55 - FOOTPRINTS FOLLOW THE GROUND YOU COVER

His note, 2026-09-03 about 20:00: **"sprint footprint glitch is still
happening"**. Earlier sessions could not reproduce a footprint glitch and
were waiting on a description. This one was reproduced with the live stepper
before anything was changed.

### What it was

The boot mark was keyed to time with a movement key held: every 0.34 seconds
at a walk and 0.22 at a sprint, a print went down at the operator's position.
Two things were wrong with that. Held into a wall, he covers no ground and
the clock still runs, so a print landed under him every 0.22 seconds and a
pile built up where he stood; sprinting is where it shows, because the
clock is fastest there and a sprint is how you arrive at a wall. And the
stamp was taken before the wall push of the same frame, so each of those
prints sat a step inside the wall, at a spot he was never allowed to stand.
Measured on a v10.52 fixture: a sprint into a wall left prints at one x,
eight units apart across the two boots, one after another.

### Now

The print is keyed to ground actually covered, measured after the wall
push: one every 56 units, which is what 0.34 seconds of walking and 0.22
seconds of sprinting both came to in the open, so the trail in the open
looks as it did. Pinned, he covers nothing and stamps nothing. The print's
direction is the direction he actually moved, so sliding along a wall lays
the trail along the slide. The same disease the Undercroft walk cycle had
at v10.51, and the same cure.

### Measured

The check runs the live loop on the first map at seed 4242. Across open
ground, a walk of 70 frames and a sprint of 45 frames must each leave a
trail with every gap between 48 and 64 units. Pinned against a wall and
sprinting into it for 120 frames, he must not move and must stamp at most
one print, and no print anywhere may sit inside a wall. On v10.54 the
pinned sprint stamps a pile, which is the finding; on this build the check
returns null.

Not verified: whether this is the glitch he saw, since his note names no
symptom; the wet trail after water, which takes the same writer and the
same spacing but was not stepped through water here; and the bot, which
never stamps prints and is untouched.
