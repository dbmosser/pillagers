## v10.97 - HIS NOTE 12: A CONTRACT TARGET IS NOT SALVAGE

"anything that can be used to craft or for a contract, etc. should not be
classified as salvage", his note of 2026-09-04.

**REPRODUCED BY ENUMERATION**, against the tables that do the wanting rather than
by reading the labels. Three items are wanted by something in this game and were
shelved as SALVAGE, described to him as "salvage, sell it", and cleared by SELL
JUNK AND LOOSE SALVAGE:

    Servo Actuator   the contract board asks for it by name
    Optics Lens      the contract board asks for it by name
    Data Core        the contract board asks for it by name, and the mainframe
                     burns one to send you up with intel

The classification was a hard-coded list of five ids. It is correct about recipes
and racks and knew nothing about the other things in the game that consume an
item, because the contract board's shopping list was a literal inside the
contract generator where nothing else could read it. So the obvious button could
sell the exact item a live contract was asking him to carry out, and the stash
told him to.

**MY ERROR, CAUGHT BEFORE IT SHIPPED, AND WORTH RECORDING.** My first pass said
the servo is a live repair part again, on the grounds that his answer makes kills
wear a gun and addWear runs on every shot. addWear does run, but WEARSTEPS
carries ONE band, so wearLive() is false and repairCost refuses every gun. The
repair economy is dormant exactly as v9.43 left it, and labelling a servo "for
gun repairs" would be the game promising a use it will not honour. The repair
answer is gated on the wear system actually being alive, which is the switch
v9.43 built for this: put a second band back in WEARSTEPS and the servo becomes a
repair part again on its own. The servo is kept today because the CONTRACT BOARD
wants it, which is true, and is his note.

**THE FIX IS THAT NOTHING KEEPS A LIST.** One function, itemWanted, answers what
wants an item and why, in words he can read, and it answers by asking the recipe
table, the rack costs, the repair parts, the mainframe and the contract board.
Where those were literals inside the code that used them they are named now, so
the next thing that wants an item cannot be added without this answer changing
with it. The stash's shelf rule, which lived inside its own renderer where
nothing could ask it, is one function at file scope. The sell button consults the
same answer, and a junk tag he sets himself still overrides all of it, so he is
never stuck holding a part he has decided to be rid of.

**MEASURED, before and after.** Before: servo, optic and core all sell. After:
all three are kept, shelved under PARTS, and the stash says what wants them. Ten
items are still loose salvage and still sell, the Titanium Cell, the Induction
Coil, the Bloom Sample, the Meridian Reactor Core and the Warden Core among
them, so this taught the sell button rather than switching it off.

**NEW CHECK v10.97** builds the wanted set from the tables themselves, then
requires that nothing in it is shelved as salvage and that nothing in it is
cleared by the sell button. Four controls: at least six items must have been
found to be wanted, or it is not enumerating the game; the contract list must be
readable at all, which on the old build it is not and which is the whole cause;
at least three items must remain loose salvage and sellable, or the fix is an off
switch rather than a rule; and a junk tag must still let him sell. It also
refuses to let the game claim a repair use while the wear system is switched off.

Fails on a v10.96 fixture: the shelf rule is unreachable, the contract list is
unreadable, and the Data Core is cleared by the sell button.
