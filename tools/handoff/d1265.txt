## v12.65 - A HAUL CONTRACT COUNTS WHAT YOU CAME BACK WITH

From the 2026-09-08 read-only audit, confirmed by a skeptic against the source.
Verified by reading at v12.64.

WHAT IT WAS. The haul contract asks you to extract with a haul worth some
amount, and the number it tested was the whole backpack at the end of the raid.

The backpack BEGINS the raid holding whatever you staged. There is no limit on
what you can stage or how much: the staging screen offers any item with a use,
which includes field guns worth thousands, and the deploy slot count is
effectively unlimited.

So the highest-paying card on the board was completable in under a minute. Stage
one expensive gun out of your own stash, land, walk to the nearest extraction
point, hold E, leave. The bag is worth more than the card asked, the card goes
ready, and the gun goes straight back into the stash through the ordinary
banking, so it costs nothing and can be repeated on every board refill.

The game already draws exactly this line elsewhere. It stamps what the lift
carried in, and the run earnings line subtracts it, for precisely this reason.
The haul contract was the one place that ignored it.

THE BUILD. It counts what the run brought back. The threshold printed on the
card has not moved; what it measures has.

WHAT IT COSTS, HONESTLY. That card is meaningfully harder now, because it asks
for what it says it asks for. I am calling that a defect fix rather than a
balance change: the old behaviour let the best-paying contract in the game be
farmed for nothing, and shipping that at alpha would be shipping an exploit.
Nothing in the settings moves and no threshold is retuned. If you want the
threshold lowered now that it means something, that is a dial and it is yours.

MEASURED. Check 12.65 runs the real extraction accounting for the board, with
the card staged by hand so the threshold is a number the check chose rather than
one a roll happened to give it. Two arms, the same value arriving two ways:
carried up from his own stash, which must not satisfy the card, and found in the
raid, which must. The second arm is what stops the build breaking the contract
instead of fixing what it counts.

Not verified: it calls the extraction accounting directly rather than staging
items and riding the lift, which is several screens away; it does not check what
the card prints on the board; and it does not cover the other contract types,
none of which were raised.
