## v12.50 - BREAKING A CRIER LINE OF SIGHT NOW ACTUALLY CANCELS ITS ALARM

From the 2026-09-07 read-only audit, specced from the source. Verified by
reading at v12.49.

WHAT IT WAS. The cancel fired the thing it was cancelling.

Breaking a Crier's line of sight for three seconds is supposed to call the
alarm off. The branch does the right things: it puts the machine back on
patrol, clears its cooldown and clears the windup. It clears the windup to
nothing, and nothing stops the rest of that branch running in the same frame.
Four lines later the windup is decremented, and nothing minus a number is a
negative number, so the very next test saw a windup at or below zero and fired
the entire alarm: the siren, the ping on the mark, the marked flag, the counter
in his run report, the line telling him they are coming to where it LAST saw
him, every machine within 900 units sent to the mark and every Listener sent
hunting.

So hiding from a Crier has never once cancelled an alarm. At best it brought
the alarm forward by under a second.

There was a second half. The three seconds were cumulative rather than
continuous, because the counter is zeroed only when the alarm begins and never
when the Crier sees him again. A man who ducked behind three different walls
for a second each had it cancel as though he had stayed hidden the whole time.

THE BUILD. Two guards on the countdown, so both lines ask whether this is still
an alarm at all, and one reset so the three seconds are unbroken.

WHAT IT COSTS, HONESTLY. The two halves pull in opposite directions. The cancel
now works, which favours him and is the counterplay the machine was designed
around. The three seconds must now be unbroken, which does not favour him, and
unbroken is what that counterplay always claimed to be. Nothing in the settings
moves. The window in which any of this can happen is small by design, because
the windup is three to four seconds and the counter has to pass three, so he has
to break sight almost immediately and stay hidden.

MEASURED. Check 12.50 puts one Crier alone on the map, already in its alarm,
with the windup still running and the counter one hundredth of a second short
of three. One step of the machines takes it over, which is the exact frame the
cancel is meant to happen and the exact frame the old code fired the alarm
instead. It requires the machine off its alarm with no siren line, no mark, no
count against him, and the windup cleared to nothing rather than to a negative
number. The control is the same Crier with the same windup and the player in
plain sight: the alarm must still fire and still mark him, or a build that
simply broke the Crier would read green.

Not verified: sight is broken by putting him on the floor, which the machine
code treats as unseen for everything that is not a pillager, rather than by
walking him behind a wall; the machines that the alarm summons are not present,
so what they would have done is inferred from the alarm not firing rather than
watched; and it runs one map and one seed.
