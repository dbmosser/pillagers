## v12.66 - THE CONTRACT BOARD COUNTS CONTRACTS

From the 2026-09-08 read-only audit, confirmed by a skeptic against the source.
This one is my own wording. Verified by reading at v12.65.

WHAT IT WAS. The only progress readout the contract board has says "Contracts
completed N". N is not a count of contracts. It is a weighted number: a STANDARD
card counts one, a HARD two, an ELITE three. Claim your first ever HARD card and
the board tells you that you have completed two contracts.

The gate under it is miscounted from his side for the same reason. "ELITE work
at 6 more" means six more of that weight, which is two ELITE claims or three
HARD ones, not six contracts. He plans against a ledger that can be wrong by
three times per card.

THE ENTRY FOR THAT LINE IS MINE, and it records a deliberate vocabulary swap
with no look at the number behind it. The line used to say "Contract standing",
which was true; standing is a banned word, so I renamed it to "completed", which
is false. Renaming is not the same as reading, and I did not read.

THE BUILD. The count and the gate are two different numbers, so both are said,
each called what it is. A true count of contracts claimed, and a gate that asks
for credits in its own words and states what a harder card is worth.

IT CHANGES NO ARITHMETIC. The credit that opens harder work is untouched: a
HARD card is still worth two of it and the thresholds are where they were. The
only change is what the line says.

MEASURED. Check 12.66 claims one card and reads the DRAWN line, because the
whole finding is a number printed under the wrong name. It uses a HARD card on
purpose: that is the smallest claim whose weight and whose count differ, so a
line printing either one can be told apart. It requires the line to say one
contract and not two. The control is the gate itself, which must still read two
credits after that claim and must still name what it is asking for, so this
build cannot pass by quietly flattening the weighting.

Not verified: it claims through the board's own function rather than clicking
the card; it does not read the next-unlock line elsewhere on the floor, which
draws from the shop table and not from this number; and it does not cover a
profile saved before this build, which simply starts its count at zero and
climbs from there, so an old save will under-report until the next claim.
